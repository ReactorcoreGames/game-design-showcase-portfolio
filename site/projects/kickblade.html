Game overview

Kick Blade is a small, easy, cathartic arcade game about kicking people away satisfyingly. A young woman in a soft pink-and-white cutecore outfit takes on greedy executives with nothing but her kick and her blade, one single-screen room at a time, and you play the whole thing with one hand.

Every goon goes down in one hit and goes flying; she can take several. The rooms are moving elevators, trains, funiculars and glass-walled penthouses, and each gauntlet of them ends face to face with an executive who did something unforgivable for money.

The movement and attacks are modeled on Kirby and Smash Bros, minus the flying and the copying, and the room-by-room structure on Virtua Cop. It deliberately leaves out endless modes, infinitely spawning enemies, high scores and stages without a reason to exist.

Target audience

Anyone who has read one more story about a company putting profit over people and wished they could do something about it, for ten minutes, harmlessly, in a game.

They want quick, clean catharsis, not a combo list to study.

It's playable with one hand on the left of the keyboard, and every enemy attack is announced a full second before it lands.

Game's purpose

Power and relief: the satisfaction of sending someone flying with one well-aimed kick, again and again.

Play and silliness: a pastel heroine against a drab corporate world, and goons who pop on impact.

Meaning: every gauntlet ends with someone who had it coming.

It avoids draining rest and ease: short gauntlets, a gentle pace, and controls that fit under one hand.

Core concept

Arcade brawlers often pad themselves out with damage sponges, endless waves and scores to chase.

Here one hit is enough, every room has a fixed number of goons and an end, and every gauntlet has a reason: a named executive and what they did.

There's always another executive, and each one beaten joins the gallery.

Lore and story

The city

A grunge-industrial corporate city: glass towers over worn concrete, cargo elevators and funiculars climbing their sides, trains running between them. Everything and everyone in it is drab, grey and tired.

Her

She's an ordinary civilian, and the only bright thing in the city: soft pink hair with heart-shaped pins, a pink ruffled top and corset, a white pleated skirt, white thigh-highs with pink bows and chunky white platform sneakers. She carries a tactical blade in her own colors, a white blade on a pink handle.

She has no organization behind her and no special powers. She's simply had enough, and she's very, very good at kicking.

Them

The executives at the top of each tower are defined by one thing: what they chose to do for money. Stole millions from people, greenlit a poisonous product, ruined a local community, paid less than minimum wage, lied to their customers, chose profit over people. Their loyal security staff stand between her and them, armed with nothing but batons.

The executives themselves don't fight, at least at first. They stand there and talk down to her, trying to scare her into giving up.

The job right now

Get to the top of this one. Then find the next.

Controls and interface

Every action sits under the left hand, so the whole game can be played one-handed. The moves combine Kirby- and Smash-style, without a combo list to learn.

An attack starts the moment its key goes down and can be cut short by letting go. It lasts a few hundredths of a second, a little longer for powered attacks, and hits every goon in its reach exactly once.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    One room

    A stylish swipe from black, "Stage 3/9", "Ready", "Go", and she's standing in the middle of a single-screen room. Goons start arriving from one to three of eight possible entry points, the edges and corners of the screen, and there are always one to four of them about.

    They come at her, stop, wind up a baton swing with a clear one-second tell, and she kicks them away before it lands. When the last one is gone, "Complete" appears, and three seconds later the swipe takes her to the next room.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    A gauntlet of eight rooms and one executive

    A gauntlet is eight rooms and a ninth where the executive waits. In that last room there's no "Ready, go": she appears on one side, the executive on the other, and their condescending words scroll across the top of the screen as subtitles, until a kick shatters the subtitles along with their speaker.

    Lose all her health and the gauntlet starts over from the first room, freshly shuffled. A gauntlet is short, so that's a few minutes, not an evening.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    The gallery of the fallen

    Every executive is generated: a made-up name, an industry, and the thing they did, which don't have to match. There's always another one at the top of another tower.

    Each one she takes down is recorded in a gallery on the main menu, with their name, their industry and their crime. It's the record of everyone she's dealt with, not a score.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A coffee break

    Start the game, start a gauntlet. A room takes well under a minute and a whole gauntlet about ten, which makes the executive's room a natural place to stop.

    There's nothing to collect, nothing to keep up with and nothing that waits for you to come back. The next executive will be there whenever you are.

Game modes

There's one mode, the gauntlet, played as many times as you like against a new executive each time. For players who want more bite, it has optional tough settings:

SettingWhat changes
NormalHealth slowly comes back, and is topped up between rooms
ToughNo slow recovery, or no top-up between rooms
CountdownA Time Crisis-style clock keeps her moving before the executive gets away

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    One hit, sent flying

    Every goon goes down in one hit. What makes it satisfying is where they go. A kick launches the goon along a trajectory set by where she was when she kicked them, so a kick from below sends them into the ceiling, and a sprinting kick sends them across the room. They burst apart, cartoonishly, the moment they hit a wall, the ceiling or the floor.

    The blade is the other half: it bursts a goon on the spot, scattering them along the way her swing was moving. Normal attacks pop goons after a beat; powered attacks do it instantly and with far more force. The physics are simple arcade physics, plain gravity and readable arcs, so you learn quickly to aim the kick at the wall you want.

  2. Playable one-handed

    Six keys and their combinations

    Move, jump, plunge, dodge, kick and blade, all within reach of the left hand, and everything else comes from combining them: sprinting, falling or rising while you attack, or holding dodge. There are no long input strings and no timing windows to memorize.

    She doesn't collide with goons, and goons don't collide with each other, so she can slip through a crowd to get the angle she wants, and a room never jams up.

  3. Easy to read

    Goons with tells and tempers

    A goon aims for the spot right in front of her, walking and jumping to get there. Once there, they stop, show clearly that they're about to swing, and swing one second later, without moving while they do it. Most of the time, you've dealt with them long before that.

    Small random habits give each one a personality: how often they stop to catch their breath, how often they jump on a whim or swing at nothing, how fast they walk. They have momentum in the air but next to no control over it, so a goon who jumps at the wrong moment is a goon who's about to be kicked.

  4. Mistakes are allowed

    Health and a goofy exit

    She can take several hits. A baton that lands costs one point of health, knocks her back a little and gives her two seconds in which nothing can touch her, and health slowly comes back or is topped up between rooms.

    If she runs out, time freezes and she does a Super Mario Bros-style hop off the bottom of the screen with a goofy face. It's a joke, not a punishment, and the gauntlet starts over.

  5. Rooms that move

    Elevators, trains and penthouses

    Every room is a single screen, and its shape changes how you fight in it:

    RoomIts shape
    Cargo elevatorSolid side walls to kick goons into, rising or falling as you fight
    Tunnel trainA solid ceiling overhead, so upward kicks have somewhere to land
    Open trainOnly the tracks below: a goon kicked off is gone at once, and she bounces back up minus a point of health if she falls
    Penthouse or sealed roomFully closed, sometimes with a window wall on one side or overhead, for the executive

    Funiculars and barracks rooms join the mix, and the executive isn't always in a penthouse: the final confrontation can just as well happen on a speeding train or in a rising elevator.

  6. The last room

    The executive

    At first, the executive simply stands there and talks, and the subtitles do their best to make her give up. One kick and the words shatter.

    Some executives fight back, though. One might pick up a baton like their own staff, with more than one hit in them; a hit stuns and shoves them. Another might shrug off every hit, so the reliable way to beat them is to knock them out of the room, like the Koopalings on their tilting platforms in Super Mario World.

Notice to the board

Kick Blade: One-Handed Arcade Brawler

To whoever is reading this at the top of the tower: there's a young woman in the elevator. She's dressed in pink, she's very polite, and your entire security team has just left the building through the window.

She'd like a word. It will be a short one.

Kick first. Ask nothing.
