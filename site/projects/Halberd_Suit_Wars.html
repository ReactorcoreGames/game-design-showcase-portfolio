Game overview

Halberd Suit Wars is a side-view mecha game of heavy, floating slugfests in zero gravity. Your squad of humanoid war machines drifts through an arena in orbit, on a moon or inside a megastructure, trading beam rifle shots, bazooka rounds and heat-axe swings with the enemy's, until one side breaks, retreats or runs out of pilots.

Every hit matters. A destroyed limb is gone for the rest of the battle, there's no healing of any kind, ammunition runs out, and when your machine is finished you can eject and keep fighting as a tiny pilot in a spacesuit. Machines are easy to replace. Pilots aren't.

It's a spinoff of Reactorcore's Floppy Heroes remix, a ragdoll gladiator game, retold in the setting of early Mobile Suit Gundam and its One Year War, with everything renamed.

Target audience

People who grew up on the gritty early Gundam war: grunts in mass-produced suits, beam rifles with a few shots each, and battles decided as much by who withdrew as by who won.

They want weight and consequence in mecha combat, where most mecha games give them regenerating shields and endless ammunition.

The controls are WASD and the mouse, with a handful of keys for orders. The pace is deliberate, so thinking matters more than twitch speed.

Game's purpose

Challenge and mastery: reading armor, angles and momentum, and making every shot count.

Meaning: each pilot is a person the war can't easily replace, and keeping them alive is the real victory.

Choice: fight, eject, switch suits or retreat, all as legitimate moves.

It deliberately avoids draining fairness (the same rules bind both sides, friendly fire included) and autonomy: operations can be fought in any order, and the war is the player's to run.

Core concept

Mecha games usually make you a lone ace who heals between fights and powers up as the story goes on.

Here you're a squad among the grunts, damage stays for the whole battle, and survival carries forward: every pilot and machine you bring home becomes a reinforcement in a later fight.

The war is the frame, and losing ground on purpose to save lives is often the smartest move.

Lore and story

The human sphere

Humanity has spread out from its homeworld into orbit and beyond: rotating colony cylinders at the points where gravity balances, cities on the moon, fortresses dug into asteroids, and graveyards of wrecked ships where the last war ended. Each of them is home to someone, and each of them is a battlefield.

Why the suits

A jamming haze released on every battlefield blinds radar and long-range guidance, so wars came back to eyesight range. The weapon that rules at eyesight range is the halberd suit: a humanoid machine as tall as a building, with hands that can wield any weapon, and limbs whose swing turns it in zero gravity without spending fuel. It's named after the old infantry weapon that let a foot soldier bring down an armored knight.

Every side builds them, and every side builds them the same way. Suits come in standard frames with light, medium or heavy armor on each limb, and carry the same family of beam rifles, cannons, bazookas, pile bunkers and heat blades.

Same machines, different flags

Each war is fought between two sides with their own names and colors. You pilot for one of them, as one more soldier in a mass-produced suit, not as a chosen hero. When the war ends, the winners endure and will turn up again in future wars, sometimes as the side you fight for and sometimes as the side you fight against. The losers are gone for good.

The job

Win this war's operations, one area at a time, and bring as many pilots home as you can. Everyone who survives fights in the final battle.

Controls and interface

You float your suit through the arena with WASD and turn it to face the mouse. In zero gravity there's no up or down, only momentum: a heavy suit takes time to get going, and time to turn around.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    A slugfest in one arena

    A wide rectangular arena: a patch of open space, a lunar crater, a colony's interior, a debris field. Your squad of two to four suits enters from one edge, and two to six enemies from the other. Rocks, wrecks and structures give cover; some can be pushed, some destroyed.

    Fights are heavy and deliberate. You drift into position, turn to bring your weapon to bear, and try to strip an enemy's armor and limbs before they strip yours, all while keeping your own squad out of your line of fire.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    An operation

    An operation is a limited military campaign over one small region: a map of 6 to 16 battle nodes, fought in any order you choose. It comes with a pool of spare suits and a much smaller pool of pilots, about five machines for every person.

    Whatever survives a battle, suits and pilots both, comes back as reinforcements you can call into later fights in the same operation. Win the operation and the survivors move up to the war's reserves. At its end, a memorial lists every pilot it cost, by name.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    A war over one place

    A war is fought over one celestial location (a planet, a moon or a stretch of deep space) between two sides with random names and colors. Its operations are nodes on that location's map, and again you choose the order.

    When every operation is done, the final battle begins: eight against eight, with reinforcements replacing the fallen from each side's reserves until one side runs dry. The enemy brings 20 to 100% more than you do, because the AI makes up in numbers what it lacks in judgment.

    The winning side joins the list of sides that endure, and may turn up in a later war as your side or the enemy. The loser goes on the list of sides that are gone, never to return. Both lists can be browsed but never edited: a record of every war you've fought, and of whom it was fought for.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A battle, an operation, a war

    A battle takes a few minutes. Between battles you're back on the operation map, which is the natural place to stop: the operation waits exactly as you left it.

    An operation fills an evening or two, and a war a few weeks of evenings. When a war ends, the next one begins over a new place, between two sides that may include old friends.

Game modes

There's one mode: the war. It's built from nested pieces you can stop between, and the same rules hold at every level:

The warOne celestial location, two sides, and a final eight-against-eight battle.
An operation6 to 16 battle nodes in any order, with its own pool of suits and pilots.
A battleOne arena, your squad of two to four against two to six.
A pilot's sortieFrom launch to the moment they eject, retreat, or don't come back.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Armor, limb by limb

    Every suit has the same frame and the same size, but each limb can carry light, medium or heavy armor, and where you put the weight decides how the suit moves:

    Heavy armor onSlows downIn practice
    TorsoBoost acceleration and turning aroundHard to kill, slow to flip (up to three seconds)
    LegsTurning speedA sturdy platform that's slow to change direction
    ArmsHow fast the arms swing to aimStrong guard, but a quick enemy can get inside your aim
    HeadWeapon switchingThe sensors survive, the next weapon comes out late

    Armor has to break before the limb under it takes damage, so a heavy torso and bare arms is a different fight from the reverse. It's mobility against toughness, decided one limb at a time, and the enemy's choices are visible on their suit for you to read.

  2. Make every shot count

    Four weapons, finite ammunition

    A suit carries four weapons, one in hand and three on its back, cycled in order. They hit hard, and their ammunition or energy is limited. An empty weapon is still a club, but a much weaker one, so accuracy is how you stay dangerous.

    KindExamples
    RangedParticle beam rifles, kinetic autocannons, rocket launchers, giant bazookas
    MeleeSuperheated axes, plasma swords, pile bunkers
    ThrownAnti-suit mines
    Head-mountedOne defense system per suit, as it comes from the factory: a point-defense gun, a directional shield, a projectile redirector, smoke launchers, a grapple, an afterimage generator or a temporary energy barrier

    Weapons themselves can't be destroyed, and their bulk blocks some incoming fire, so a held bazooka doubles as a shield for the arm behind it.

  3. No healing, ever

    Limbs that don't come back

    When a limb's armor and frame are gone, the limb doesn't fly off: it bursts into a fireball and vanishes, leaving a spray of hydraulic fluid, oil, sparks and flame at the joint. An arm lost takes its weapon hand with it; a lost leg changes how the suit turns.

    Nothing repairs it until the battle is over, and there's no health regeneration of any kind. Damage is permanent for the fight, so cover, distance and timing are worth more than any amount of firepower.

  4. Machines are cheap

    Eject, and keep going

    Every operation has about five suits for every pilot, so a wrecked suit is a setback and a dead pilot is a loss. Press E and the pilot ejects as a tiny figure in a spacesuit with a jetpack, armed with a slow-recharging bazooka, an SMG for duels with other pilots, and a vibro-knife for sawing through debris.

    On foot you're fragile: one hit from any suit turns a pilot to dust, and suits are half again as fast. The point is to get somewhere: off the battlefield to safety, or into a fresh suit, either a pilotless one already on the field or one command sends in when you call for it.

    Your pilots aren't heroes with names on the box. Each is an ordinary soldier, and when one dies, the next pilot in the pool takes over.

  5. Friendly fire is on

    A squad, not an army of one

    You fight with one to three squadmates, and one key cycles their orders: stay close, hold back at the spawn, or attack. You can call in up to three reinforcements per battle from whatever survived earlier fights in the operation.

    Friendly fire is on for both sides. Careless shots hurt allies, and a skilled pilot can use boosts and positioning to bait an enemy into firing straight through their own comrade.

  6. Live to fight again

    Retreat is a real choice

    Out of ammunition, missing an arm, outnumbered? Get back to your own edge of the arena and press Z three times to withdraw. You lose the area, but the suit and its pilot are saved for a future fight. The enemy can do the same when its ammunition runs out.

    A good withdrawal usually comes after you've done some damage first: every enemy suit and pilot you take down here is one fewer in the final battle.

Squad deployment orders

Halberd Suit Wars: Ragdoll Mecha Combat

Suit assigned: standard frame, armor configured to your request. Four weapons on the back, one in hand. Ammunition is what you're carrying. Repairs are after the battle, if there is an after.

Command has plenty of suits. It does not have plenty of you. If the fight turns, eject. If the area's lost, withdraw. Come home.

Machines can be replaced. Pilots can't.
