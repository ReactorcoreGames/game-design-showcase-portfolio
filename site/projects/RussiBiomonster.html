Game overview

You are the monster: an amorphous, pulsating blob of flesh about a metre across, slipped into a grey concrete apartment block in a rough industrial city. You can't walk. You fling tentacles at the walls and pull yourself through each apartment, then skewer, harpoon, smash and devour your way through the building, room by room.

The tenants are not helpless. They're tough, proud people in a rough neighbourhood, and every one of them has something stashed in the furniture for exactly this kind of day: an old rifle in the sofa, a rocket launcher in the cupboard, a frying pan behind the toilet. One of them is no match for you. A whole building of them might be.

Underneath the slapstick is a dark satire. The blob is a weapon, and the page that appears after every building, a cheerful propaganda bulletin, makes it slowly clear who's pointing it. It's a spinoff of Reactorcore's Floppy Heroes remix, a ragdoll physics combat game, turned inside out: this time you're what everyone is fighting.

Target audience

People who like playing the monster (Carrion) and physics chaos with a grappling hook, and who enjoy dark comedy with something to say.

They want to be the overwhelming force for once, and they're tired of games where the monster only exists to be defeated.

The mouse aims everything; a handful of keys switch abilities on and off.

Game's purpose

Power and freedom: a body that swings, slams and skewers, and a room full of physics to throw around.

Excitement and silliness: fast, brutal momentum, and tenants pulling absurd weapons out of their furniture.

Meaning: the slow, chilling understanding of who sent you.

It avoids making cruelty the reward: there are no points for suffering, the tenants fight back, and when they win, the game records it as their victory.

Core concept

Monster games usually pick one side: a mindless power fantasy, or a monster that's only there to be beaten.

This one gives you the full power of the monster, and then turns the camera on whoever released it. The horror you control pales next to the one that signs the paperwork.

And it doesn't end: there is always another building, generated fresh.

Lore and story

The neighbourhood

An unnamed industrial city of identical concrete apartment blocks, factories and smoke, ruled by a regime that calls everything it does progress. The low-income blocks are full of people who've learned to look after themselves: workers, war veterans, pensioners, homemakers, skinny lads and hard men, all in their slippers and house clothes, all with something valuable, or something dangerous, hidden in the furniture.

The weapon

Outside the city lies a lake of radioactive waste, and in it something mutated. The regime's laboratories grew it into a bioweapon. The regime wants land: a new plant, a war factory, a coal mine. Some tenants won't leave. So instead of paying them or arguing with them, it lets the blob into their building, through a door, a window, a ventilation shaft, a sewer grate or the toilet plumbing.

The heroes

When the blob has emptied a building, the army arrives, oh so conveniently, to "stop the monster" with flamethrowers, and everyone sees them as heroes. The scorched, dormant blob is loaded into a van and taken back to base, where it slowly regrows until it's ready for the next address. Then the bulldozers move in.

Right now

A new address. A building full of people who don't know yet. Get through it before they put enough holes in you.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    One apartment

    Each room is an enclosed arena of furniture and tenants. You swing in on a tentacle, slam into the sofa, pin someone to the wall with a harpoon, reel in another with a tentacle and open the maw.

    Meanwhile the tenants scatter, and the smart ones run for the furniture to dig out whatever they hid there. In the first rooms, they're caught by surprise. Further in, they've heard the noise.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    The building

    A building is a gauntlet of apartments, cleared one after another until the whole block is empty, or until the tenants have done enough damage to bring the blob down.

    The blob doesn't heal between rooms. It grows instead, so every building is a race between how big you're getting and how many holes they've put in you.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    Address after address

    There's always another building the regime wants, each one generated: its address, its layout, its tenants and what they've hidden.

    Every one ends up in a permanent propaganda bulletin, a page per address announcing what will be built there, in the bright, upbeat voice of a ministry press office. Reading it back, knowing what really happened, is the most unsettling part of the game.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A room or a whole block

    A room takes a minute or two of fast, brutal action; a whole building somewhere around half an hour. The game saves at every room's door, so you can stop between apartments and the building waits, with its damage and its survivors, exactly as you left it.

Game modes

There's one mode: building after building, each one a complete story with its own bulletin at the end.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Moving by tentacle

    The blob can't walk. It moves the way a grappling hook gun does: launch a tentacle at a wall and it sticks, pulling the blob toward that spot, faster and faster, until you let go. Let go at the right moment and you fly across the room with all that speed. Apart from that, the blob can only roll.

    Launch it at a piece of furniture or a person instead, and it's them who get pulled toward you. That's how you drag the sofa out of the way, or the tenant out from behind it.

    Everything else follows from speed: a hardened blob arriving fast is a wrecking ball that smashes furniture to bits, and a blob arriving slowly is a target.

  2. What the blob can do

    A body made of weapons

    All of it is aimed with the mouse, and most of it combines:

    AbilityHow it works
    Bone spikesFive metre-long spikes fan out from the blob. Anything they impale, furniture or people, stays stuck on them until they're pulled back in.
    Bone harpoonA heavy shot that skewers and drags whatever it hits with its momentum, nailing it to the wall. Three on board; go and fetch them to reuse them.
    Bone dartA light shot that sticks in surfaces and people for small damage. Twelve on board, regrowing one every three seconds.
    HardenThe front third of the blob (a 120° arc) turns hard: a shield against incoming fire, and a battering ram at speed. The rest stays soft.
    Blender mawA mouth opens in the blob's side, pulls in anyone close enough, and grinds them up.

  3. Bigger, not better

    Growth instead of healing

    There's no healing, regenerating or armor. What the blob gets from devouring people is mass: it grows from a metre across to as much as two, takes less damage from every hit, and puts more force into every slam, pull and shot.

    Its wounds stay, though. A big blob that has been shot to pieces on the way up is still close to the end, so every building asks how greedy you can afford to be.

  4. They saw this coming

    Tenants who fight back

    An unarmed tenant runs away from the blob and toward the nearest piece of furniture, where something is always stashed. Out comes an old surplus rifle, a hunting shotgun, a rocket launcher, a homemade weapon or a kitchen knife, and they turn and start shooting, stabbing or swinging.

    The game is balanced in the blob's favor, so any single tenant is weak. But the damage they do adds up across the whole building, and if enough of them get their stash in time, the tenants win.

  5. Ragdoll physics

    Rooms as arenas

    Built on the same ragdoll engine as Reactorcore's Floppy Heroes remix, every tenant is a floppy physics body and every armchair, wardrobe and fridge a physics object. Harpooned things hang on the wall where they were nailed, impaled things swing on the spikes, and a fast enough slam breaks the furniture apart.

    So the room itself is a weapon and a shield: a wardrobe pulled in front of you stops bullets, and a fridge flung across the room stops a tenant.

  6. The living history

    The propaganda bulletin

    After each building, a new page is added to the bulletin: the generated address you just emptied, and the regime's cheerful announcement of the modern facility that will soon stand there, a new plant, a factory, a mine, with thanks to the residents for their cooperation.

    It never says a word about the blob. Page after page, the bright tone over what you know happened turns into dread, and the monster you've been playing starts to look like the least evil thing in the city.

    When the tenants win, the bulletin records that too: the army still collects what's left of the blob, but the page announces that the development at that address has been "postponed". It's the one page you can be glad of.

Bulletin of the Ministry of Development

Biomonster Neighbourhood: Apartment Ragdoll Rumble

Good news for your neighbourhood! A modern, productive future is coming to your block, and the Ministry thanks every resident for their cooperation in making room for it.

Residents are reminded that there is nothing in the walls, nothing in the plumbing, and no need for anything hidden in the sofa. Should anything unusual occur, our brave soldiers will arrive promptly. They always do.

The monster is in the walls. The real monster signs the paperwork.
