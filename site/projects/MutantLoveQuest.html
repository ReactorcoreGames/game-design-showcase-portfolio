Game overview

Mutant Love Quest is a goofy, floaty ragdoll slapfest in a radioactive wasteland. You play a mutant who has matched with someone in the next village over, and everyone between you and them says no: your parents, your village's officials, raiders on the road, their village's officials, and finally their parents.

So you slap some sense into them, one fight at a time, until each one is knocked clean out of the arena and concedes. It plays like a light, trashy take on Super Smash Bros: damage builds up until a big hit sends someone flying off the screen. The twist is your body. Your mutant's head, torso, arms and legs are random in every fight, and you can re-roll one of them mid-fight, so you're always adapting to a body you've never fought with before.

It's a spinoff of Reactorcore's Floppy Heroes remix, a ragdoll gladiator game, set in a world somewhere between Mad Max and the film Idiocracy.

Target audience

People who love the knock-them-off-the-stage chaos of platform fighters and the slapstick of ragdoll games, and like their post-apocalypse silly rather than grim.

They want laughs and warmth, and they're tired of fighting games that ask them to grind one character's combos for months.

WASD, the mouse and five keys. Every fight hands you a new body, so nobody starts out ahead on muscle memory.

Game's purpose

Play and silliness: absurd bodies doing absurd things, and gatekeepers sent flying over the horizon.

Variety: a new set of limbs every fight, and a new one whenever you choose.

Love and connection: every quest ends with two people together, celebrated and remembered.

It avoids draining acceptance: anyone can love anyone, and the only villains are people who say no. A lost fight is never the end: the mutant pulls themselves together and tries again.

Core concept

Platform fighters are about mastering one chosen fighter. Here the fighter is rolled at random, so the skill you build is adapting: reading a new body and finding its trick fast.

A romance usually has one story. Here each quest is a new couple, generated with names, villages and gatekeepers of their own, with no end to the people looking for love.

Lore and story

The glow

A strange radiation washed over the world and turned everyone into super mutants with unstable bodies: limbs that shift and change every so often, heads that do odd things, torsos with a mind of their own. Society carried on anyway, in scrap-metal villages along broken highways, as loud, petty and bureaucratic as ever.

A match

Lonely mutants post on the wasteland's dating board, and yours got a match: a mutant from the neighboring village, with a name, likes and dislikes of their own. Every quest rolls a new pair, of any gender, so love comes in every combination.

Everyone says no

Your own parents say no. The keepers of your village's shrine say no. The officials at the village gate want a form you don't have, and the mayor says no on principle. Raiders block the road. Then the other village starts over: its gatekeepers, its shrine keepers, its mayor, and finally your date's parents, who have Concerns.

None of them is evil. They're just in the way, and in this world, an argument is settled by slapping.

The job right now

Get from your village to theirs, knocking every no out of the arena on the way, and end the day in your date's arms.

Controls and interface

You move your mutant around a side-view arena with WASD and turn it toward the mouse. Each mouse button fires the limbs on one side of your body, whatever they currently happen to be.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    A slapfest in one arena

    You against one to four opponents, all floaty ragdolls with bodies as strange as yours. You look at what you've been given this time, work out what it's good at, wear the opponents down with quick hits, then land the big one that sends them sailing off the edge of the arena.

    When the body you rolled isn't working, you mutate a part and see what you get.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    The quest: two villages, eleven fights

    A quest is one love story in three zones: four fights in your village (parents, shrine keepers, gatekeepers, mayor), three against raiders on the wasteland road, and four in theirs, ending with your date's parents.

    Some fights are one on one, others one against two, three or four, and some opponents are big or hardy, two, four or even eight times harder to knock away. Win the last one, and the quest ends in a still picture of the two of you hugging and kissing.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    Every couple that found each other

    Each new quest generates a new couple: a new protagonist and a new date, with random names, genders, likes and dislikes, new villages with new names, a new cast of gatekeepers, and new arenas.

    Every completed quest joins a list of lovers who found each other, with a group photo of the couple and everyone they had to get past, each labelled with their name underneath. You can look back through it, and share the photos.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A fight, or a whole romance

    A fight takes a few minutes, and a whole quest about an evening. The game saves between fights, so the road waits for you at the next gatekeeper.

Game modes

There's one mode: the quest, one love story at a time, as many as you like. It nests like this:

The list of loversEvery couple you've brought together, and their photos.
A questOne couple, two villages, eleven fights: about an evening.
A zoneYour village, the wasteland road, or theirs.
A fightOne gatekeeper or a gang of them: a few minutes.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Mutation on demand

    Your body is never settled. Every fight starts with a random head, torso and four limbs, drawn from a library of parts that each bring their own attributes, passive abilities or active abilities.

    And once every 30 seconds, you can press X to mutate: one part, chosen at random (head, torso, or any of the four limb sockets), is swapped for another of its kind. Losing badly with stone legs? Roll the dice. You might get something better, or something funnier.

    It turns the fighting game's usual skill, memorizing one character, into a different one: sizing up a body you've never had before and making it work, fast.

  2. A library of body parts

    What each part does

    Each kind of part has its own job. Legs tend to be defensive and passive, arms offensive and active. The torso is passive too, with bonuses the legs don't have, and the head is active, with utility powers:

    PartUsuallyFor example
    LegsPassive, defensiveLegs of stone: you turn slowly, but take far less damage
    ArmsActive, offensiveAn arm that shoots fire like a flamethrower
    TorsoPassive, toggled with QA torso that pushes away anything that gets close
    HeadActive utility, on EA long tongue that works as a grappling hook, pulling you to walls or opponents to you

    The library is the game's content, and it multiplies: every new part combines with every part already in it.

  3. Four sockets, any limb

    Arms where the legs were

    The head and torso always stay where they are, but the four limb sockets don't care what goes in them. An arm can land where a leg was, and a leg where an arm was. You can end up with four arms and no legs, or four legs and no arms, and the controls still work: each mouse button fires whatever is on its side of your body.

  4. Soften them up, then send them flying

    Hurt hits and smash hits

    There's no dismemberment, no armor and no weapons in this one. Instead, damage builds up, shown by a fighter's deepening red tint, and the more damage someone has, the further a big hit sends them. Almost every attack belongs to one of two classes:

    ClassHow it hitsUses built-up damage
    HurtQuick, cheap, low damage, little knockback10%: it rarely sends anyone flying
    SmashSlow, heavy, high damage, big knockback100%: a softened target goes over the horizon

    So a fight has a rhythm: pepper them with hurt hits until they're glowing red, then wait for the opening to land a smash.

  5. Some people are harder to move

    Stubborn gatekeepers

    Opponents are as randomly built as you are, and some of them are simply bigger or hardier: two, four or even eight times harder to knock out of the arena. A mayor like that means working overtime, building up a lot of red before a smash will shift them.

    Group fights (one against two, three or four) are about keeping everyone in front of you, and letting a smash on one send them crashing into the others.

  6. Love doesn't give up

    Knocked out, not done

    If you're the one sent flying, the quest doesn't end. Mutant bodies are hard to keep down: your mutant comes to in a scrap heap or a village healer's hut, pulls themselves back together, and walks back to the same gatekeeper, who's still standing there with their arms folded.

    Nothing is undone and nothing is taken away. You just try again, probably with a different body.
