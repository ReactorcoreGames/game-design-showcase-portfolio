Game overview

Unhinged Lander Heist is a short game in two halves. First you steal canisters of something nobody understands from a guarded lunar bunker. Then you drop them into a little pixel scene you set up yourself, and watch what comes out.

The heist is a side-view physics game: an unlicensed space taxi that flies like Lunar Lander, and a small, sketchy troublemaker in a jetpack with a blaster shotgun and grenades. The bang is a falling-sand simulation in the spirit of The Powder Toy, where each canister releases a different, random kind of living pixel that grows, converts and fights everything around it, Game of Life style.

It's one of Reactorcore's falling-sand games: small, complete and satisfying, about the size of a good Newgrounds or Kongregate game. You're openly the bad guy in this one, and the game is cheerfully honest about it.

Target audience

Players who want a short, sharp blast after a long day: a tense run with a clock on it, then a toy to play with. Lunar Lander fans who like thrust and momentum, and Powder Toy fans who wish the sandbox came with a reason to open it.

The whole game fits in one sitting. Mouse and WASD, with the taxi on four keys.

Game's purpose

Play and silliness: being a troublemaker somewhere that very clearly asked you to leave.

Excitement: an escape with artillery closing in and a fragile getaway vehicle.

Beauty and whim: every detonation is a one-off storm of colour that nobody, including you, can predict.

It avoids draining your time (a whole run is minutes long) and honesty: it never pretends the thief is a hero.

Core concept

Falling-sand games are usually sandboxes with no reason to do anything; arcade games usually ramp up until you lose. Here the heist is the fuse and the sandbox is the bang.

Each half gives the other meaning: what you carry out is exactly what you get to set off, and every scene you set off is kept, so it can be watched again.

Lore and story

The containment site

On the moon, an anomalous materials containment site keeps canisters of things too dangerous and too unpredictable to use. It's a bunker maze of shafts, tunnels and spacious rooms, guarded by fifteen to thirty robots with plasma rifles and watched from far above by a surveillance artillery drone.

The site isn't evil. Its whole job is to keep troublemakers away from the canisters, and it's polite about it at first: "This area is restricted. Turn back and leave."

The troublemaker

You. A small figure in an armored battlesuit rated for space infantry, which raises questions that are best not asked. You arrive in an unlicensed space taxi: a boxy lunar lander that never runs out of fuel and shrugs off bumps, though not gunfire.

The materials

Each canister holds a material that behaves like living pixels. Released, it grows, turns whatever it touches into more of itself, pushes things around, and reacts violently with other materials. No two canisters hold the same thing, and nobody knows what a given one does until it's open. That's the whole reason they're locked up.

The job right now

Land, get in, collect every canister in the maze, get back to the taxi and fly out of the top of the map before the artillery finds you. Then strap a charge to each canister, and find out.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    Thrust, shoot, grab, run

    In the heist it's momentum and nerve: feathering a jetpack through a shaft, bouncing a grenade round a corner onto a guard, grabbing a canister and moving before the shell arrives. In the scene it's the opposite: you take your time placing canisters, then sit back and watch.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    One run

    A run is a freshly generated containment site with three to five canisters in it, followed by one scene of your making. It has a clear start (the landing), a climax (the getaway under fire) and an ending you design yourself.

    If the troublemaker or the taxi is destroyed, that heist is over and the next taxi ride goes to a new site. A run is only minutes long, so trying again is never a big loss.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    The scene log

    Every scene you set up and set off is kept in a log, with the name you gave the place and its seed. Load one and it plays out exactly as it did the first time, without doing the heist again. Over time the log becomes a gallery of your strangest storms.

    That's as deep as the structure goes, on purpose. This is a small game: there are no levels to work through, nothing held back, and the site is always a new maze.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A lunch break

    Start a run and you're landing within seconds. The heist takes about ten minutes, the scene a few more, and Esc stops the show whenever you've seen enough.

    Quit in the middle of a heist and it's saved where you left it. Play one run, or open the log and watch an old favorite again.

Game modes

There's one mode: the run, two stages back to back, plus the log for watching finished scenes again.

  1. LandSet the taxi down somewhere in the maze.
  2. StealFind every canister, past the guards.
  3. EscapeBack to the taxi and out of the top, under fire.
  4. Set the sceneChoose the place, the building, the time of day.
  5. DetonateDrop the canisters and set them off.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Living-pixel payloads

    When a canister detonates, it spawns a burst of one random type of cellular automaton, a different one per canister. Each grows by its own rules, the way Conway's Game of Life patterns do, applies physical force, converts the scene's own pixels into more of itself, and reacts violently when it meets another canister's material.

    Every material spreads in one random colour and flashes a second random colour wherever it touches something that isn't itself or empty space, so the clashes between materials draw their own borders. The usual result is that dirt, concrete, glass and wax are eaten, assimilated or thrown out of the sides and top of the scene in a storm nobody can predict.

    Drop them all and detonate together, or set them off one by one and watch each one meet the last.

  2. The clock

    Artillery that follows you

    The surveillance drone warns you first. Pick up a canister and it gives a last warning to put it down and leave. Pick up the second, and it's authorized to fire.

    From then on it tracks your coordinates and sends an explosive shell to wherever you were, landing 8 seconds later. Each shot after that arrives 0.2 seconds sooner, down to 4, and the drone fires more often, from every 6 seconds down to every 3. The shells don't care whether you're in the taxi, or whether the taxi is parked where you left it.

    It's a timer you can read: the shell always lands where you were, so the answer is always to keep moving.

  3. Thrust and momentum

    A taxi and a jetpack

    The taxi looks and flies like a lunar lander in low gravity: a strong main thruster, weaker side and down thrusters, and no way to turn over. Its fuel never runs out and it shrugs off gentle bumps, so the difficulty is purely in your hands: crash damage grows with speed, and gunfire or shells will wreck it.

    On foot, the troublemaker rotates and thrusts the same way, with air brakes that can hold them hovering in place to line up a shot. Getting in and out takes one key, so you can park the taxi under a shaft, fly up on the jetpack, and drop back in with a canister.

  4. Found in the maze

    A shotgun, grenades and guards

    The blaster shotgun starts with one slug and 99 shots, each flying straight at a constant speed. The grenades start at 10: they fly, slow with friction and gravity, bounce off walls and explode after 3 seconds for triple a slug's damage, so you can bank one round a corner without showing yourself.

    The maze has pickups lying about: an extra slug per shot (with a little more spread, for the same ammo), faster fire, more damage, faster projectiles, ammo in packs of 20 and grenades in packs of 5. They last for the run, like anything you'd find on a real job.

    The guard robots stand their ground with rifles much like your shotgun and fire whenever they can see you. They don't chase, they take a few hits, and when struck they get knocked around like the physics objects they are. Dodge, take cover, or lob a grenade.

  5. Your diorama

    Set the scene

    Before the second stage the game asks: "You got some nasty things there, where do you want to use them?" and gives you a short form. A button randomizes the lot, and the place name has its own roll button.

    SettingChoices
    NameAnything you type, or a rolled one, shown above the scene
    BuildingAn outhouse, a small house, a tall five-floor building, a wide two-floor one, or a big wide four-floor one
    PositionStanding on the ground, or buried underground
    FiguresOne to fifty tiny wax figures dotted around the surface
    TimeDay, night, dusk or dawn: the sky's colour gradient
    TerrainFlat, crater, hill, cliff, gently wavy, or a bridge

    The game builds it in a moment, deliberately simple: muted dirt filling the bottom 15 to 35% of the scene, a jagged strip of indestructible bedrock under it, a grey concrete block with light blue windows, and the wax figures, each one pixel wide and five tall, standing wherever they landed, rooftops included. Then you click open space to drop each canister, and it falls and settles where physics puts it.

  6. Every storm kept

    Seeds and the scene log

    A scene is fully described by its settings and a seed number, so the game saves only those, not a recording. Reloading one rebuilds the scene and lets the same canisters play out exactly as before.

    The log lists each scene by its place name and seed. It costs almost no storage, it never expires, and it means the best thing you ever stole is never really gone.

unlicensed taxi, no questions asked

Unhinged Lander Heist: Anomalous Materials Hooligan

Pickup: one lunar containment site, heavily guarded. Cargo: three to five canisters of something nobody has a name for. Fuel: unlimited. Armor: sketchy. Drop-off: anywhere you like.

They will ask you to leave. They will ask again. Then the shells start falling where you were standing a moment ago, so don't be standing there.

Steal the weird stuff. Find out what it does.
