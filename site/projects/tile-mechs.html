AAL Tile MechsTactical Grid Combat

Reactorcore's Personal Game Design Project

Free to forkNo licence and no copyright claim: build this game under your own name, or lift one idea from it. Why?

Game overview

AAL Tile Mechs looks like a turn-based tactics game and plays in real time. One prototype mecha steps from tile to tile across a 16x16 pixel battlefield in the style of Advance Wars, against a whole regime's army, and every enemy shows you where it will turn and step before it does.

You move the way Cave of Cards moves: one press, one tile, and the action happens the instant you arrive. Everyone on the field, you included, does one thing at a time: move, turn, fire or swap a weapon. That turns a firefight into a readable puzzle that runs at the speed you can think.

Around that sits a battlefield built to be wrecked, after Just Cause 2 and Red Alert 2: grass that burns to ash, sand that a plasma beam turns to glass, fuel depots that go up in chains, and a mech with four hands' worth of slots that picks its next weapon up off the floor. The scale and feel come from Front Mission's wanzers and the 08th MS Team's grounded mechs.

Target audience

Players who love the look of Advance Wars and the loadouts of Front Mission but find waiting through enemy turns slow, and shooter players who wish a firefight were something you could read and outthink instead of out-twitch.

Also anyone who enjoys a chain of explosions they set up themselves.

It's keyboard-first: WASD to step, four keys to fire, with a mouse scheme and a one-handed scheme that never leaves the WASD area. Precision comes from reading the grid, not from aim.

Game's purpose

Power: one mech against an army, and winning.

Excitement and play: mass destruction, fire, glass and chain reactions on a battlefield that keeps every scar.

Mastery and variety: reading telegraphs, choosing tiles, and fighting with whatever the battlefield hands you.

It deliberately avoids draining clarity (every enemy move is shown before it happens), autonomy (regions in any order, restraint or rampage, both supported) and ease (there's always a fresh part to launch with).

Core concept

Turn-based tactics make you wait; real-time shooters bury precision under reflexes. Here the grid makes every situation readable and the clock never stops, because the enemy is slower, clumsier and always telegraphs.

A campaign is a regime's territory broken into regions you take in any order, Mega Man style, each a ten-map gauntlet. There are no upgrade trees: your weapons come off the battlefield and your parts from the engineer's stock.

Lore and story

The Type 3

In the AAL universe, war machines are classed by type. Type 1s are primitive; Type 2s are advanced specialists. Both fight with weapons bolted on at the factory, and the best they have for hands are claws.

A Type 3 is something else: a full-aspect humanoid the size of a Gundam, balanced in armor, weapons, mobility and sensors, and above all fitted with humanlike hands with opposable thumbs. It can drop one weapon and pick up another in the middle of a battle. One of them is an army of one that only needs a base for repairs.

The prototype

You pilot one of the first. It's being field-tested against conventional forces in the gritty old-world eras of the AAL timeline, and the people testing it decided the test might as well do some good: take apart dictatorships, monster nests and anything else that's hurting people, one territory at a time.

The mobile base and its crew

Home is an armed, all-environment mobile base in the tradition of the White Base and Cryptark's ship: it can fly, float and cross space, it can defend itself, and the mech is its spear. It carries a small crew who follow and react to your campaigns: the captain and astrogator, the mechanic and engineer, the scientist and doctor, the janitor and maid, and you.

The captain is an older man, like the admirals of Japanese science fiction, and he throws up whenever the war turns ugly. It isn't aimed at you; the violence around him just does that to him. It happens a lot.

The job right now

A regime holds a territory with its armies, depots and bases. Break it region by region, take out the lieutenant who runs each one, then bring down the fortress at its heart. When it's over the newspapers write it up, and the base sets course for the next one.

Controls and interface

You control one mech, one tile at a time. Moving never changes which way it faces, so you can back away from a tank while keeping your guns on it. Turning is its own action.

A one-handed scheme keeps everything around WASD: R + WASD to turn, Space to fire the selected weapon, Ctrl + WASD to select one of the four, Shift + WASD to swap it with the floor, X for the map. Inputs are buffered, so a turn and a shot pressed together come out in order.

The interface is Advance Wars 2's: compact info boxes on demand, and tabbed screens for the mech, the map and the crew, all navigable with the same keys.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    One battlezone

    A map of grass, roads, sand, forest, water and mountains, full of tanks, bunkers, depots and civilians. You step in, read the floor markers, and pick your moment: a shot down a row of tiles, a step out of a firing line, a fuel tank set off beside a column of trucks.

    Take out every objective, walk to the exit point and press E to leave. Most of the time you'll leave the map visibly worse than you found it.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    A regime's territory

    A campaign is one regime, Mega Man style: its territory splits into regions you take on in any order. Each region is a gauntlet of ten maps, ending with the local lieutenant or leader who runs it. Between maps you're back aboard the base, swapping parts.

    When every region has fallen, the finale opens: the regime's fortress, generated piece by piece like Warning Forever's bosses, and taken apart in one huge chain of explosions. It can fight back, or it can simply be the biggest thing you'll ever demolish.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    The Type 3's record

    The field test never runs out of places to go: there's always another regime, monster nest or threat somewhere. After each campaign the papers run a report on the aftermath, and the base keeps a log entry for it, with headlines and sentences generated from what you actually did.

    Those reports are the game's memory. Spare the civilians and keep the land clean, and the press calls the Type 3 a paragon; leave a scorched, poisoned map behind, and the headlines turn renegade and unhinged. Both are real ways to play and the game stops neither. The log just keeps an honest account of every campaign, like a scrapbook of the prototype's career.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A map at a time

    Open the campaign map, choose a region, and launch. A battlezone takes a few minutes; a region of ten is an evening; a whole regime is a week of evenings.

    The game saves between maps, so stopping costs nothing. If the mech goes down, the base pulls it out, the regime regroups, and that region's gauntlet waits to be started again, freshly generated; the regions you've already taken stay taken.

Game modes

There's one mode: the campaign. It's built in nested pieces, so it already fits any amount of time:

The Type 3's careerEndless: regime after regime, each written up in the log.
A regimeIts territory, its regions in any order, then its fortress.
A regionA ten-map gauntlet ending at a lieutenant: an evening.
A battlezoneOne map of objectives and an exit: a few minutes.

Each battle takes place on one layer at a time: the ground, the sea, underground, underwater, the air, orbit or hyperspace.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Looks turn-based, plays in real time

    Every unit, the mech included, does one thing at a time: move a tile, turn, fire, or swap an item with the floor. You act almost instantly (a tenth of a second, too short to feel). Enemies act after a delay, and they show you what's coming.

    A wide arrow on the floor under each unit shows which way it faces. The tile it's about to step onto lights up with a marker that grows brighter the closer the step is. You always know where a tank will be next and which row of tiles its gun will cover.

    It's one player against many, so the many are slower and clumsier on purpose. That's what keeps it fair, and what turns a firefight into something you read like a board: step out of the lit row, turn, fire down it as the tank arrives.

  2. A battlefield that keeps its scars

    Terrain that changes

    Every tile has a ground type, and what happens on it can change it. Fire is the clearest case: a burning grass tile grows its fire until it's big enough to jump to the tiles beside it (up, down, left and right, never diagonally), and the tile it leaves is ash. Units standing in fire take steady damage; bunkers usually shrug it off, forests usually burn down.

    GroundWhat happens to it
    GrassCatches fire, spreads it slowly, burns out to ash
    SandTurns to glass under plasma: you slide on it like an ice puzzle, but can still change direction mid-slide
    Soft groundDirt, mud, gravel, asphalt, ash and soft stone melt into lava under plasma or atomic blasts, then cool into obsidian craters that can't melt again
    Ice and frozen groundExplosions melt lake ice into water and frozen ground into ordinary ground
    WaterBoils into steam and fog that stops laser weapons
    ForestBullets pass through it; explosions and fire erase it
    MountainUnits can stand on it; anything behind it is in cover; only the heaviest weapons blast it away

    Hardened things like bunkers don't vanish when they break: they become a brittle ruin that bursts apart if you shoot it again. Weaker things go in one hit. The design calls the result the pollution of war, and every map shows it.

  3. Just Cause on a grid

    Explosive props and sabotage

    Regimes keep a lot of volatile things lying around. A fuel tank bursts in a cross along the grid; a fuel silo in a rarer square; a gas tank throws a jet of fire one way; an oil drum spills fire that spreads; a transformer arcs lightning in one to three directions, one to three tiles long. A pressure tank, when shot, launches itself in the direction the bullet was going, as an explosive missile of its own.

    They come in sizes, materials and volatilities, and they set each other off. Some things don't need a shot at all: walk up to a pipeline valve, a transformer regulator or a power box, face it and hold E, and something somewhere on the map goes dark or goes up.

  4. Readable rules

    Shots and blasts on the grid

    Weapons fire only north, south, east or west, by the way their carrier faces. A projectile flies straight at a constant speed from the edge of its tile and stops in the middle of the tile it was meant to reach, so its range is simply a number of tiles. Some weapons fire instantly; heavy ones, and most enemy guns, wind up first.

    Explosions come in Bomberman's cross, plus shaped charges that blow only one, two or three ways, and rare squares. A direct hit never hurts the shooter, but a blast does, so standing next to your own target is a choice. Even machine guns fire in compact packets of rounds, the way Front Mission's combat sounds: one burst, one blob, many shots.

  5. One weapon system

    Every weapon from the same dials

    There is no special code per gun. Every weapon, from a rifle to a beam to a fire hose, is the same set of parameters with different values, so any new weapon works with every unit, every tile and every other weapon from the day it's made:

    DialWhat it sets
    Hit and reachDamage per hit, range in tiles, projectile or instant
    RhythmBurst size, rate of fire, cooldown, wind-up delay, ammo or durability
    Blast shapeNone, cross, three-way, two-way, one-way, square
    ElementFire, kinetic, plasma, laser, particle, EMP, smoke, lava, water, freeze, dirt, chemical, force, hack, hard-light shield, microwave, flashbang, tether
    PushKnockback on hit, knockback from the blast (negative to pull), recoil on the shooter (negative to ram forward)
    StunStops moving, turning, firing or two of them, for how long; how long the shooter staggers
    PenetrationHow many targets a shot passes through
    TrailSomething left on each tile the shot crosses, after Liero's bomblet-dropping gun: fire, steam, gas, goo, foam, chaff, dirt, lava, frost haze, gravity wells, even laser prisms

    That's how the arsenal can run from swords to atomic guns, modern to futuristic, combat to terrain-shaping, without ever needing a new rule.

  6. What hands are for

    Four slots and the floor

    The mech carries four weapons or items, one per fire key. Everything else comes off the battlefield: destroyed units drop what they carried, and you step onto the tile and swap it into a slot, leaving your old one on the floor.

    A tile holds one floor item at most. If something blows up over a tile that already has one, the old item is crushed and replaced by the new drop. So the battlefield keeps handing you new tools, and keeps taking the ones you didn't pick up.

  7. Light Armored Core

    Parts, terrain and stamina

    The mech is built from a top, a bottom and internal parts. Movement works like Advance Wars 2, except fuel is stamina, refilled by the mech's reactor: each ground type has a cost, and each bottom multiplies it, or can't cross it at all.

    • Legs take recoil and knockback in their stride.
    • Hover crosses almost anything at full speed, but recoil and knockback push it around.
    • Spider legs walk over mountains and obstacles, slowest of all.
    • Wheels on roads and rollers on rails move for free.

    Parts also set hitpoints, stamina and recharge rate, and carry traits: fire resistance, a better melee strike, being impossible to shove, or a drawback like only two of the four slots holding weapons. Every unit's mass decides what happens when one walks into another: blocked, shoved, shoved and hurt, or crushed against a wall. Enemies follow exactly the same rules.

    Parts wear with use, so they rotate through maintenance, the way XCOM: Long War rotates tired soldiers. The engineer always keeps a fresh stock, so you're never left without something to launch with, and you'll find yourself fighting in builds you'd never have tried.

  8. Ground, sea, sky and orbit

    Strike packs and battle layers

    The core mech is at home on the ground, on the sea surface, in shallow water and underground. For the sky and space, the mobile base launches a strike pack, as in Gundam SEED: a jet unit that docks onto the mech and makes it airborne. It changes how every part behaves, and brings that layer's rules with it, so you only take it where you need it. A hyperspace add-on extends it for the stretches between.

    LayerHow it moves
    Ground and undergroundRegular combat with terrain
    SeaLike the ground, mostly water; the mech wades like Advance Wars infantry
    UnderwaterEverything slower, automatic braking, shorter knockback and recoil
    AirConstant motion, a minimum speed, limited turns and stalling, or hovering in place
    OrbitMomentum drift: you thrust the other way to stop
    HyperspaceThe zone squeezes toward the middle of the route and opens at the exit; turning and strafing only

  9. Content that multiplies

    One sprite per thing

    Everything is 16x16 pixels. Ground tiles are seamless textures. Units and objects are single side-view sprites that face left or right by flipping; the floor arrow shows whether they face up or down. Muzzle flashes, fire and blasts are particles and overlays, not new frames.

    So a new unit, structure or prop costs one sprite and a list of values. Opponents can be any 16x16 thing: a regime's tanks and trucks in Advance Wars style, space monsters, dungeon freaks, rabid animals, robots, cyborgs, the undead. Each set can be its own expansion, with its own structures, objectives and tiles, and every new one works with everything already in the game.

Field test orders

AAL Tile Mechs: Tactical Grid Combat

Unit: one prototype Type 3, four slots, two hands. Opposition: a regime's entire army. Terrain: flammable.

Watch the floor. Step out of the lit row. Turn, fire, and let the fuel depot finish the column. Pick up whatever they drop and keep walking. The captain will need a bucket.

One mech. One tile at a time. The whole regime.
