Cathartic DismountPrecognition Ragdoll Finisher

Reactorcore's Personal Game Design Project

Free to forkNo licence and no copyright claim: build this game under your own name, or lift one idea from it. Why?

Game overview

Cathartic Dismount is a small physics game about wrecking someone who truly has it coming. Each time, you get a randomly built hazard, a randomly posed ragdoll and a random way to set it off. You choose how the strike goes, watch the fall, and at the moment you pick, deliver one slow-motion finisher.

Before anything happens for real, you see it in a precognition vision: run the fall as many times as you like through a dreamlike filter, adjust, and only then make it real. The real run happens once, and so does the finisher.

It's a remix of the Stair Dismount and Truck Dismount games, with Toribash-style ragdolls that can be cut and broken apart, the precognition of Katana Zero, the slow-motion cuts of Metal Gear Rising: Revengeance, and the target profiles of Watch Dogs, all kept in the bare grey primitives of the originals.

Target audience

Adults who come home angry at the world and want somewhere harmless to put it: people who'd enjoy a punching bag more if it could fall down a flight of stairs.

They want release without effort. Physics sandboxes ask them to build a scene first, and fighting-game fatalities ask for memorized inputs under a timer.

The interface is a mouse and a few keys, and nothing depends on reflexes: time slows to a near stop whenever precision matters.

Game's purpose

Catharsis: anger at cruelty and injustice is real, and needs an outlet. Here it goes into a fall that hurts nobody real.

Power and choice: small but meaningful control over exactly how a terrible person's end goes.

Ease: the feeling of tossing a ball up and batting it out of the air at exactly the right moment, without needing the skill or speed to pull it off.

It deliberately avoids draining rest (no setup, no reflex tests) and safety: the targets are built from behaviors, never from real people or groups, and there's no score for suffering.

Core concept

Dismount games repeat one staircase and score the damage, so the fun becomes bug-hunting for bigger numbers.

Here every scenario is generated from randomized frames, poses and strikes, and there's no score at all. The point is the one ending you chose.

Precognition removes trial and error from the real thing: you rehearse freely, then commit once, and the finality is what makes it land.

Lore and story

A bare world

The game takes place in a world of grey concrete primitives under a bright fog: cubes, cylinders, ramps and slabs arranged into hazards. A metro platform, a flight of stairs, a road with traffic, a grinder, a press, a pit lined with bars. No textures, no detail, nothing to look at but the fall. It's the look of the original dismount games, kept on purpose.

The targets

Every ragdoll is a unique, randomly generated person, and every one of them is irredeemable. Each has a name, a set of colors, a belief they hold, a terrible thing they did in the past and are proud of today, and a terrible thing they intend to do soon.

Their wrongs are built from behaviors anyone could fall into, like cruelty, greed, contempt and the joy of hurting others. Never from who someone is: no nationality, faith, group or real person is ever the target.

The vision

You never appear. You're the one who sees ahead: every possible version of the fall plays out first as a vision, softened and dreamlike, and you choose which one happens. When the filter drops, it's real, and real only happens once.

Controls and interface

You don't build anything and you don't control the ragdoll. The game hands you a scenario already set up, with the target in place and guaranteed to be hit. You choose how the strike goes, and when and how to finish. A suggested layout:

A dreamlike filter over the whole screen tells you whether you're watching a vision or the real thing, so there's never any doubt which run counts. In slow motion, the finisher's cut line or impact point is drawn on the body before you commit to it.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    The strike, the fall, the finish

    Set the strike's direction and power, and the ragdoll goes: off the platform, down the stairs, into the fan. Its body reacts to every surface it meets, joint by joint.

    At any moment after the strike, time slows almost to a stop. You pick a body part, a finisher and its strength, and land it at the exact point of the fall you wanted.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    One case

    A case is one target in one scenario. It opens with the target's profile: who they are, what they did, what they're about to do. Then the scene, already set.

    You rehearse in precognition until the fall looks right, make it real, deliver the finisher, and it's over. A case has a clear beginning and an ending that can't be undone, usually within a couple of minutes.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    The case archive

    There's always another case: the scenario generator and the target generator never run dry, and no two cases come out alike.

    Every closed case goes into an archive: the target's profile, the scenario, and how it ended, with a still frame of the finisher. It's a record of what you did, kept for you to look back on, never a score or a collection to complete.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    Two minutes, or twenty

    The game opens straight into a case. There are no menus to get through, no level to build and no setup to do.

    Play one case after a bad meeting, or ten in a row after a bad week, and stop whenever the anger has somewhere to go. Nothing waits on you and nothing is lost by leaving.

Game modes

There's one mode: the dismount, an endless run of cases. What changes from case to case is the scenario, and with it the way the strike comes. The game rolls one of four strike methods for each scenario:

StrikeWhat happensYou choose
PushThe ragdoll is shoved, hit or launched straight at something hazardous: a train, a staircase, traffic, fan bladesDirection, power, and whether the hit is soft, hard or cutting
RideThe ragdoll sits on a moving platform or vehicle that crashes into something stationaryThe launch speed and angle
ShotA projectile is fired at the ragdoll somewhere the hit can send it into troubleThe aim and force
ImpaleA simple spike-shaped projectile embeds in a body part and drags the ragdoll along with itWhich body part, and where it's flung

The scenarios are where the variety lives: pushed off a metro platform, down stairs, into busy traffic, into dull or sharp fan blades, into a giant rotating bat, through a fence, into a grinder, down a pit of bars that bats the body around like a pachinko ball; pancaked by a press from above, from two sides or from four corners; hit on the tracks, on a road by a truck, a bus, a racing car or a tunnel boring machine; riding a mine cart into a bridge too low, or loosely strapped to the front, top, side or back of a vehicle. Any of them can also happen in a tunnel or a closed room.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Precognition

    Every dismount starts as a vision, the way Katana Zero's hero sees each fight before it happens. Set the strike, play the vision, watch the whole fall, rewind and change it, as many times as you want. A dreamlike filter covers every vision, so it can never be mistaken for the real thing.

    When you're happy, you make it real, and the filter drops. The physics runs from the same start, so what you rehearsed is what you get, and you don't have to hope a lucky bounce repeats.

    That removes the frustration of trial and error from the moment that matters, without removing the choice. You still decide everything: the precognition only lets you see your decision before you make it.

  2. No two alike

    Randomized frames and poses

    Each scenario is a frame with randomized parts rather than a hand-built level. A train always smashes into the ragdoll, but the front of the train can be any shape: one block, a shape made of smaller shapes, several copies of one shape, or a jumble of different ones. Every hazard works the same way.

    The ragdoll starts in a pose that's sensible for a real body (sitting, standing, kneeling, prone, lying, crouching, even a handstand), with its torso, head, arms and hands rotated at random within their natural limits. It's always placed so the hit is guaranteed.

    Frames, poses and strikes multiply each other, so a handful of scenarios becomes thousands of different falls, all without anyone placing anything by hand.

  3. Slow motion

    The finisher

    At any moment after the strike, you can drop into slow motion, as in Revengeance's blade mode, and deliver a finisher. It snaps to the body part you point at, and its strength goes anywhere from the minimum lethal hit to complete overkill. A second and a third hit can follow, if you want them.

    FinisherWhat it does
    SliceA single cut, an X-cut, or a double cut 5 to 35 degrees apart, with a katana, a two-handed greatsword or an oversized broad blade
    SmashA kinetic force punch
    LaunchFire a simple physics shape into the body hard enough to break it apart
    BlastDetonate one body part, carrying the fall's momentum
    ImpaleEmbed a spike shape instantly and fling it, the body with it; a smear of motion shows it going in, so it never looks teleported

  4. Once, for real

    The finisher can't be rehearsed

    The fall can be planned in precognition as often as you like. The finisher can only be done once, in the real run, and there's no rewind afterwards.

    That's deliberate. The release comes from finality: the target is truly gone, and it can't be taken back. A finisher you could retry forever would turn into practice, and the feeling would drain out of it.

  5. Who's at the top of the stairs

    Target profiles

    Like the passers-by in Watch Dogs, every target is generated with a profile: a name, their colors, a belief, a heinous thing they did and are proud of, and a heinous thing they plan to do soon. Reading it takes seconds, and it's what makes the case feel like justice instead of cruelty.

    The profiles are assembled from behaviors and deeds, never from real people, nationalities, faiths or other groups. The anger they draw out is the anger at what people do, and that's where it stays.

  6. Grim, not gleeful

    Sound and gore

    Meaty impacts, gnarly grunts, and screams that are suppressed rather than dramatic: less a movie scream and more the quiet, eerie "oof, that's grim" of something that really hurt. Bodies cut, break and come apart, with blood kept to stains and spatters on the concrete.

    The tone keeps it honest. Nothing cheers, no points pop up for damage, and suffering is never the reward. The game shows the end of a terrible person plainly, and lets that be enough.

Trivia

The original Stair Dismount (2002), a demoscene group's game, had a backstory: a superhero couldn't claim tax deductions for the damage he caused the city on duty unless he could prove he'd been injured too. Pushing him down the stairs was a favor.

Case file attached

Cathartic Dismount: Precognition Ragdoll Finisher

They did something unforgivable, and they're proud of it. They're planning to do it again. Right now they're standing at the top of a very long staircase.

You've already seen how this ends. You've seen it a dozen ways. Pick the one you like, and when the fog lifts, make it real.

You only get to finish it once.
