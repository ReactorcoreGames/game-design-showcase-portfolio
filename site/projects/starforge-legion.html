Starforge LegionMecha Space Combat

Reactorcore's Personal Game Design Project

Free to forkNo licence and no copyright claim: build this game under your own name, or lift one idea from it. Why?

Game overview

Starforge Legion is an arcade flight-combat game where your fighter jet becomes a mecha the moment you need one. You fly for a band of troubleshooters for hire, across planets, moons, orbits and nebulae, and take apart the fortresses of the worst people in a settled galaxy, one ten-stage campaign at a time.

It's the bigger sibling of Thunderstrike Justice, a simpler fighter-jet game set on one world. It shares that game's foundations and moves them into a space-opera universe inspired by the anime Crusher Joe, with jets that transform like the ones in Macross and the game Gunmetal, the weapon designs of Gundam 00 and Gundam Wing, and the raw, overkill explosions of Michael Bay's Transformers films.

Target audience

Players who grew up on Star Fox 64's all-range mode, Ace Combat, Gunmetal or a Macross anime: people who want to fly fast and blow up big things without reading a flight manual.

They're hungry for power and spectacle in sessions that end, and put off by flight sims that take a week to learn and by arcade shooters that run out after one playthrough.

Mouse aim, WASD and a transform key. Locking on is automatic and the enemies are many but slow-witted, so the challenge is in the flying, not in split-second reflexes.

Game's purpose

Power and catharsis: a machine better than anything it faces, turned on the people who most deserve it.

Excitement and play: speed, transforming mid-flight, and explosions far bigger than they need to be.

Meaning: every campaign ends one named villain, and the win log keeps the record.

It deliberately avoids draining rest (a stage takes minutes, and you can stop between any two) and clarity (no stealth, no jamming: the radar shows everything).

Core concept

Flight-combat games usually make you choose: a jet game where you keep overshooting the fight, or a mecha game with no sense of speed. Here you switch between the two at will, on battlefields big enough for both.

And instead of a scripted campaign that's over in a weekend, every contract is ten generated stages ending at a generated villain, and the next contract is always a different one. Winning is normal.

Lore and story

The Starforge Legion

Out among the settled planets and moons, the law reaches only as far as the nearest fleet, and the worst people know it. Warlords, slavers, crime bosses, cult leaders and corporate tyrants build their fortresses where nobody can touch them.

The Starforge Legion is a band of troubleshooters for hire, in the spirit of the crushers of Crusher Joe: freelance pilots who take the jobs nobody else can, flying transforming jet-mecha forged for exactly that work. Their clients range from a mining colony with nowhere else to turn to a government that can't be seen to act.

Where the work is

The jobs go everywhere: low over the oceans and deserts of living worlds, across airless moons in low gravity, through asteroid belts and wreck fields, into nebula clouds thick enough to slow a missile, and along orbits where anything that falls too far burns up.

The targets

Every contract ends with one person. Each gets a generated name and a two-word profile, a flaw and a crime: a narcissist dictator, a sociopath slaver, a greedy oligarch, an apathetic warlord, a manipulative cult leader. Their portraits stay vague silhouettes in soft tints, because they aren't anyone in particular. They're what people become when power meets one of those flaws.

Around each one stands everything that keeps them in power: slave camps, black sites, propaganda stations, poison-drug labs, polluting refineries, surveillance hubs, and the walled enclaves of the people who profit.

The job right now

Take the contract, fly the ten stages, and erase the name at the end of them. Then take the next one.

Controls and interface

You fly one machine with two forms. In jet mode it always moves forward, turns slowly and is the fastest thing on the battlefield. In mecha mode its top speed and acceleration drop, but it turns instantly, dodges sideways and flies in any direction on its boosters. The HUD keeps it simple: your machine's state, the current weapon and its ammunition, speed and altitude, a compass and position, the kill list of targets left, and a round radar that shows enemies as dots when they're level with you and as up or down arrows when they're above or below. The current target always has a red box around it, shrinking to a dot when it's far, and an arrow at the screen's edge shows the quickest way to turn toward it.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    One battle, one cube of sky

    Every battle happens inside a cube ten kilometers on a side. You arrive already in flight at its edge, in a one-kilometer margin that's yours, and the enemies, bases and supply caches fill the eight kilometers in the middle. Three seconds in comes "all systems go", and the targeting locks onto the nearest enemy.

    Then it's strafing runs in jet mode across the long distances, a transform whenever something needs facing, an engine cut to drift and spin round for a strafing run in reverse, and a kill list that shrinks until the battle is clear.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    Ten stages to the target

    A contract opens on its target: a silhouette, a name and a profile. Then comes a gauntlet of ten generated stages, shown as a row of ten boxes with an arrow on the one you're about to enter. Each stage gets a random difficulty, so the curve might run hard, easy, hard. The target waits in the tenth.

    Lose your machine and the contract is lost, and back you go to the start screen. A lost contract never enters the win log and its seed is retired: the universe moves on, and so does the target. The next contract is a new one.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    The win log

    The win log is the Legion's history book: every contract you've completed, with its name, every stage's completion time and the total. Any contract or single stage can be flown again as a flashback, from its stored seed, to fly it better; a faster time updates the record.

    In a flashback the target is already gone, so they appear as a faceless shadow with a garbled name. What you're replaying is the challenge, not the kill. The pilots lost along the way are remembered in the log too, each under their own call sign, which is never used again.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A stage at a time

    From the start screen, continue the current contract, start a new one, or open the win log. A stage is a few minutes of flying. After its last kill, the world slows to a standstill over five seconds while you swing the camera around the wreckage, and the next stage waits until you start it.

    Stop between any two stages and the contract waits where you left it. One stage over coffee or a whole contract in an evening; nothing in the game cares which.

Game modes

There's one mode, the contract, plus flashbacks of the contracts you've won. It's built in nested pieces you can stop between:

The win logEndless: contract after contract, each one remembered.
A contractTen generated stages and one target: an evening or two.
A stageOne battle in a ten-kilometer cube: a few minutes.
The kill listEvery target in the cube, cleared one by one.

Flashbacks replay any won contract or single stage from its seed. They change your times in the log, and nothing else.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Jet or mecha, at will

    You can transform at any moment, and each form is the right tool for a different part of a battle:

    Jet modeMecha mode
    SpeedFastest top speed and accelerationAbout 60% of the jet's
    TurningWide and slow; tighter at lower speedOn the spot
    MovementAlways forward, plus strafingAny direction, driven by boosts
    CollisionsDangerous at speedShrugged off unless the momentum is extreme

    Hit terrain in jet mode and the machine snaps into mecha mode on impact. To fly as a jet again, move clear and build up some speed first. A jet that slows too much stalls and starts to fall, but stalling alone never forces a transformation: only a collision does.

    Machines are premade, each with its own armor, speed, and weapon and item slots, and a reusable special ability, like the ships of Cryptark. Every one starts with one reliable weapon and the rest rolled at random, and you choose the color of your booster flames.

  2. Momentum

    Drift, boost and skate

    Both forms can cut the engine and drift on their momentum, as in Freelancer, which turns them faster still. Cut the engine at full speed, swing around and you can strafe a target while sliding away from it: a strafing run that you throw yourself into.

    The overboost is the long-range burst, the fastest you'll ever go. On a planet's surface the mecha can land and skate along the ground under gravity, or walk; a double tap switches between the two, and another between the ground and the air.

  3. Distances and speeds

    Ten kilometers to cross

    Every battlefield is a cube ten kilometers on each side, so speed matters and every mode of travel has its place:

    SpeedWhat flies that fastCrossing the cube
    100 km/hJet cruise (a mecha cruises at about 60 km/h)6 minutes
    200–300 km/hMecha boost; jet afterburner2 to 3 minutes
    600 km/hOverboost1 minute

    Bullets and beams fly two to four times as fast as you; missiles and rockets come slow, medium and fast, at half to four-fifths of your speed, so a fast enough pilot can outrun one. Reach the cube's edge and you bounce off it and get launched back in, as in Star Fox 64's battle arenas.

    Your one-kilometer edge also holds the support vessel: a floating platform, a crawler, a carrier boat, a blimp, a mothership or a small depot, depending on the world. Fly into it to repair, rearm and refuel, then go back in.

  4. Weapons from shapes

    One stats sheet, endless guns

    Every weapon is one self-contained object, as in Hotline Miami, made from one of three simple kinds of attack:

    KindHow it works
    ProjectileLeaves the barrel, flies and hits. No gravity, no arcs: it goes where it's pointed.
    Charged areaA cone, cylinder, line or sphere that visibly charges up to 100%, then strikes after a short delay.
    Expanding shapeA sphere, cylinder, line or cone that grows outward and hits on contact, instantly.

    One stats sheet turns those into everything from a pistol to a planet-cracker: range, area, charge time, fire rate, projectiles per shot, spread, speed and acceleration, how hard it homes, how fast it locks on, recoil on you and push or pull on the target, screen shake, mass, how long it takes to come online after you pick it up, and how many hits it survives when thrown.

    Starforge Legion also has weapons that come in stages, which its simpler sibling leaves out: split missiles, cluster munitions, grenades that redirect themselves, and gun drones that fly off and fight on their own.

  5. Their guns are your guns

    Take it, use it, throw it

    Enemies carry the same weapons you do, as in Einhänder: destroy one and its gun is yours for the taking. You carry two to four at once and can grab oversized weapons left on the battlefield, the way Lost Planet's soldiers pick up mech guns.

    A weapon's mass matters both ways. Heavy guns slow your acceleration, top speed, turning and boosts, but a heavy gun you're done with makes a heavy thing to throw, and a solid melee weapon hits harder the faster you swing it. Ammunition is generous, in the style of Super Cryptark, so spraying is fine; only the rare superweapons need saving.

    Friendly fire is on for everyone. Your own blast can catch you, and enemies blunder into each other's shots all the time.

  6. Overkill

    Hyper weapons and elemental finishes

    Some weapons are deliberately, massively overkill, after the UT99 mod Nali Weapons 3. Its best idea comes along too: an overcharger that pushes a regular weapon up to a superweapon, and a superweapon up to a hyper weapon.

    Every weapon has an elemental type that decides how its kills go off, from a plasma flash to a cloud of shrapnel. Explosions are expanding spheres in the spirit of Star Fox 64 and Bomberman Hero, with the brutal punch of a Transformers film. A big enough superweapon can even boil off part of a planet's sea, dropping the water level for the rest of the battle.

  7. Worlds in layers

    Ground, orbit and open space

    A ground world is a stack of layers: a flat floor, an optional sea on top of it, and atmosphere up to the ceiling. Big indestructible terrain sits on the floor (islands where there's water, hills, mesas, dunes and mountains where there isn't), and smaller destructible things are scattered over it: seaweed under the water, trees on the ground, clouds in the air.

    Gravity is real there, stronger on a heavy world and weaker on a moon. Flyers resist it until something knocks them around; ground units knocked into the air fall, and a hard enough landing breaks them. Push or bait an enemy into terrain and the collision does the damage for you. Air and water add drag: weapons lose range, movement turns sluggish, and slow projectiles fade and detonate. Some weapons lay smoke fields that make drag on purpose, and a plasma lance cuts through it.

    In space there's no floor, no gravity and no drag, except in nebula pockets. Orbital battles have a planet below and a burn-up zone that destroys anything that falls into it. Comets, debris and drifting wrecks sometimes cross the cube from one side to the other.

  8. Built from rules

    Battlefields that generate themselves

    No battlefield is built by hand. On a ground world, the generator casts a ray down from the ceiling to find the highest solid ground, places a target or a cluster of them there, and clears the destructible scenery around it so nothing spawns inside a tree. In space it grows the battlefield outward from the center:

    1. ZonesRings around the center: core, inner, outer and edge, each a nebula, a void, a debris field or a minefield
    2. BudgetHow many objects, how far out, and an off-center starting point
    3. SpokesA direction and a number of spokes, spirals or branches
    4. Step outPlace one, step along the spoke, place the next
    5. Clear a bubbleEach placed object clears the space around it

    A handful of rules and a random number give an endless supply of battlefields, each with its own shape, and every new kind of object or zone shows up in all of them. Big scenery is kept sparse, about a hundred pieces always loaded, so there's always room to fly fast.

  9. The opposition

    Many of them, none of them clever

    Tanks, anti-air batteries, missile sites, jets, drones, helicopters, gunboats, trucks, artillery, blimps, gunships, bunkers, and explosive silos that set each other off. Each is a vehicle with mounts for one to four weapons, the same weapons you use. They make up for their numbers by being individually worse than your machine: slow, delayed, unable to lead a shot, and happy to shoot through their own side.

    BehaviorWhat it does
    PatrolMoves about and shoots
    SentrySits tight and shoots: far and accurate, but slow to track
    ParkedDoesn't move or shoot: buildings and idle vehicles
    ConvoyJust drives
    BerserkComes straight at you, shooting
    AvoidantKeeps away from you, shooting
    HunterBerserk from the very start of the battle
    ThiefHunts you, backs off when it gets close, and comes back when you're far

    Nobody retreats, repairs, sneaks or jams. Everything fights to the last, and there's no stealth on either side.

Contract offered

Starforge Legion: Mecha Space Combat

Client: withheld. Target: one name, one profile, one fortress at the far end of ten battles. Location: a moon, an orbit, a nebula, a sea. Payment: on completion.

Your machine is a jet when you need distance and a mecha when you need a fight. It carries whatever it can take off the wreckage. The opposition is many, slow and badly led, and every one of them is in your way.

Fly the ten stages. Erase the name.
