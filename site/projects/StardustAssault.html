Game overview

Stardust Assault is a side-view, auto-scrolling shoot 'em up that mixes R-Type, Missile Command and Space Invaders. You fly a starmecha, a starfighter-sized robot, into the alien hive inside a planetoid, aiming freely in every direction, against swarm after swarm of colourful geometric invaders.

Every invader you destroy bursts into falling sand dust, simulated grain by grain, that starts in its own colour and burns through fiery neon to black before it's gone. The rarest, darkest embers stay behind and drift, and over the length of a run they build up into a nebula behind the battle. When you finally blow up the hive's core, the whole canvas is saved as an artwork, with you posing in the middle of it.

It's one of Reactorcore's series of small falling sand games, each a short, complete game built on a pixel simulation, and this page presents it at that honest size.

Target audience

Shoot 'em up fans who wish they could aim anywhere, and anyone who loves the particle spectacle of Geometry Wars.

They want flow and spectacle, and they're put off by shmups that demand memorizing bullet patterns and get harder until you lose.

WASD to fly, the mouse to aim, three keys for missile, shield and teleport.

Game's purpose

Excitement and play: the fast combat dance of dodging, aiming and clearing a swarm.

Power: one well-armed starmecha against countless invaders, getting stronger with every wave.

Beauty and creation: every run paints a picture nobody else will ever have.

It respects the player's time: you choose how long the run is before it starts, and it never ramps up until you lose.

Core concept

Shmups usually escalate stage by stage until you fail, and leave nothing behind but a score.

Here the waves are random but never stronger, while your power keeps stacking, so finishing is normal. The length is yours to choose.

And the run leaves something behind: a painting made of every explosion in it.

Lore and story

The invaders

They come in every shape and colour, triangles, cubes, rings and stars, small and large, always in formations and always in numbers. Individually they're fragile. Together they're endless.

The hive

They pour out of a hive grown inside a planetoid, layer upon layer of alien tunnels around a single glowing core that makes them, one after another, for as long as it exists.

The assault

So instead of waiting for the next wave, someone takes the fight to them: one starmecha, flying into the planetoid itself, through every swarm between the surface and the core.

The goal

Reach the heart of the hive and blow it up, and be there, striking a pose, when it goes.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    One wave

    A stage is one wave of 10 to 40 invaders, mixed shapes and sizes in a random formation. They fly in together, then decide whether to keep their formation or scatter, and bounce around the stage firing the odd shot.

    You weave between them, carve lines through them with the mass driver and drop missiles where they bunch up. The last one drops an artifact, and about two seconds later the next wave arrives.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    An assault on the hive

    An assault is a run of rapid stages, 20, 80 or 200, chosen before you start, with your powers stacking all the way through. After the last wave, the core appears: a huge orb that spawns an invader every second, up to ten at a time, and that explodes, on any hit at all, in a burst four times bigger than any ship's.

    Pick your mecha's colour before you launch: it's the colour you'll paint the hive with if you're hit.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    A gallery of hives

    Every destroyed hive leaves its canvas: the saved picture of the final blast, layered with every explosion of the run and your mecha posing in the middle. The canvases are kept together as a gallery, each one unique, sparse and minimal after a short assault, dense and rich after a long one, and easy to share as an image.

    There's always another hive to fly into, and another picture to paint.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A coffee break or an evening

    A wave lasts well under a minute, so a short assault fits a coffee break, a medium one an evening, and the endurance run several sittings. The game saves between stages, so even a 200-stage assault can be left and picked up again, its nebula intact.

Features and mechanics

  1. From Missile Command

    Missiles that bloom where you point

    Press Q and a missile leaves the starmecha and flies to the exact spot under the mouse pointer. There it detonates into a blast circle that slowly grows, reaches its full size, slowly shrinks and disappears, as in the classic Missile Command. Every invader that touches it while it lasts is obliterated.

    That makes it a tool for space, not just a weapon: you place a lingering wall of fire where the swarm is about to bounce, and let them fly into it. You're immune to all your own weapons, so you can sit right in the middle of the blast; the invaders, likewise, are immune to theirs.

  2. Three tools, three shapes

    The arsenal

    WeaponHow it worksBest for
    Mass driverA rapid burst of high-velocity rounds, like an A-10's cannon, that tears right through everything in its pathA whole line of invaders at once
    Frag shotgunSlow-firing; a wide spread of superheated pellets that stop at the first thing they hitA cluster in front of you (the ones hiding behind it survive)
    MissileFlies to the pointer and leaves a growing, lingering blast circleWhere the swarm is going to be

    Because the formations bounce around the stage, a long mass driver burst is also a trap: hold the line of rounds in place and let the bouncing invaders run into it.

  3. Ways out

    Shield and mirror teleport

    Holding E raises a shield that blocks everything while it lasts: about three seconds in total, draining as you use it, and recharging from empty to full in nine seconds when you let go.

    X teleports the starmecha to the mirror image of where it is, flipped through the centre of the stage: from the top left corner straight to the bottom right. Cornered by a formation? You're on the other side of the screen.

  4. Quantity against quality

    Invaders that never ramp up

    Every invader dies to a single hit. The starmecha takes five hits or collisions before it's destroyed. They have the numbers; you have the quality.

    And the waves don't get stronger or weaker as the assault goes on: any wave can hold any kinds of invader, in any formation, at stage 3 or stage 193. Your power grows and theirs doesn't, so a long run turns into a spectacle of mastery rather than a grind toward an inevitable death.

  5. Power that stacks

    Artifacts

    The last invader of every wave drops an artifact, and its effects stack for the rest of the assault:

    ArtifactEffect
    RepairRestores one hit point, if you're missing any
    MissileBlast radius and duration +5%
    Mass driverBurst duration +5%: a longer line of rounds
    ShotgunPellet velocity +5%, so more range
    Defence orbAn orb circles the mecha and absorbs up to three shots or collisions; up to six at once

    Over 200 stages that adds up to a starmecha whose missiles fill half the screen and whose bursts stretch across it.

  6. The heart of it

    Falling stardust, and the canvas

    The game runs in three layers. The ships, invaders and shots live in the game layer. In front of it, a falling sand layer carries every weapon trail, explosion and burst of debris as simulated grains: when anything blows up, it shatters into dust in its own colour, the invader's or your mecha's, which shifts to a fiery neon, then to black, then disappears.

    Behind them all, a third layer catches the rarer, dark and muted embers thrown out by ship explosions. Those don't disappear. They float and gather through the whole assault, building a painterly nebula behind the battle. Three seconds after the core blows, with you striking whatever pose you like in the blast, the canvas is saved: a picture of that one run.

Mission brief

Stardust Assault: Twin-Stick Mecha Shooter

Target: the invader hive, deep inside the planetoid. Assets: one starmecha, in the colour of your choosing. Opposition: uncountable.

Route length: your call. Twenty stages, eighty, or two hundred. Objective: reach the core and destroy it. Recommended: strike a pose when it goes.

Fly in. Light it up. Leave a masterpiece.
