Mafia TakedownDismemberment Ragdoll Vigilante

Reactorcore's Personal Game Design Project

Game overview

Mafia Takedown is a side-view ragdoll fighting game about one ordinary person who decides a gang has run their town for long enough, and goes in through the front door alone. Room by room, with whatever's lying around, they fight their way up to the people at the top.

Every fighter is a floppy paper doll with armor on each body part. You win a fight by bumping your weapon or your boots into the right part of the right person: armor breaks first, then limbs come off, and a lost head or chest ends it. It's slow, physical and tricky, and the vigilante will very likely die along the way.

That's not the end. When a vigilante falls, someone who knew them takes up the mask and starts again, until the gang is gone. The game is a spinoff of Reactorcore's Floppy Heroes remix, a ragdoll gladiator game, retold in the spirit of Kick-Ass and Sin City, with the succession of The Phantom comics.

Target audience

Players who love physics fights where every hit is a real collision (Toribash, Stair Dismount, Floppy Heroes), and the old fantasy of the bystander who finally stands up to the bullies.

They're tired of two kinds of death: the reload that makes failure meaningless, and the permadeath that wipes everything and teaches them to stop taking risks.

Controls are mostly the mouse, plus a few keys. Fights are slow and deliberate, so positioning matters far more than reflexes.

Game's purpose

Challenge and mastery: reading armor, angles and recoil, and outlasting a whole building of opponents.

Meaning: deaths are permanent and they count. Each fallen vigilante is remembered by name, and the one who finishes the job does it for all of them.

Protection: force used to free a town from people who hurt it, not to punish anyone.

It avoids draining autonomy (you pick which towns to help and how hard the first attempt is) and fairness (the same rules bind everyone, and the gang is predictable on purpose).

Core concept

Most games treat the hero's death in one of two ways: it never happened (reload), or nothing you did matters any more (start from scratch).

Here, death is real and the mission continues. The vigilante stays dead, the thugs they took down stay down, and the next person carries on from the front door.

Each takedown is a complete story with an ending. When it's done, another town needs someone.

Lore and story

Towns with a problem

Every story happens in a different town, each with its own name, and each run by its own gang with its own name. The gangs aren't any real-world group. They're defined by what they do: protection rackets, threats, front businesses, people who are afraid to talk. They hide in corporate towers, office floors, apartment blocks, malls and fenced compounds.

Someone who's had enough

The vigilante is nobody special: a person with a mask, an alias and a grudge on behalf of their town. They're the only one brave enough, or angry enough, to try taking the whole organization apart on their own.

The mask passes on

If the vigilante dies, the alias doesn't. Someone close to them puts on the mask out of respect and carries on: their mom or dad, a brother, sister or cousin, an uncle, a grandparent, their own grown child, a friend, a friend of a friend, a classmate, a colleague, an inspired fan, a local activist, a police officer or a paramedic who saw too much, a defector from the gang itself, a bored teenager, a local weirdo, or a total stranger nobody can explain.

Each successor has their own name and looks, and some dress like the original. To the gang it seems as if the vigilante can't be killed.

The job right now

Get through the gang's turf, room by room, to the last room and the people in charge. Once they're gone, the town is free, the vigilante retires, and that story is over for good.

Controls and interface

The fights are floaty on purpose. As in Floppy Heroes, fighters drift through each room as if gravity were turned way down, so you steer your ragdoll with the mouse and every fight is about momentum, angles and which part of you arrives first.

The camera sits between you and the pointer, inside the room

Every hit pops with a little burst of knockback and recoil for both sides, a moment of slow motion, and floating damage numbers for each, so you can see exactly what a collision cost. Your character's face turns to look where you aim, and winces when hit.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    One room, one fight

    An enclosed room of the gang's turf, seen from the side: an office floor, a back kitchen, a parking garage, a penthouse. You drift in, read who's waiting and what they're wearing, and look for an opening.

    You chip away at their armor, knock the weapon out of their hand or take the hand itself, and keep your own head and chest out of reach. Items lie around the room or turn up mid-fight, and some rooms have traps of their own: grinders, saw blades, spikes on sensors, walls that punch out.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    A takedown

    A takedown is one gang in one town: a gauntlet of 5 to 15 rooms from the front door to the leadership, fought in order. Opponents arrive partly armed (a quarter, half or three-quarters of a full kit) or fully equipped, and the rooms escalate in what they throw at you:

    RoomWho's in itWhat changes
    1 vs 1One thugA straight duel
    Half-sizeA small, quick oneHalf the health, armor and damage, same speed
    1 vs 2, 1 vs 3A groupUse them against each other
    GiantA brute twice your sizeDouble health and armor, same damage and speed
    Mega giant, titanFour or eight times your sizeHealth and armor scale up, damage and speed don't

    If the vigilante dies, the successor starts again from the first room. The thugs who were taken down stay dead, but a gang has recruits, and the rooms fill up again.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    Every town that needed someone

    Across the world there are endless towns and endless gangs, and endless ordinary people taking them on. The game offers three cases at a time. You take one, set others aside for later, or leave one to the locals to handle on their own. You also pick how intense the first attempt will be: who's waiting in the rooms, and what you start with. Each successor can keep that setting or change it.

    When a gang falls, the game stops for a moment: a memorial for everyone who died taking it down, with every vigilante you played listed by name, and a moment of silence for the town's own victims. Then the gang's name is stamped destroyed over the town, free again.

    Each takedown goes into an archive styled as a newspaper, with a snapshot of the last fight, to browse and remember. A case you left to the locals isn't in it, because you weren't there.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A room, an evening, a town

    A room takes a minute or a few. A takedown, with a successor or two, fills an evening or so. The game saves between rooms, so you can stop after any fight and come back to the same vigilante in the same doorway.

    Fed up with a case? You can drop it at any time and leave it to the locals. You just won't be in that town's story.

Game modes

There's one mode: the takedowns, an endless run of towns, each a story with a beginning and an end. It nests like this:

The archiveEvery town you've freed, as long as you care to keep going.
A takedownOne town, one gang, one alias: 5 to 15 rooms to the top.
A vigilante's attemptFrom the front door until they win or fall.
A roomOne fight, a minute or a few.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Soft death: the mask passes on

    Reloading a save makes a death not count: the game becomes a prophecy you're bound to fulfil, and nothing you do is really yours. Permadeath swings the other way and punishes every risk until you stop experimenting. Mafia Takedown takes a third path.

    The character really dies, and the death stays in the story. Then someone who knew them takes up the alias, with their own name, face and kit, and starts again from the first room. The takedown only fails if you walk away from it.

    It turns the most likely event in a hard game, dying, into the thing that gives each takedown its meaning. By the end, a gang might have fallen to a vigilante's grandmother, avenging her grandson and his cousin before her.

  2. Armor first, then the body

    Every part of the body is its own fight

    Each body part (head, upper and lower torso, upper and lower arms and legs, hands and feet) can wear its own piece of armor, and has its own health. A part can only be hurt once its armor has shattered. Every piece of armor has its own stats: health, weight, thorn damage (what it does to whoever hits it), knockback and recoil.

    What hits what decides who gets hurt:

    ContactResult
    Weapon on bodyDamages their armor or the bare part, and wears your weapon a little
    Weapon on weaponNeither body is hurt; both weapons lose durability
    Your feet on their bodyDamages them, not your feet
    Feet on feetZero damage, but the usual push on both
    Feet on a weaponYour feet take the weapon's damage
    Body on bodyBoth sides take equal damage, unless armor's thorns tip it

    Damage per contact is fixed, with no bonus for speed, so a careful poke is as good as a wild swing. About one piece in twenty spawns with a colored trait that bends its numbers: red for attack, green for defense, blue for speed, brown for a tortoise-heavy plate, white for home-run knockback, black for lethal hits at a cost in weight.

  3. Whatever's lying around

    Weapons, shields, and the kitchen sink

    Before a takedown, you choose your vigilante's look, alias colors, starting armor and two one-handed items or one two-handed one. Inside, you'll lose most of it, and scavenge from what the rooms and the fallen leave behind: melee weapons, shields, guns, hybrids of all three, armor, food and repair kits.

    Pick-up grabs whatever's nearest your chest, so in a pile you may have to grab, turn and toss a few things to get the one you want. A two-handed weapon in a single remaining hand flails around awkwardly. Guns fire slow projectiles with limited ammunition and break quickly if you club with them. There are no explosions: every hit in the game is one object touching another.

    Anything thrown becomes a weapon for a moment, glowing green until it lands.

  4. Losing a hand changes the plan

    Limbs that come off

    Every part except the head and upper torso can be lost. A part whose health runs out breaks away and floats off, spraying blood for a moment; hit it hard enough and it bursts apart. Lose a hand and you lose what it held. Lose both legs and you lose your best kicking weapons. Lose the head or the upper torso and the fight is over.

    The fastest way to win is the head, but the rest of the body, its armor and its weapon keep getting in the way. A lost limb can be grown back by eating: grab food and use it, or, with no hands left, nuzzle into it with your head. Food only regrows lost parts, never heals damaged ones, so each hit keeps its weight. Rare kits fully heal or repair.

  5. One against a horde, fairly

    A dumb, predictable gang

    It's one person against a whole organization, so the gang is deliberately simple. A thug with a melee weapon flies at you, turning lazily to face you, bounces off after a hit, backs away for a second or three, sometimes waits, then comes again. A gunman fires the moment he's lined up, with no aiming ahead.

    They're dumb and predictable, but chaotic, because momentum and spin make even a simple plan go sideways. That's where the player's edge comes from, along with a slow pace that leaves time to think. No friendly fire for anyone, but allies still collide with you, so you can't hide inside a friend.

  6. Chaos you can read

    Clear signals in a messy fight

    A fight scatters limbs, armor and broken weapons everywhere, so the game outlines everything by state. White means alive and active. Red means inert wreckage: it can be pushed around but hurts no one. Green means a thrown object in flight, still able to hurt. Team markers show who's on which side.

    Shots crack the walls where they land, and the camera keeps every fighter in view, panning and zooming as needed, so a trap is always seen before you drift into it.

A note taped to the gang's front door

Mafia Takedown: Dismemberment Ragdoll Vigilante

You've had this town long enough. The corner shop, the bus depot, the people who stopped talking. I'm coming in through this door, and I'm not stopping until I reach the top floor.

Maybe I won't make it. Then someone else will come through this door wearing this mask. My brother. My neighbor. One of yours, even. They'll know the way, and they'll know your names.

You can stop a vigilante. You can't stop the mask.
