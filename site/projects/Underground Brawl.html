Underground BrawlFloppy Ragdoll Fight Night

Reactorcore's Personal Game Design Project

Free to forkNo licence and no copyright claim: build this game under your own name, or lift one idea from it. Why?

Game overview

After dark, in basements, parks, parking lots and empty warehouses all over the city, people meet to let off some steam the old-fashioned way. Underground Brawl is their fight night, played with floppy ragdoll fighters who bump, poke, tackle and shove each other through a gauntlet of bouts until only one is still in one piece.

It's the original form of Reactorcore's Floppy Heroes remix, a ragdoll physics combat game: chunky 2D paper-doll fighters who float freely through the air of the venue, each of their body parts wearing its own scrap of armor, fighting by collision. Where a weapon or a boot lands decides everything. It's slow, deliberate and tactical, and very silly to watch.

The film Fight Club gave it the setting. Like the floating, the limbs that pop off are never explained: nobody bleeds, and the club's medic tapes everyone back together after the bout.

Target audience

Fans of physics brawlers like Gang Beasts, Toribash and the original Floppy Heroes, who want a fight that's about positioning and timing rather than combos.

They want to outthink an opponent with their body, and most fighting games ask them to memorize inputs instead.

Mouse-driven: point to move, a few keys for items and a surge.

Game's purpose

Release: letting off steam with a good, harmless scrap.

Mastery: learning which part of you to put where, and when.

Silliness: floating fighters, popping limbs, sandwiches thrown in from the crowd.

It avoids draining safety and fairness: every fighter chose to be there, nobody is really hurt, and the opponents are all computer-controlled, never another player you have to make lose.

Core concept

Fighting games usually measure who knows the moves. Brawlers usually measure who mashes fastest.

Here a fight is physics and armor: strip the opponent's pieces off one collision at a time, protect your own, and use the recoil of every hit to set up the next.

And the city never runs out of clubs, each one a generated night with a champion at the end.

Lore and story

The clubs

Every club meets somewhere it shouldn't: a boiler room under an apartment block, a parking garage's lowest level, a park after the lights go off, a warehouse with the forklifts pushed aside. Each has its regulars, its crowd, its own way of doing things, and its champion.

The fighters

Office workers, mechanics, couriers, students: people who need to let something out. Everyone in the ring chose to be there. They tape their hands, strap on whatever padding they could find, and float out into the middle of the room, because in these clubs, for reasons nobody discusses, fighters float.

You

A fighter with a name or an alias, your own colours and face, and the gear you walked in with. You're working your way around the city's clubs, one night at a time.

Tonight

One club, one gauntlet of bouts, and its champion waiting at the end of the night.

Controls and interface

The fight is seen from the side, and your fighter is steered with the mouse, drifting through the air toward wherever you point.

Body

Move toward the pointer
Mouse 2
Turn toward the pointer
Hold W
Surge harder toward the pointer (also Shift or Space)
Tap W
A quick burst, on a cooldown

Hands

E / M4
Use the held item: tap for its main use, hold for the other
Q / M5
Throw what you're holding, or pick up what's nearest
Wheel
Zoom in or out, in slow motion while zooming

Let go of everything and the fighter brakes in mid-air. The camera follows a point between your fighter and the pointer, as in Soldat, so you can look ahead without losing yourself. Live fighters and items have a white outline; anything knocked loose and inert, a limb, a broken plank, a shed pad, turns red and is just debris to push around.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    One bout

    Drift in, turn so your pipe or your boot meets their chest, take the recoil, drift back, look for the next opening. Knock a pad off, then the arm under it, then the hand holding their bat. Grab whatever the crowd throws in. The bout ends when someone's head or upper body gives out.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    A night at one club

    A night is a gauntlet of 5 to 15 bouts back to back, against opponents who grow in number and size, ending with the club's champion. You start with the loadout you chose and fight on with whatever you still have: armor that broke stays broken, and lost gear gets replaced by scavenging.

    Get knocked out and the night is over. The club remembers: next time you come back, patched up and with a fresh loadout, you pick up the gauntlet at the bout you lost. The fighters you already beat stay beaten.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    The city's clubs, and the flyer wall

    There's always another club. Each evening you're offered three, each generated with its venue, crowd and champion: take one tonight, set the others aside for later, or let them go. And for each, choose its intensity: who you'll face and what handicaps you start with. You can change it every time you go back.

    Every night you fight goes up on your flyer wall: a flyer for each club, with the night's bouts, the fighters who beat you along the way, and a snapshot of the final knockout once its champion falls. It's a record of your fights, never a ranking.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A bout or a night

    A bout takes a few minutes; a full night anywhere from twenty minutes to an hour or so, depending on its length. The game saves between bouts, so you can stop at any break, and the club, your gear and your bruises wait for you.

Game modes

There's one mode: the circuit of clubs, one night at a time.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Fighting by collision

    There are no attack buttons for your body. You hit someone by moving a part of you into a part of them, and what hits what decides who gets hurt:

    What touches whatWhat happens
    Your weapon on their bodyThey take the weapon's damage; the weapon wears a little
    Weapon on weaponNobody is hurt; both weapons wear down
    Your foot on their bodyThey take damage, you don't
    Foot on footZero damage either way
    Your foot on their weaponYour foot takes the damage
    Any other body partBoth sides take the same damage

    A weapon always does the same damage, however fast it's moving, so a careful poke is as good as a wild swing. Every collision kicks both sides apart, knockback for them and recoil for you, with a moment of slow motion and damage numbers hanging in the air, so every fight bounces around the room and the recoil is something to plan with.

  2. Scrap armor

    A piece of armor on every body part

    Head, upper and lower torso, each arm, leg, hand and foot can wear its own piece: a motorcycle helmet, hockey pads, a taped-on phone book, shin guards, a trash-can lid strapped to a forearm. A body part can't be hurt until its armor has been knocked off, and every piece has its own hit points, weight, thorn damage (what it does to whoever hits it), knockback and recoil. So armor is a weapon too: a spiky shoulder pad turns a tackle into an attack.

    About one piece in twenty comes with a coloured tint that bends its numbers: red hits harder, green lasts longer, white sends people flying, black is heavy and lethal, brown turns you into a turtle. A fight is gradually stripping someone's pieces off one collision at a time, while protecting yours.

  3. Grab what's near

    Weapons, shields and the pile

    Hands hold two one-handed things or one two-handed thing: lead pipes, planks, bats and chains, bin lids for shields, and the odd slow, silly ranged thing like a slingshot. Picking up always grabs whatever is closest to your chest, so in a pile you grab, turn, throw the junk away and grab again until you have what you want. Thrown things hurt whoever they hit.

    A two-handed bat held in your one remaining hand flails around wildly. Wear things out, or take off the hand holding them, and the other fighter is disarmed, literally.

  4. Nobody bleeds

    Limbs that pop off, food that grows them back

    Every body part except the head and upper torso can come off. Damage an exposed arm enough and it pops off and floats away, inert. Lose the head or the upper torso and you're out of the bout. The fastest way to win is the head, and it's never easy, because the rest of their body, armor and weapons keep getting in the way.

    During a bout, food is the only way back: pick up a sandwich and eat it to fill a bar, and a full bar regrows a lost limb. No hands left? Nuzzle into the food with your face. Rare treats also heal damaged limbs, and repair kits patch armor and weapons. After the bout, the medic tapes everyone back together.

  5. The gauntlet

    Opponents in every size

    Fighters are scalable ragdolls, so a night's bouts mix numbers and sizes. A typical night climbs like this:

    1. 1 v 1A fair fight
    2. Half-sizeSame speed, half everything else
    3. 1 v 2, 1 v 3Watch your back
    4. GiantTwice the size, twice the armor
    5. Huge, titanFour and eight times: the champion

    Big fighters have more hit points and armor but hit no harder and move no faster, so a titan is a slow wall of padding to dismantle. Opponents arrive anywhere from a quarter equipped to fully equipped. Their thinking is dumb, predictable and chaotic: turn lazily toward you, charge, bounce off, back away for a second or three, sometimes just hover and wait. It's one of you against many, and that's what keeps it fair.

  6. The room joins in

    The crowd and the venue

    The crowd doesn't just watch. Every so often someone throws something in, like the power-up drops in Liero or Worms: a pipe, a pad, a bin lid, a sandwich, a repair kit. A good throw can turn a lost bout around, for either side.

    The venues have their own hazards too: a boiler that vents steam, a loose forklift in the warehouse, a car that someone starts at the wrong moment. Shots that hit the walls leave cracks that fade over time, and the club's mess builds up through the night.

Underground Brawl: Floppy Ragdoll Fight Night

Fight night. Tonight. You know where. Bring tape, bring padding, bring whatever you can hold in one hand or two. The crowd brings sandwiches.

Bouts until somebody's head comes off. Champion at midnight. The medic stays till close and nobody leaves in more pieces than they came in.

Tape up. Float in. Knock their pads off.
