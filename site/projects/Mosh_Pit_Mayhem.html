Mosh Pit MayhemHeavy Metal Brawler

Reactorcore's Personal Game Design Project

Game overview

Mosh Pit Mayhem is a human demolition derby in front of a heavy metal stage. You're one of 4 to 12 metal fans in the pit, and for the length of each song you all build up speed and slam into each other, over and over, until limbs fly off and only the toughest are still standing.

It's played with the mouse alone: point to turn, click to run, right-click to dash. The trick is momentum. Whoever hits harder and from the better angle barely feels it; whoever gets hit takes the damage. So you build up speed, and at the last moment you twist so your elbows and boots arrive first while your head stays out of it.

It's comical by design: nobody dies, and between songs the paramedics sew everyone back together, good as new. It's the most minimal of the spinoffs of Reactorcore's Floppy Heroes remix, a ragdoll gladiator game: no weapons, no armor, just bodies and speed.

Target audience

Anyone who's felt the joy of a mosh pit, or wanted to: metal fans, physics-brawler fans (Gang Beasts, Toribash), and people who like a game they can pick up with one hand.

They want loud, silly, harmless chaos, and most fighting games ask them to learn combos first.

One mouse, three inputs, and a song's length per round.

Game's purpose

Play and silliness: bodies bouncing off bodies to loud music, and limbs flying for laughs.

Movement and release: the pure physical rush of building up speed and crashing into something.

Mastery: learning the angles, the wall boosts and the timing of the twist.

It avoids draining safety: everyone is a willing participant, nobody is really hurt, and the medics fix everyone between songs.

Core concept

Brawlers usually add more moves, weapons and meters until the fun gets buried in the controls.

This one strips it down to speed, angle and contact. Damage comes from who was moving faster and which part of them hit.

And the pit remembers every song: the stains stay, so each concert leaves a painting nobody planned.

Lore and story

The pit

In front of every metal stage there's a small patch of floor where the fans who came to move instead of watch do their thing: run, slam, bounce off each other and come back for more. Everyone in it chose to be there. Here it's taken to comical extremes, and that's the whole joke.

The fans come in every look the genre has: denim vests, leather, corpse paint, kilts, band shirts, spikes. Outfits change only how a fan looks, never how they fight.

The band

A band on stage plays a ten-song set, and the songs are the clock: a round in the pit lasts as long as a song. At the end of the night, the band joins the crowd in celebrating whoever made the biggest mess of it.

The medics

Behind the barrier, a team of heroically unbothered paramedics waits with needles and thread. Anyone knocked out is carried off, every lost arm and leg is stitched back on between songs, and they all head back into the pit.

The night ahead

Ten songs. Stay on your feet, hit hard, hit smart, and be the one still standing when the last chord rings out.

Controls and interface

The pit is seen from above, and your fan always faces the mouse pointer. There are no other keys to learn.

Each fan's body parts show how much punishment they've taken, so you can see who's one hit from losing an arm, and who's one hit from going out.

Game layers

  1. 01 · Moment-to-moment

    One song in the pit

    4 to 12 fans, one pit, free for all. You pick a target, build up speed across the floor or kick off the barrier, and twist into the hit. Then you bounce, recover, and look for the next one, while someone else is lining up on you.

    The round ends when the song does, three or four minutes, or sooner if only one fan is still standing. A fan whose head or torso runs out of health is knocked out and carried off until the next song.

  2. 02 · Low metagame

    A concert of ten songs

    A concert is a set of ten songs. Between songs, every fan is healed and stitched back together, and the loose limbs are cleared away. The stains aren't: they dry dark and stay, so the floor builds up over the night.

    The night keeps a running tally of what each fan did in the pit, and after the last song the top fan is crowned lord of the moshpit, celebrated by the whole crowd and saluted by the band, in front of the painting the night left on the floor.

  3. 03 · High metagame

    The tour and its floors

    There's always another gig. Concert after concert, each with a different band, venue and crowd, and a different number of fans in the pit.

    Every concert's finished floor is kept as a poster in a gallery of nights: a picture of everything that happened, painted by everyone in the pit, which you can look back on or save as an image. It's a record of the night, never a prize for it.

  4. 04 · Practical game flow

    A song or a whole set

    A song takes three or four minutes, a concert around forty. The gap between songs is a natural place to stop: the concert waits with its stains and tally intact.

    Knocked out early? You can watch the rest of the song from the barrier, or skip straight to the next one.

Game modes

There's one mode: the tour, gig after gig, each concert a complete night with its own ending.

Features and mechanics

  1. The heart of it

    Momentum decides who gets hurt

    Every collision has a winner and a loser. The fan with more speed and the better angle takes only a little damage; the fan on the receiving end takes a lot. So the whole game is about arriving faster than the other person, from a direction they're not ready for.

    Then there's which part of you lands the hit. Damage is multiplied by the body part it hits, both ways:

    Part that's hitDamage takenSo
    Head× 1.5Keep yours out of it
    Torso× 1.0Aim for theirs
    Arms and legs× 0.5Hit with these

    The best charge builds up speed, then twists at the last moment so an arm or a leg makes contact with the other fan's head or chest: maximum damage out, minimum damage back.

  2. Use the barrier

    Dashes and wall boosts

    Right-click does two things. A tap is a quick dash with a short cooldown, about 1.25 times your speed. Holding charges a bigger dash: the longer you hold, the harder you launch on release, from 1.2 up to 2.5 times your speed.

    Do it next to a wall or the barrier and you kick off it. The quick dash doubles to 2.0, and the charged one ranges from 1.5 all the way to 4.0 times. The edges of the pit, where the cautious fans hang back, are the most dangerous place to be.

  3. Nobody dies

    666 hit points, and limbs that come off

    Every fan has 666 hit points, spread across their limbs, torso and head. Take enough on an arm or a leg and it comes off, spurting comically, and goes skidding across the floor in its sleeve or boot. A fan can keep going on a torso and a head alone.

    There are no weapons, no armor and no healing during a song: what you lose stays lost until the music stops. If the head or the torso runs out, you're out for the rest of the song. Then the medics sew it all back on.

  4. Thrown in from the crowd

    Relics

    Once a minute, a relic lands somewhere random in the pit. Anyone who touches it gets its power for 30 seconds, and relics stack, even two of the same kind:

    RelicFor 30 seconds
    StrengthYour hits do double damage
    DefenseYou take only a quarter of the damage
    HasteYou speed up half again as fast
    BoostEvery dash and wall boost launches twice as hard
    KnockbackFans you hit fly twice as far
    TranslocationA hard enough hit teleports you to a random spot instead (light hits still land)

    Because anyone can grab one, a relic landing in the middle of the pit turns the whole floor into a race for it.

  5. A record of the night

    The floor becomes a painting

    Every fight leaves marks: red splatters where hits landed, dirt smears where fans pushed off to accelerate, scattered limbs in their clothes. The longer and wilder a song, the more vividly the pit gets painted.

    When the next song starts, the limbs are gone, but the old stains dry dark and stay, with fresh red on top. After ten songs, the floor is a unique painting of that night, and it becomes the backdrop when the lord of the moshpit is celebrated.

  6. Who made the biggest mess

    The night's tally

    Through the concert, each fan's deeds are tallied, so the end of the night has a story to tell:

    DeedTally
    Every 10 points of heavy damage dealt+1
    The hit that knocks a fan out+5
    Last one standing in a song+5
    Still standing at the end of a song that took out more than half the pit+2 for every survivor

    The other fans in the pit are all computer-controlled, so the tally is a way of telling the night's story, not a ladder against other players. The crowning is a celebration, and the band always joins in.

Admit one, pit access

Mosh Pit Mayhem: Heavy Metal Brawler

Ten songs. One pit. No weapons, no armor, no rules except physics. Doors open at eight, the barrier opens at the first chord, and the medical tent is staffed all night.

Bring nothing but your elbows. Leave with everything you came with, more or less, sewn back on in roughly the right places.

Lose your arms. Keep your head. See you in the pit.
