# TopVerse: metagame, structure and purpose

What TopVerse is for, how a session flows from the region map into play and back, the persistent world and its principles, the player's characters and faction, start/mid/endgame, resources, wildlife, growth and business. Sources: `TopVerse.txt` (the overview), `1 TopverseNotesBU2_Cleaned.txt` (principles, combo game flow, character manager, growth potential, variability, todo list), scans TN7, TN24, B2, B6, B7. Scan codes are explained in [index.md](index.md).

## Purpose

"A super game: an endlessly playable persistent universe that you can jump into and have it create a game experience of your choosing, each session creating an impact on the world, altering it permanently in some meaningful way" (overview). It answers two problems: single-player campaigns that are predictable and finite, ending just as you've learned to play; and games as separate silos that can't share systems, so a game on foot shows vehicle sections as cutscenes or a line of text. Content is data-driven, modular and reusable in any context, so content multiplies instead of adding up: "even minimal game content [can] offer more gameplay hours than the time that went into creating them."

The inspiration is modding **Cortex Command**: data-driven content that shows up in any scenario, physics, and a buy menu with costs and rules that wasn't a cheat. What it lacked was a metagame where sessions aren't reset to zero: "a perpetual universe simulator of sorts."

In the universe, the player's purpose is "to grow, become prosperous and meet their real life needs (action/excitement, creativity, relaxation, silly fun, discovery of new, venting frustrations into cathartic outlets, socializing and sex)" (Notes). Actions matter, nothing is reset, and the game runs forever as a place to enter and enjoy. "Regardless if I want chill cozy or big action, the game will provide specifically that, based on what I engage."

## Two modes (Notes)

- **Quick play** (arcade run): any game mode as a self-contained, throwaway mini campaign, with no bearing on the persistent world; like playing a subgame as a standalone title.
- **Persistent world**: the full universe simulator, with mysteries, places, hazards, calamities, discoveries and opportunities.

## The loop

**Combo game flow** (Notes, B2 "Multi Combo Mega Game"):

1. Start in the **region view** and make a decision (auto-save here).
2. Play it out on foot or in a vehicle: room networks, room gauntlets, vehicle arenas, on-foot arenas (and derby arenas), regional vehicle tunnels, high-atmo/space acceleration runways, mid-flight high-atmo/space/hyperspace interdiction tunnels.
3. Return to the region view when the scenario is exited, whatever the outcome (cancel, tactical retreat leaving it half-finished, full success, full failure).
4. Local changes apply to the tile, then regional changes, then continental and higher.
5. The universe runs its global effects from top to bottom; regional, continental and celestial forces (natural, factions, the player's race, structures, objects) act on their protocols, with some chance for variation or chaos.
6. Repeat.

From the region view you choose or switch characters, play solo or with a gathered squad, or play a random civilian or military character, and choose what kind of play to have: combat, leisure, city building, exploration, strategy/tactics, terraforming, colonising, thriving (B2). Around it: global events, persistence, consequences, decisions, combined multi-role games, Pokemon as wildlife and kaiju, the wilderness, customisable avatars, fleets and facades. The whole game "plays like an *8089: The Next Action RPG*-style RPG, focusing on the player and their direct action, keeping the region tile view as a subsystem for requests, info, city building, intel, planning and special actions, not as a primary game layer" (Notes todo). B12 takes the area grid toward 4X-style strategy, with vehicle battles in generated zones.

The pitch to Stefan (Notes) describes the overworld as an Advance Wars-like turn-based grid showing a planet, star system, continent or galaxy; at a location, depending on context, you start a tactical combat sequence, a city-builder sequence, a firefighter rescue or a garden/farm/factory session, and every sequence permanently affects the map. "Now if you look again at the list of smaller games above, you might notice that they're effectively pieces of a larger puzzle."

Main menu (B2): options, exit, info/credits, Ad Circus, a random topic from the wiki, feedback/website/news/blog/social media.

**Ad Circus** (explained by Reactorcore after the digest): an experimental ethical, non-intrusive monetisation idea. A dedicated main-menu page shows a small grid of 4–8 adverts piped from Google Ads or a similar service, refreshed every few hours. Nobody is shown ads unless they open the page: "ads let me discover new things, but I only wanna view it when I wanna view it", so you visit to see what's on offer today, maybe find something cool, or laugh at bad ads, hence "circus". Shared reference: `reference/ad-circus.md`.

## Principles of the persistent world (Notes)

- **Equilibrium universe.** The universe settles into a dormant, lazy state. Dangerous areas exist, but they never come to the player first: "Nothing majorly bad ever happens or becomes a threat if the player does not provoke it or poke at it." Only neutral or positive events arrive from outside, seldom and slowly. The player must venture into volatile areas for new benefits, at the risk of triggering a **calamity**: a crisis over a region that will cause a permanent negative or neutral change unless the player stops, defuses, clears or conquers it. "If I go bother a bees nest in an unexplored zone, it may create a calamity that will threaten the cozy side for that area if not dealt with." One calamity can trigger another, but none can end the universe; at worst you relocate and regroup. Once the core settlement is established it usually stays safe, and the risk moves to frontier outposts and colonies.
- **Live in the moment: minimise persistent instances.** Most of the world is unloaded and doesn't exist until interacted with, which usually creates a temporary generated zone or route; it deletes itself when left, and a revisit generates a new one. The exception is **persistent settlement hubs**, under a strict density quota per region, continent and celestial. Around a hub, everything else stays unloaded and random: a city is a cluster of tiles (residential, industrial, educational, logistical, military districts), each existing only as a tile on the area map "kind of like Advance Wars structures". Hubs are mainly player-customised, persistent districts with a **nexus** showing the city's status, resources, locations, services and connectivity, and an access terminal for switching to the characters of role stations (police, fire, rescue), so the player doesn't have to travel there to take a role mission.
- **Persistence tiers.** A map tile location is (A) a randomised instance, (B) a temporary DRC/MDRC staging/parking area, or (C) a permanent parking room with player-set, decorated DRC plots, allowed only at a limited density; more, and the chaos system steps in. Player-owned DRCs/MDRCs are persistent within universe-wide limits; non-owned ones keep their health and condition but may randomise inside when not in use. A player DRC/MDRC can be **truly persistent** (personal HQ, flagship, a mobile base with stashed items, furniture and décor kept exactly as left) or **semi-persistent** (location, type, facade, armament and inside elements mostly remembered but reshuffled). The truly persistent quota is universe-wide, tied to a maximum save size; above it, the player must demote one to semi-persistent.
- **The chaos system enforces performance**, through hidden and overt measures that activate in a critical emergency (see [universe.md](universe.md)).

## Characters

**First version (Notes): nine slots.** The player plays as a group of characters, one at a time, and can switch at any time like GTA 5, or join up with their own characters as a pair or group. Nine because it is the largest party on one map: one in the middle, eight around it.

- **Slots 1–3, resurrecting permanent**: slot 1 is the player's self-insert ("Optimus Prime, player themselves"), who always comes back after death with the same name, look and person; the gift can be extended to slots 2–3 for a loved one or partner ("BFF of the player. True family").
- **Slots 4–6, saved mortals**: characters the player liked, kept across reboots; they come from a population pool the player is responsible for, and saved people are put at the bottom of the casualty list in disasters.
- **Slots 7–9, a rotating cast of random mortals**: truly random people with random equipment and roles, for experiencing life as a stranger; re-randomised on reboot, after a while, or on demand. If the player loses all their people, slots 4–9 have no one to fill them.
- Death is an inconvenient setback: a dead character is unavailable for a long in-game time (an hour, a day, a week, a month) and drops special artifacts or heirlooms to retrieve. If all characters are "in the cooler", time skips and whatever calamity was under way succeeds.

**Later version (Notes, "character manager").** The player ideally never resets their universe. To get past boredom with a character, they customise the avatar and press **Rebrand**, which makes it feel like a new character. A repository of **character presets** (name, colours, body/head shapes, voice; no separate inventory) lets them switch identity. **Partners** (formerly slots 2–6) are a team of customised NPC avatars fielded as teammates in some scenarios, "the council of immortals"; the right number needs testing. **Randoms** (formerly slots 7–9) are rotating settlers with predetermined names and looks and permadeath.

Variability (Notes): transforming, being corrupted or becoming another species, body types, heads, race attributes, transhumanism, hives, goo girls, monster girls, maybe playing as a Pokemon or kaiju; creeds, motives, traditions, cultures and goals; playing as villain, antihero, menace, wild Pokemon, hivemind, AI or robot, nomad, solo outlaw, a party RPG, assimilator, converter, cult, devourer, an unkillable eldritch god, a ghost. Roles change at a changing station with its supply system; vehicles can be entered by anyone, though some need a key, suit, diploma or skill (TN7). Remaining design topics from B6/B7: character tracking; char switching, followers?; friendly AI reacting to player carnage (a karma system?).

## Start, middle and end (Notes, "Topverse Combogame Flow")

**Start.** You begin as a villager in a local region, with most of the world shrouded in fog and mystery and some hints and warnings from locals. The intro either zooms from planet to area tile into an on-foot tutorial, or starts on foot and shows the region afterwards. You reveal the fog by exploring, later with towers, satellites, probes and scouts that reveal it ahead of you (active opaque and passive transparent fog of war). You choose between developing the starter village first or heading out and acquiring as you go. Meeting an enslaving despot army unprepared can wipe your civilisation or subjugate your people, leading to guerrilla resistance, abandonment, joining another village to return stronger, or rising through the despot's ranks to overthrow them. You have a home faction and can become a member of other benevolent factions, gaining their tools, people and systems with respect and loyalty. The player is immortal, but their faction can be annihilated: unless already rising in another friendly faction, the next death forces a reset in a new starter village after a time skip, with ruins of the old one left for others to find. Recovering is cheap early and costly later, but a bigger empire is more stable and needs a bigger disaster: a nuke on an area tile, a colony drop on a region, a moon collision on a continent, a nova, Death Star or invasion on a celestial, a supernova or hyperweapon on a star system, AI war, brood fleet or chaos eruption on a star sector, a galactic nucleus detonation on a galaxy, a big bang at a supercluster, a universe god reset (player only). Against non-cataclysm threats like an enemy army, the player can submit, losing resources, people, fleets and structures and accepting restrictions, with penalties for breaking them.

**Middle.** Expand from region to continent to the whole celestial, develop vehicle/MDRC fleets, relax at your settlements, and venture into the unknown on foot or with fleets for loot, tech, information and resources. Events keep arising in your territory, where you can intervene to save what's unprotected or earn gifts, reputation and gratitude. Explore underground and underwater, reach space (satellites, orbital stations, derelicts), then the star system (other celestials, fields, anomalies, megastructures, ruins, aliens, monsters, miracles, exotic materials). Every planet has its own quirks. Giants, stars and black holes are visitable but need special vehicles to dive their layers without instant death. Build star system infrastructure (trade lanes, jump gates, catapults, hypergates, defences) and, with FTL, go anywhere in the galaxy and beyond (see [travel-and-combat-layers.md](travel-and-combat-layers.md)). Travel can trigger hostile armies, outbreaks, traps, hives and calamities, usually with warning signs ahead, or neutral and positive discoveries: friendly factions, resource caches, artifacts, tech, logs, maps.

**End.** Even after "total universal domination" there's plenty to do: some areas always stay wild, contaminated, corrupt or ungoverned because the universe is too big; settlement tasks, gratitude and chill activities never run out; the population always has needs (without facilities, unrest follows: accidents, crime, terrorism, arson, coups, protests); blind spots remain in hard places (bedrock, depths, mantle, cores, stars, giants, deep nebulae); the Wilderness holds oddities and user-made challenges; old areas trigger predictable events from their conditions; and wild Pokemon/kaiju remain a menace that keeps the ecosystem working, mixed nice with nasty so neither can be cleansed. New official content and generator rules refresh the experience. In the end "the game is inherently fun intrinsically, eventually shifting the focus from destination to the moment-to-moment fulfillment and exploration of the already discovered world; to live in it."

## Resources (Notes)

Mostly automatic: a discovered resource is marked as the player's. Near a settlement, the player's people can gather it into the local stockpile after a delay; otherwise it is just catalogued as owned ("like owning a hectare of forest"). The player can also gather and refine it on site with a vehicle and crew and haul it back. Wealth goes into fleets, city building, repairs, megastructures, civil improvements, outposts, installations, facilities, equipment and industry. Resource types are bundled into generic groups: light metals, hard organics (wood), soft biomass, heavy metals, volatile chemicals, inert chemicals, exotic metals.

## Wildlife: the fear/loyalty index (TN24)

A **regional Pokemon fear/loyalty behaviour index**, with a general human attitude and faction-specific overrides. A wild, untamed area's monsters may approach and attack humans like predators. If humans retaliate, fear grows and they avoid humans unless one is obviously weak and alone. If tamed (fed or given something nice, while the player sets boundaries by blocking or dodging their attacks without counterattacking), they become friendly and allied to humans or a faction, and a faction-tamed monster that sees its people attacked comes to heal, defend, attack or harass. The values are region-specific.

## Growth and business (Notes, B2)

**Growth per subgame**: new guns, abilities, tools, devices and items; armours; enemies, NPCs and Pokemon; hazards, obstacles and objects; surfaces, materials and biomes; role-specific hats, hair and torsos; map and network generator parameters; objectives and rules; weather. Plus user-added assets (hats, hair, body textures and shapes), user suggestions, and bought or rewarded user IP as official inclusions. **Universe-wide**: new scrolls, autoscroll rules, and Unigen patterns, layers, encounter types, events, calamities, discoveries and variants. **Other**: the "Ad Circus", new hires to make content and other projects, online co-op/collaborative multiplayer.

**Modding** (Notes): "i dont know." Everything is pre-standardised so that the universe never needs a reset, even with official updates. Cosmetic mods (hairstyles, faces, bodies, vehicle facades) and scrolls are safe; new Pokemon or weapon definitions are tricky for balance.

The physical **Token Fleet Pack** (B2) is for testing scenarios and design prototyping on paper.

## Proposals

Claude's suggestions for decisions Reactorcore left open. Not Reactorcore's design.

- **Proposal, which character model.** Use the later character manager as the structure (self plus Rebrand and presets; Partners as the council of immortals; Randoms with permadeath) and keep from the nine-slot version the rule that ties it to the world: Randoms are drawn from the player's real population pool, so losing people in a calamity visibly shrinks who you can play.
- **Proposal, partner count.** Up to 8 Partners, so self plus partners fills the nine-character formation the first version was built around (one in the middle, eight around).
- **Proposal, karma** (B6 asks "friendly AI reaction to player carnage, karma system?"). Reuse Regime Eradicator's karma rule universe-wide, but per region: collateral damage lowers that region's loyalty (like the Pokemon index), which changes how friendly NPCs, hubs and factions there respond, and recovers slowly in the equilibrium universe.
