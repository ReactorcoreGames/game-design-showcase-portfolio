# TopVerse: origin (Syndemic to TopVerse)

How a feedback document for someone else's microbe shooter turned into a universe design. Sources: the `0 Syndemic - loose inspiration to TopVerse` folder (Reactorcore's Syndemic report and Adjuvant's crowdfunding page), the txts `4 Syndemic Hijack Project Aka Topverse.txt`, `2 Syndemic - enemies_hazards.txt`, `3 Syndemic design - weapons.txt`, `6 Topverse weapon slot system VARIANTS.txt`, and scans TN5, B4 and B8. Scan codes are explained in [index.md](index.md).

## Syndemic

Syndemic was a randomly generated top-down action shooter by a solo developer working as **Adjuvant**: you play an artificial microbe that destroys diseases inside human bodies, using the right antibiotic weapon for the right enemy, with roguelike upgrades and shoot-'em-up reflexes. Adjuvant crowdfunded it as a game to inspire people about real biology. Reactorcore was a beta tester (version 0.43) and wrote a very long analysis and overhaul proposal for it, the "Syndemic Report". Adjuvant eventually gave up on the game. Reactorcore's B8 note "Syndemic-hijack project: make Syndemic my way if Adj. fails" records the moment the plan changed to "fine, I'll do it myself".

The name means two or more interrelated biological factors that make a health crisis worse together. Reactorcore looked the word up while testing and built the report's proposals around making that happen in play: several diseases overlapping in the same rooms.

## What the Syndemic report proposed (and what TopVerse inherited)

The report is unfinished (the author's estimate: 35%), but its core proposals are what TopVerse's framework grew from.

- **The body as a city with districts.** Instead of separate one-way linear missions that end in a contrived boss room, each patient becomes a small procedurally generated **room network** of about 6–20 rooms: the body's organs as districts, blood vessels as **highway rooms** (long rooms with slow flow streams that push everyone, fun to speed through, combat still possible), **toll gate** rooms that orient the highways so you never travel "downward" from the groin to the head, hubs, junctions, optional dead ends with a **symbiont nest** (heal, change loadout) or a data particle, and **healthy rooms** as calm, safe pacing spaces. The network is never stored as coordinates on a map; it only exists as door connections, which still line up logically for the player. This is the room network TopVerse uses for every on-foot zone (see [on-foot.md](on-foot.md)).
- **Main Insertion.** You always enter from outside: an always-safe exterior room, a transit room, then a hub room from which you choose your route. Exits stay available, so you can leave the body at will. An alternative **Crash Landing** entry drops you into a random room with a shockwave, as a one-way, risky option.
- **Doors** are entered with a key press (E), not automatically. Doors stay open by default; a door can be held shut by a guardian enemy or a destructible barricade, never by an arbitrary script. No one-way doors.
- **Room generator.** Each room is divided into a 3x3 grid of anchor zones (8 door positions and a centre), whose lines act as anchors for spawn lines, rectangles, spots and circles. Generation runs in steps: standards (max objects, min/max room size), external input from the network generator and diagnosis screen (room type, context, disease list, danger classes, enemy point budget, patient traits, doors), then heuristics with dice rolls and biases, always keeping a 1–5% chance to randomise freely within the standard frame.
- **Diseases as recipes.** A disease is a formula that defines what to add to the network and how: groups of enemies, roaming "bandits" to chase down, objects to destroy, triggered challenges, network-wide events. Patient variables (age, diet, smoke, drink, genetic trait, context) tune how the generator sprinkles enemies, obstacles, hazards, allies and objects. Separate point pools exist for enemies, beneficials/allies/neutrals and obstacles/terrain; only the enemy pool counts toward objectives (mild 50%, moderate 70%, powerful 85% cure).
- **Standardised tiers.** Enemies and objects come in tiers (tiny 0.4 shots, small 1, medium 2.5, large 5.2, huge 9 shots with the benchmark weapon), so former bosses become Cyberdemon-style "huge" regulars that can appear anywhere. The enemies text file has a later variant: small 0.5, medium 1, large 1.5, huge 2.5, giant 4, mega 6.5, massive 9 shots, with the rules "nothing is indestructible", "no bosses, no sponges, no invulnerability", "empower, tactical, avoid frustration/anxiety". Objects and hazards are destructible too, some only by the right element.
- **Collateral damage as the core tension.** You are a precision cure that must not kill the patient. Beneficial microbes and support structures cost the host HP if you hit them; immune cells are the body's police and firefighters, who turn on you if you misbehave. Enemies should not hunt the sensitive targets themselves; the onus stays on the player to aim well.
- **No time pressure.** Timed gauntlets felt artificial; wave defences start when the player triggers them.
- **Bubbles on the world map.** Patients grouped into themed, finite clusters (a pub, a hospital, a school) instead of an endless void, with a clear start, middle and end to a playthrough, consequences that carry forward (a Dishonored-style chaos outcome), and a quick arcade version of a single bubble.
- **Content as a growth engine.** With modular, standardised content, every new enemy, object or room rule multiplies the possible situations instead of adding one more scripted moment, so the game could keep a studio going on a steady trickle of content. The report sketches four development phases (imitation prototype, bare bones, road to ultimate form, infinite growth).

## The weapon slot idea

Syndemic's arsenal became TopVerse's standard: **10 slots**, one per job. In Syndemic they are one weapon per enemy type (microbial, fungal, viral, cancer, grease, particles, gases, support), plus an **aux** slot (mover tool, booster, tactical mobility; "think gravity gun") and an **exo** slot (a special omniweapon that kills all types at the cost of damaging the host; emergency use). The weapons text adds a list of weapon properties to mix (penetrative, spread 0–360°, shot count, range, cluster, nuke, area denial, beam/projectile/ray, homing, elemental status, arcing, bouncing, spawning allies, curving, precise "only hits correct targets", two-stage, timed, attract/repulse/pin, reverse AI, hijack AI), and a lab ritual: before a run you test and freely reroll your arsenal like a scientist cultivating a new strain, or a symbiont nest rerolls it between checkpoints while you test it in a dreamlike simulation space. An effectiveness scale was planned (worst = instant boom, bad = heals the enemy, neutral, good, best = quad damage).

`6 Topverse weapon slot system VARIANTS.txt` generalises the slots for every subgame: one weapon active at a time (Syndemic), all selected weapons firing together on primary/alt fire or an alpha strike (vehicles and starships), paired slots 12/34/56/78/90 (mecha), several weapons per slot cycled Doom/Half-Life style, slot contents swapped via an inventory (Starbound/Minecraft), or MMORPG-style press-to-fire abilities, in any mix a game needs. On top sits an optional consumable item list (Doom-style, one key to use, one or two to cycle).

## The hijack: from one game to many

`4 Syndemic Hijack Project Aka Topverse.txt` is the turning point. It starts with "Syndemic Hijack Alternatives": Symbiont (Syndemic itself, rebuilt with Asset Forge, primitives and stock illustrations, with simple biology sources standing in for a biologist colleague), then **Regime Eradicator**, the first reframing of the same systems for a different theme (see [subgames.md](subgames.md)). Its analysis is the general lesson: the Syndemic room system, briefings, data logs, debriefs and collateral karma translate to a top-down human-scale world almost one to one, while the elemental weapon slots become "use the correct weapon for the correct target" (light calibre, heavy calibre, siege/explosive, flame, electric/EMP, chem/toxic, super weapon, affector wild card, plus first aid for civilians and a move/translate tool to manage targets before attacking). Group after group of other games follows in the same file (civil service, alien wars, mecha, X-COM, Pokemon TCG, Hyperspace God Mecha), each noted as "combinable" with its neighbours.

Reactorcore then noticed that the shared foundation could carry something much bigger, and the microbe theme stopped being the point. The B8 note's name ideas for the hijacked Syndemic (The Miracle Cure, Supercharged Cure, Omnicure, Disease Eradicator, Final/Ultimate Cure, Power Cure, Crosscontamination, "We are the Cure", or any goofy health-shop name) are what is left of that standalone plan. In the final design, Syndemic survives as one small subgame, the "Micro BIO" scale below the human-sized room on B4's zoom chain.

## Tools named along the way

The hijack plan names GDevelop (top-down only, with shaders and basic physics), Asset Forge (3D kit-bashed models rendered to sprites), an FX particle generator, PSP9 batch filters and fre:ac for audio; B8 adds GameMaker Studio 2, Construct 3 or a simple maker. These are historical; the overview says "any 2D engine".
