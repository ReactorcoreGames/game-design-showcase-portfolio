# TopVerse: the subgames

TopVerse is "30+ games all-in-one as a huge universe sim" (B2, "Multi Combo Mega Game"). Each subgame is a small focused game on the shared top-down framework, playable on its own as a quick run or as one activity inside the persistent universe. Sources: `1 TopverseNotesBU2_Cleaned.txt` (the list, Astral Psychic Fighter, Hyperspace God Mecha), `4 Syndemic Hijack Project Aka Topverse.txt` (most descriptions), scans TN1, TN2, TN6, TN7, B3. Scan codes are explained in [index.md](index.md).

## The shared frame

Every subgame keeps the same core (overview `TopVerse.txt`, slots txt, hijack notes): a top-down twin-stick character (move with WASD or the left stick, aim with the mouse or the right stick), basic fully destructible levels, simple physics objects, an interchangeable player character with up to 10 active item slots and auxiliary ability buttons, a procedural level generator and a campaign generator. All are physics-driven top-down action games except the city builder. Each has roguelite-inspired campaigns of 10–40 minutes that are "not about challenge nor permadeath", so each is "effectively infinitely playable as a 10–40 min campaign dispenser" (Notes, the pitch to Stefan). What changes per subgame is the theme, the character, what the 10 slots hold (weapons for combat games, tools and equipment for the rest), the objectives, and the generator parameters. Some subgames have a fixed loadout, some shuffle; shields and slow-motion safety nets exist in some and not others (hijack NOTE2).

The hijack notes also mark which games are "combinable": Regime Eradicator with Corporate abuse and Crime slayer; the civil service jobs into one **Civil Service Heroes** game (either a squad of specialists or one omni-role character who changes class at a role-specific supply pod, Thunderbirds style, with the player deploying the pods); Robot Wars, Ballistic Weapons and Ghost Busters as one alien/monster war. TN7 turns this into a universe rule: the player can only take another class (police, firefighter, soldier, engineer...) where that class's changing station and supply system is available.

## The list (Notes, "List of planned games")

Reactorcore counts 33 games in seven groups by scale (1 + 20 + 3 + 6 + 1 + 1 + 1), "all combinable into one".

| 1 | microscopic | Syndemic |
| 2 | human size | Regime Eradicator; Corporate/criminal/abuser slayer; Police Enforcer; Fire Fighters; Rescue Team; Off-road Trail Engineer; Parkour Gadgeteer; Jet Set Radical Janitor; Mad Dash Medic; Zoo Pacifier; Robot Wars; Ballistic Weapons; Ghost Busters; X-COM overkill; Super Warrior (Emtag var fighter unit); Astral Psychic Vengeance; Pokemon TCG; Miner vs Beasts (Pokemon TCG supplied); Tacticool Teenage Operator; Civilian Animal Crossing (the default human) |

The pitch version of the list (Notes, to Stefan) phrases some of these more plainly: an overpowered artificial microbe curing diseases without killing the patient; Pokemon TCG as a real-time top-down action game; Battleships Forever-style vehicle combat extended to ground, air, sea, underwater and underground with transitions between them; mecha vs mecha; mecha vs kaiju (huge versions of the Pokemon-like monsters); kaiju vs kaiju with lots of collateral damage; demilitarising authoritarian regimes as a power-armoured trooper; grid-based simplified city building on ground, sea and space; a puzzle-like transport tycoon of highways, starways and FTL links; firefighting where the enemies are flames and the weapons are extinguishers; plus rescue, police, trail engineering and parkour.

## Group 1: Syndemic

The origin, now the smallest scale ("Micro BIO", B4). See [origin.md](origin.md).

## Group 2: human size

**Regime Eradicator** (hijack). A vigilante in a heavily armed mecha-suit strikes deep into an oppressive authoritarian regime, a thinly veiled near-future stand-in with knock-off drones and robots. Weapon slots: light calibre, heavy calibre, siege/explosive, flame, electric/EMP, chem/toxic/acid, super weapon, affector wild card, plus first aid for civilians and a move/translate tool to get bystanders out of the way. IFF matters (enemies, allies such as political prisoners and rebels, neutrals). Structure mirrors the Syndemic overhaul: chapters with a briefing, an assault on a local stronghold, a debrief and a chapter map of locations; three acts; good/mediocre/poor endings driven by **karma points** (bad karma from killed bystanders and destroyed good infrastructure, measured against the level's total entities). Story set pieces (atrocities) would be generic props with a hover-for-info explanation. An alternative framing is an environmental protection agency's secret cadet programme with many mechs, where a dead cadet is replaced by the next. Reactorcore flagged the obvious risk of the real-world political theme and broadened it in "extra thoughts" into an X-COM-like coalition against corruption and abuse worldwide, with an "I can't do this" button for grey moral moments and a clear framing as cathartic fantasy that encourages non-violent change in real life. Working title favourite: **Peaceful Reunification Force**. *(For any page: real countries and leaders are named in the notes; use fictional stand-ins.)*

**Corporate/criminal/abuser slayer** (hijack Group 1b, combinable with Regime Eradicator). "Corporate Abuse Must End" and "Crime does pay in death": power-fantasy catharsis against those who make others suffer on purpose. Title-level only.

**Civil service jobs** (hijack Group 2):

- **Police Enforcer**: most slots become non-lethal subjugation tools (taser, net, tear gas, baton, rubber bullets, cuffs, flashbangs). Killing is penalised according to the victim's influence and the witnesses; a bodycam appears after suspicious disappearances. Paragon or renegade cop, with different challenges and endings.
- **Fire Fighters**: the weapons are extinguishing equipment, and different fires need different agents. Uses an advanced fire/burning system (see TN4 in [on-foot.md](on-foot.md)). "Sort of like Dark Souls, but about places that are burning down": smoke with limited gas mask filters refilled at safe spots, collapsing structures, blasts. Two mission types considered: escape a burning area as a civilian, or conquer the whole fire tactically from outside, with generous milestones rather than a tight timer.
- **Rescue Team**: tools for overcoming obstacles; mostly a puzzle of reaching stranded victims and organising a safe evacuation.
- **Off-road Trail Engineer**: tools that build platforms, bridges, ziplines, ropes and ladders to make new paths for people to enjoy. Cozy.
- **Parkour Gadgeteer** (Courier): mobility tools; linear gauntlet levels (chasms, river flows, whirlpools, cliffs, ice, forests, cities) with no time pressure, only an optional seeded timer. A courier or Mirror's Edge-style runner.
- **Jet Set Radical Janitor** ("Janitor Radically Extreme"): Viscera Cleanup Detail but satisfying, maybe on rollerskates or a skateboard, with the option to clean up or to cover the place in graffiti (Sludge Life).
- **Mad Dash Medic**: humble close-range healing tools; comedic, as the medic leaps through fire and explosions to stabilise or carry people to evacuation, knocking out hostiles with one whack on the way.
- **Zoo Pacifier** (zoo containment failure): capture loose, dangerous, mostly large animals without anyone getting hurt.

**Alien and monster wars** (hijack Group 3, combinable): **Robot Wars** (robots with different armours need different weapons), **Ballistic Weapons** (brutal alien invaders with shields against certain attacks, fought as a "spectrum warrior" with every elemental type), **Ghost Busters** (a supernatural removal team using elemental weapons to defeat, capture or nullify ghosts and monsters).

**X-COM overkill** (hijack Group 5, "Extraterrestrial Commander"). A half-parody where a united Earth throws absurd force at alien invaders: one heavily armoured operative at a time, armed with the best explosives, blowing up UFOs so thoroughly that the chief scientist is furious there's nothing left to research; most tech comes from unopened alien supply strongboxes. Five acts: scout fleet, battlefleet, mothership fleet, alien outer worlds, alien homeworld, then home to pretend aliens don't exist while keeping the tech. Alternative: a tense tactical shooter with named, permadead troops (portrait, name and age; a death log per campaign), one on the field at a time, with at least two entry points per map against spawn kills.

**Super Warrior** (TN6). The "Emtag var fighter unit", armed with the **EMTAG** multi-weapon (electromagnetic thermo atomic graviton, variable). Primary modes: pierce, nuke, spray/bombard, beam/sword (Akira-style sweep), on two primary rifles mounted on secondary arms with auto or manual aim and fire. Secondary: cold photon projectors on wrists, feet, heels and elbows (shields and blades), a suit-wide 360° auto reflector beam (attack and defence), a shoulder-mounted indirect homing/curving plasma-EM beam launcher. Tertiary: a back-mounted auto-turret EM-railgun pellet shooter, pull/push gravity tethers and grab hooks, a light projector suite (radial suit lights, helmet night/thermal vision), and a dozen throwable detonation devices (sticky, rolling, homing, remote, proximity, timed, impact). Super Warrior 2 and 3 scale the same idea to Type-2 power armour and Type-4 mecha.

**Astral Psychic Vengeance** (Notes, "Astral Psychic Fighter"). Inspired by Focus (freeware), Thy Blood Konsumed and Akira: a cornered, tormented, awakened person with fully unlocked psychic powers that are potentially limitless in strength but tremendously short-ranged (about 3 m), so the player must be creative to close the distance. Powers: dash teleport (12 m, raycast-checked), telekinetic crush pulse, time slow (full pause to look, or 0.1% slow motion), grab-rip-launch, hyper barrier sphere, deflector walls (Absolute Terror Field-style panels), aura field (keeps gas, insects, rain and radiation out, holds air in vacuum, gives light or darkness), 360° repulsor blast, single-target healing, alchemy, a pull/push beam, psy vision (souls through walls, thermal, wireframe "dev vision", "logic vision" of stats and states), mind crush, levitation and flight, boost jump, threat precognition; from Thy Blood Konsumed the gesture attacks (blade, hammer, lance, claw matrix, arm blades with mini spears), short-range elemental powers (pyro, frost, thunder, radiation within 2 m, stronger on contact), chaos system "evoke" functions (merge, delete, relocate, freeze entities within 20 m), a black hole compactor, summoned decoys and golems, a vortex, a fear field, a spear-buckshot perforator, a defensive spike porcupine and a phoenix self-destruct (healing or fusion) that reforms the astral after a while. Balance comes from physical needs (food, sleep, water), willpower and confidence, the **synchronisation percentage** with the astral plane, health and gear; gestures with one or both arms or the whole body strengthen the powers. Astrals are empathetic and wild in body language; there is no mind control, only consensual read-only connection between astrals. Weaknesses: ambushes, snipers, flashbangs, being overwhelmed before entering battle mode, and their own minds. **Awakening tiers** go past 100% only with large external support (vehicles, structures, megastructures, group effort): Super (>100%, 21 m on foot, 1.2 km in a vehicle), Hyper (>1000%, one adjacent area tile), Mega (>10,000%, 120 km regional), Tera (>100,000%, planetary), Ultimate (>1,000,000%, stellar), God (9,999,999%, universe). "So no, I can't casually become Vegeta and blow up a planet with a random finger laser."

**Pokemon TCG / Evolver Devourer** (hijack Group 6, B3). Real-time top-down combat with odd elemental monsters in sizes S/M/L (sprites 16/32/64 in the hijack notes; B3's sprite generator gives S 14, M 30, L 62 px, with XL 126 dropped as too slow), plus a MEGA kaiju size that rivals Type-3 mecha. B3 names it **Evolver Devourer**: Pokemon TCG rules as a real-time action game, wind-up attacks, morphing between 3–6 swappable characters, two or more elements per monster, and a flying Domokun-like fairy as the "trainer" and item carrier. Four framings (a Pokemon party with elemental weaknesses and five swappables against a rival team; solo PUBG on an open map; "eat, evolve, reach Eden, avoid being eaten"; wild gauntlet → target territory → assert dominance) are concluded to be "all the same". The B3 monster pipeline: generate a top-down sprite, find a matching full-body portrait, define type, abilities, class, stats, biome, attacks (official TCG cards as balancing templates; get the cards as a folder of images for reference), give a name from a generator or dictionary. "Read the rest in its own document" (hijack): that document isn't in the project folder; `misc relevant/Pokemon TCG types.pdf` is the type reference. In the universe these monsters are the wildlife (see [metagame.md](metagame.md), fear/loyalty).

**Miner vs Beasts / Demolition expert** (TN2; "Pokemon TCG supplied", so its beasts are the TCG monsters). Ten tool families: weld/pin/connect/drive spike; saw/cut/slice/trim; smash/pound/shockwave; drill/dig/shred/dismantle/rip; burn/incinerate; force push/shove; tether pull/grab; remote detonation/explosives; chem/acid/weaken/dissolve; water hose/foam spray (push, wet, extinguish, obscure line of sight, coating).

**Tacticool Teenage Operator** (TN1, "TTO"). Kids on harmless "operations" in a cozy local urban or mall setting: knife/saw/crowbar/window hammer, an omnitool, foam shooters (dart, ball, rocket), airsoft (spring, AEG, grenades), a fire maker, a water gun/sprayer (with a shaky skill-based aiming reticle), fireworks and smoke bombs, spy and comms gear (walkie, listening dish, mirror, loudspeaker, smartphone), foldable vehicle boots (ski, ice, skate, snow, fins), a stick or spear; all gear is bagged in water. Activities: put out minor fires, remove obstacles, repel pests (up to cat/dog size; fox/eagle noted), break in, prank, steal, help, rescue, find, goof around. Rules: no human enemies or beasts, no death, no serious crisis. Context: missions, weather, environments, "don't get hurt, don't get caught", resupply.

**Civilian Animal Crossing** (TN7, Notes). The default "off-duty" mode every character reverts to. Three roles: **Decorator** (paint, move/add/delete furniture, colours, tints, decals, text, symbols, signs), **Communicator** (an interaction system with inhabitants, NPC or online; the note says "MMS-style", a reference Reactorcore no longer remembers the reason for), **Facilitator** (find, claim, distribute, convert, dispose, send resources; crafting, manufacturing, construction; "sort of Factorio-ish"). Characters can enter and pilot vehicles at any time, some needing a key, suit, diploma or skill.

## Group 3: minor vehicle size

**Crash Car Derby** (B3): cars with melee attacks; modes dominate (moshpit), survive (free for all), target destroy (static/mobile), cause havoc (scene). Maybe an advanced damage model and building collapse ("truck dismount scoring?").

**Tunnel travellers**: convoy defence and interception in transit tunnels. Title only; the tunnel system it would use is in [travel-and-combat-layers.md](travel-and-combat-layers.md).

**Super Warrior 2**: the Emtag fighter as Type-2 power armour. Title only.

## Group 4: mega vehicles and MDRCs

**Planetstorm** (B3, B12). Starsector-style ship combat but momentum-driven, with starships, groundships, water and submarine ships and airships, bases and environments with weather; Battleships Forever-style ship switching and MechWarrior-style weapon slot customisation. Movement modes per medium (B12): space/sky/orbit has omnidirectional thrust and free momentum; ground/underground has no sideways movement and turning needs forward or backward motion; sea uses the space model with high friction, drift, waves and wind; underwater uses the sea model without wind (light underwater has heavy weather effects; deep underwater is dense and calm, with rare quake waves). "Vehicle propulsion setup matters! Every vehicle different." The name comes from *Angels Fall First: Planetstorm* (AFF), an indie game where players walk inside space warships, man stations, repair systems, pilot the ship as a huge vehicle, launch and dock fighters, and fight FPS battles in the corridors against dropship boarders; that walkable-interior warship is what the DRC/MDRC model brings to TopVerse.

**Eradication Wars** (hijack Group 4, M v M). Two sides past any reconciliation in a solar system colonised without FTL, mixed together everywhere but living in hostile bubbles. Same tech on both sides, different paint (EFSF vs Zeon, but symmetric). Robots and drones are rare because they can be hacked; Type-3 mecha (10–15 m humanoids) are centre stage. Missions: weapons-free enemy territory, hostages in a combat zone, and flushing invaders from an allied base with minimal collateral. (Reactorcore has a separate AAL Eradication Wars design; this is the TopVerse slot for it.)

**Kaiju games** (hijack). Kaiju Defence: the Eradication Wars mecha against premade kaiju while protecting civilians, "Pacific Rim, but not stupid". Kaiju Attack: play a kaiju dismantling mecha and cities, Rampage-style. Cyber Kaiju Attack: a kaiju fighting alongside mecha against evil counterparts. Kaiju attacks are stamina/energy abilities from the creature itself (spikes, mouth beams, sonic roars, eye lasers, acid, claws), combined with the Pokemon TCG system.

**Super Warrior 3**: the Emtag fighter as a Type-4 mecha. Title only.

## Group 5: Hyperspace God Mecha

(Notes, hijack Group 7.) High-speed continuous mecha flight: two or more mecha joust in a scrolling corridor with controlled acceleration and some lateral movement, moved into hyperspace so nothing on the map can be collided with, which requires an FTL drive. Only horizontal or vertical tunnels (diagonals are uncomfortable on a square screen), so diagonal trips take two tunnels. A distance bar on the HUD shows thresholds and other entities (jammable). Eligible: MDRCs with FTL drives and **god machines**, giant MDRC-sized mecha ("a Buster Machine", the Death Star of TopVerse) that wield superweapons able to destroy celestials, balanced by cost, fuel and gargantuan wind-up for the strongest weapons. Groups and convoys can open a tunnel together; enemies can enter it anywhere; nobody collides in the tunnel; cheap tricks like flak pebbles must not instakill a god machine (high-HP targets vs many small enemies, limited momentum damage). When idle on the map, god machines attack in Super Robot Wars-style grid patterns (one cell, a row, rows, a radius, a cone, the surrounding cells, random cells, single or multi hit). The tunnel idea generalises to every kind of transit (see [travel-and-combat-layers.md](travel-and-combat-layers.md)).

## Group 6: City builder / World terraformer

(B2.) Also "Station builder / starzone layout", for surface, air, space, underwater, underground and star-system megastructures. Works like SimCity with premade, pre-balanced blocks: a **placement grid** (4 DRCs max, one per corner quadrant, with terrain obstacles), **prefab exteriors** (the entrances of DRCs/MDRCs) and **internal variants** (room layouts with doors, windows, supports and walls). Build houses, stations, plants, factories and parks; manage smart layouts, supply, sewage, environmental friendliness and signage.

## Group 7: Transport Network Tycoon

(B3, "Transport Network (Game?)", flavour name Sunshine Heavy Industries.) Networks per scale: city/area (metro, shuttle, boat), regional (rail, bus, helicopter), continental (hyperrail, jet, freight carrier), stellar/orbital (trade lane, docking ring), interstellar/FTL (jump gate). Build sensible routes and stations at minimal cost with maximum maintenance access, meeting requirements. Puzzle-like, per the pitch.

## Proposals

Claude's suggestions for subgames Reactorcore titled but never described. They are not Reactorcore's design.

- **Proposal, Tunnel Travellers.** A convoy game in the transit tunnel: the player escorts or intercepts a convoy of up to four vehicles crossing a region's tunnel, using the tunnel's side exits, chase reticles and maneuvering box. Defence wins by getting every vehicle to the end tile; interception wins by forcing a drop-out, which turns the encounter into a normal vehicle arena at that tile.
- **Proposal, Super Warrior 2 and 3.** The same EMTAG loadout and three slot tiers, scaled: at Type-2 power armour the secondary and tertiary systems become vehicle modules and the suit can be packed into a flagship like any minor vehicle; at Type-4 the unit is an MDRC whose interior is boarded on foot like any other, so the Super Warrior line spans all three scales with one weapon identity.
- **Proposal, Corporate/criminal slayer.** Fold it into Regime Eradicator's coalition framing as its "lesser evils" chapters (the hijack notes already order targets from worst to lesser), using fictional companies and gangs, with the same karma and "I can't do this" button.
