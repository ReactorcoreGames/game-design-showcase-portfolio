## S21 — RS behaviour: gatherers and defenders (`IMG_20191005_145911.jpg`)

Grid paper, blue pen, spiral holes on left edge, portrait, prose. Continues the RS section.

Most RS units are independent and very uniformal in terms of chassis types and manufacturing standards.

They use fairly straight-forward and simple tactics and become aggressive only if attacked and this hostility will be maintained within the entire star-system as the RS communicate constantly to maximize efficiency. This does not mean that will know where you are at all times, but they will remember you and your actions against them. Upon detection they will attack immidiately and attempt to hunt you down as long as they detect you.

RS forces are divided between "gatherers" and "defenders". Gatherers are geared toward resourse gathering/harvesting and have more appropriate designs for their task. Defenders are pure combat units and their task is to protect the gatherers. They will attack anyone that is an identified enemy of the RS.

## S29 — Metagame: Beginning (`IMG_20191005_150235.jpg`)

Grid paper, blue pen, portrait, prose then bullet list. Heading underlined.

**Metagame: Beginning**

Every campaign is generated individually and at random. While a player might be shown a picture of the galaxy he/she is in, the is no location that is shown or what areas of the galaxy are cleared or not. Even stats are not shown openly. ^(not on the front page any way)^

Both the player character (which the player may create any number of instances/profiles ^(1 char/profile at a time)^) and the universe are persistant, and both also generate statistics.

A campaign game might be anything. There are many variables that may decide radically what kind of a metagame scenario will the player find himself in. Some might be relatively easy, while others far more serious.

- Size of area & # of celestial objects (Minimum 1 object is must.)
- Amount of stars (0-1-2-3—9)
- Is the entire area within a cloud, field or similar?
- Enemy amount and group amount (dispersal)
- Enemy progress (Already occupied, In Progress or Yet to Arrive)
- Chance of events/disasters.

## S30 — Campaign start, flow and death (`IMG_20191005_150342.jpg`)

Grid paper, blue pen, portrait, prose.

When a campaign is started, the player is presented with the generated metagame map that represents local space (star-system, planetary system or starless space). At this point, the player will see the situation he will need to deal with and what he can expect. There will also be a short briefing that gives some additional details about the area, the foes and what is going on and any additional warnings. This situation report can be requested by the player at any point during the metagame screen.

After that, the player chooses where to enter the zone at any edge location of the map.

From there, the game progresses in semi-realtime, similar to starfox 2.

The campaign will progress from there on with the player trying to defeat the enemy, while the enemy trying to achieve their own plans. ~~The outcome~~ If the player dies, no progress is lost, instead, the dead character is gone forever and the enemy gets plenty of bonus time to move about until the next character arrives at the scene.

## S31 — Campaign end conditions and saving (`IMG_20191005_150354.jpg`)

Grid paper, blue pen, portrait, prose. Heading underlined.

**The campaign will continue until:**

- All threats are removed (or retreated)
- The entire place explodes (disasters and/or superweapons)
- User uninstalls the game (joke)

The campaign may end good or bad, but no matter what happens, the campaign will be over and the player will get a short debriefing and some related statistics on what happened and how. There is no active traditional save system and the player cannot reset or restart the campaign he just played. The only option is to play the next, new campaign.

This system, while strict and constant, will actually make entire experience serious and meaningful as the lack of an <u>active</u> save-system prevents the player from going back in time and undoing ~~[?]~~ mistakes, which forces players to learn to move on and live with their concequenses.

While there is no active save system that allows players to "save/load" a game, there is an automatic save system that will save any progress automatically. This system extends all the way to the actual gameplay

## S33 — Saving (cont.): backup saves; "everything explodes" (`IMG_20191005_150413.jpg`)

Grid paper, blue pen, portrait, prose. Continues the last sentence of S31.

so the player can pause the game at any moment, but also to prevent him from undoing any mistakes.

This system keeps 2 additional backup saves that will only be used if the primary save fails for some reason. The first BU save is made between battles on the metagame, while the second BU is made between campaigns. These saves are invisible and are ONLY to be used in an emergency. There will be a way to force these saves, but absolutely not through the game itself. A third party config or such. This information only appears in the troubleshooting section of the technical manual.

Also to add on the end condition of a campaign "everything explodes" means that the entire area becomes lethal to stay in. It does not mean that all planets, fields or clouds are gone. If all of those are destroyed but the enemy is still present, then it will simply be an all out battle in empty space. What it does mean if a supernova happens or a massive matter disintegrating cloud lands upon the place, rendering the local space lethal for anything and anywhere for an extended time.

## S35 — Quick Game (`IMG_20191005_150518.jpg`)

Grid paper, blue pen, portrait crop showing the top part of a page, prose. Heading underlined.

**Quick Game**

This is another, non-persistent and non-serious gamemode. It allows ^the^ player to randomly generate an action scenario or build one himself (with saveable ^OR NOT^ prefabs).

After a scenario has been made, a quick briefing recounts the situation and what to expect, after which the game begins and ends when the player or enemies die.

These do not generate statistics, as they are not part of the persistent world. The quick game is more of an mock battle/skirmish thing just for fun. Dying in a quick game will not erase a player character.

You could say its a Virtual Reality simulation or maybe a spinoff fight that is not taken seriously.

## Glossary additions

- **RU / RUs**: resource units, harvested by RS gatherers and listed on the Tactics Screen ("Available RUs and product stock piles").
- **Gatherers / Defenders**: the two RS force types: harvesters that fight only under direct attack, and pure combat escorts that attack any identified RS enemy.
- **Threat identification**: RS mechanic; units that retaliate are marked as threats, shared across the whole star system (not beyond it).
- **Homeworld**: the RTS whose ships inspired the RS look.
- **AW style / FL style / SWiBF2 style / SF64 style / SPAz style**: reference metagame map layouts (S26); likely Advance Wars, Freelancer, Star Wars Battlefront II, Star Fox 64, Space Pirates and Zombies.
- **Starfox 2**: reference for the campaign's semi-realtime progression.
- **BU save**: backup save; two invisible ones (between battles on the metagame, and between campaigns), forced only via a third-party config.
- **"Everything explodes"**: campaign end condition: local space becomes lethal (supernova, matter-disintegrating cloud).
- **Edge buffer / wrap edges / retreat boundaries**: Tactics map edges: wrap on planets, retreat boundary in space.
- **Q to MM / Q to PC**: pause menu buttons, quit to main menu / quit program.
- **PLAY SECTION**: the part of the main menu the pause menu returns to.
- **Training**: replaced "Tutorial" as the mode name; Intro (slide show), Action (challenges 2.1–2.3), What's next (slide show).
- **Quick Game**: non-persistent sandbox/skirmish mode, "a Virtual Reality simulation"; no statistics, dying does not erase the character.
- **Randomization lock / OLD/NEW flag / Hide**: Quick Game setup controls per category (world, enemies, player).
- **CHAR**: the character/profile button on menu screens; pulses yellow and greys out the lessons when no character exists.
