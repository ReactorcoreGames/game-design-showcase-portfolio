+AAL Eradication Wars: Apocalyptic Mecha Warfare

Summary 
Type-3 Mecha + arms forts, soldat like gameplay, token driven respawn battles in varying battlefields.

Lore:
early space expansion, no FTL, only sublight and jumpgates, exactly like Freelancer. Sirius sector sized overworld of star systems clustered under "world names" - bretonia, liberty, kusari, borderworlds, industrial worlds, omega, Tau, sigma, omicron, etc.
Bigotry, hate and anger has boiled over to a point where 2 factions/nations have decided to fully kill off each other in a brutal conflict where no expense is spared, nothing is held back, no collateral damage is cared for, no mercy and a given and there is no turning back until one side has ceased to exist - eradicated forever.

Metagame:
finite campaign on overworld, renameable factions, anyone can win but final devastation is brutal. All factions/nations are equal in strength/numbers/resources, use same tech. Each faction/nation has its population count in billions of people total, each battle will deplete the population of either sides depending on where the battlefield is. Each Mecha/arms fort pilot/crew kill also counts towards that number.  Overworld map is divided into regions - whoever succeeds in depleting the enemy forces in the area will be rewarded with a one-sided map where they can use Mecha/AFs to blow up large unarmed civilian megashelters, annihilate enemy civilian populations without retaliation - effectively eradicating them to extinction. A murder fest - hence the name of the game. Player can participate, but doesn't have to - npc allies will pepper the area with firepower while going for resupplies occasionally.

Combat:
Static maps with hardened props (rocks, structures) and soft props (trees, houses) scattered on levels. Player spawns offmap, can use a replaceable/disableable mobile repair base for resupply and rearmament. Player chooses loadout before/during spawn and goes in, trying to kill as many opponents before dying self. Player has a primary, secondary, a grenade, an addon slot for temporary powerup + also fists for last resort disarm and attack. Player can drop and pickup gear at will, looting friends and enemy alike for weapons. Ammo supplies are finite for each weapon, but reserves are massive (DxM/AC style) and resupply is possible via spawn point supplies or looting (spawn camping is possible, but mitigated with multiple spawn zones). Player mobility is good and varied, but has limitations that can be exploited by player or enemies to defeat an otherwise fast opponent. Battlefield also gets random powerups and supplies occasionally thrown in via artillery insertion from both sides.

Arms Forts (AF) are giant NPC piloted mobile weapons platforms available and used by both sides, player can't control them but they spawn in and slowly advance towards the enemy base where a spawn zone forcefield may be, making spawn camping harder or there are linear catapults or other beneficial systems that allow an enemy mech to get around faster or become empowered on the battlefield - like stationary guns. AFs hunt down these structures and destroy them, while defending allied counterparts. AFs carry ammo supplies, so T3Ms can get ammo, healing and occasional random goodies by quickly docking at the AF or picking up the kits it drops off randomly when a friendly unit is near and presses E.

Combat is often dependent on map. Each map is custom made and determines team sizes, spawn zones, terrain, props and battlefield supply style/intensity. Weather events and map hazards are possible too.

Most combat is done via 4v4 to 16v16 team battles. Small narrow maps are the 4v4 variety while the biggest open spaces maps are of the 16v16 variety. Tokens usually number around a hundred-ish for small maps and up to a thousand for epic battles. There are also separate tokens for AFs too.

There's also an idea for a DM - Despair Match, essentially a deathmatch where whoever kills a set amount before anyone else, wins. It's named like that because lorewise is a bunch of pilots that have veered out from the official battlezones and fight meaningless battles of insanity due to trauma of war. It may be that the pilots are dead and they're fighting in the afterlife as troubled restless ghosts that are both immortal yet manifest as themselves as repeatdly killable warrior that a stuck in an infinite loop of combat and death. Metawise it's like a deranged fight club campaign to see who can reach afterlife in peace by becoming the master of combat for that campaign. Essentially do a set of 3-12 DM maps back to back, floppy heroes style and if you win them all, you attain nirvana and ascend to heaven peacefully. Each campaign gives a random name, random color scheme, but loadouts are always player choice just like regular soldat DMs.

Weapons wise there are two slots, but player cannot directly select another primary weapon during spawn. They can however discard their secondary to grab another primary class weapon into their second slot - as in soldat.

Weapons come in two classes, primary and secondary. Primaries are often full power weapon, meanwhile secondaries are specialized, distinctly lightweight or unconventional. They're not "lesser" per se, but for continued direct combat primaries are often way better.
Grenades come in many types, but they are not selectable - player find them on the field ala Lost Planet Extreme Condition. Players do start with 2-3 grenades though. Max grenade capacity depends on grenade type.

Differing slightly from soldat, weapons may have additional parameters or features to make slightly more diverse weapons, inspired by Cryptark. Things like weapon weight that interferes with movement speed or prevents the use of a specific move or demands a specific move to use. Also things like weapon rotation slowness while firing similar to Cryptark or idle rotation slowness similar from Bunkerships.

There is also no maximum cap for weapons that can exist in the game. However, players will be presented with a list of randomized 10 primaries and 5 secondaries to choose from. Each list is different per battlefield, but it remains same for that battlefield, for both sides(?). Player can still occasionally find "rare exotics" aka weapons from outside list that are delivered as powerups.

Lorewise weapons are typical Gundam/armored core/MechWarrior style rifles, bazookas, launchers, cannons off all kinds, all varieties - each a highly effective weapon that has pros and cons. Energy and kinetic weapons are both in, including elemental weapons like fire, acid, frost, electric, gravity and others. Weapons can even be absurdly powerful - as in straight up superweapons, like nuke launchers, ion cannons, microwave megacannons, super particle beams and more. Things that keep everything balanced is ammunition counts, weight, delays, rotation slowness, recoil, bink, reload time, slow projectile speed, charge up requirement, mobility locks and weapon autodestruct after x uses. That said, most standard weapons are snappy, have abundant reserves that'll last multiple tens of reloads easily (machinegun with 50rnd mags, has 1000 ammo reserve) and have a relatively fast kill time.

Respawns:
Players will likely die often because each mecha on the battlefield is equal to the player. There are no other differences between player and npc besides skill and tactics. Both have same access to same weapons, both can swap and loot guns, both can have grenades and healing. Player will merely have the advantage of being a human being with faster reflexes that are not capped for the AI. Player is slightly empowered in this sense to make them effective, mainly because it's fun and player stamina is finite, where as AI never goes tired and theoretically could be coded to be flawless at aim and mobility, which would be awful.

Regardless, every death has a 3-5 second delay, after which player respawns at an allied spawn zones, having a chance to switch weapons before dismissing the menu and regaining control.

Essentially each death has a cost; lose current kill streak (encouragement/celebration mechanic for excellent play), lose a token for your team, be reset back to base, needing to haul your ass back to the front line again, lose your current equipment and bonuses (two primaries, special grenades, powerups). It also will sting your pride and incur a personal death count counter by +1.

Death can be used beneficially too, but it's rare. Namely, there's a large enemy group is at base, you need to be there now and youre already quite hurt and far away. Doing a selfkill and respawning in 3 secs can be faster to get there to deal with it. You'll forfeit a token and take a hit to your pride as a price.

Friendly Fire:
By default, player cannot harm their own people, cannot harm their own allies or inflict damage upon allied shelters. This is mainly to prevent player hesitation during combat as an arbitrary gameplay design decision, it's not realistic. However there is an exception to this commonality; first, weapon fire can and will still push allies, people and loose props. This can be used to boost allies or to juggle loose civilian people out of harms way. Second, some weapons deliberately ignore friendly fire and will kill/damage friendlies too if hit. These are often powerup based and not part of the main arsenal lists, but they are obscenely powerful superweapons, that often have an area-of-effect type of quality to them. I'm a bit in the fence about this idea and consider ditching it.
Besides friendly fire, self damage does exist at a 50% reduced amount. Player can in theory blow themselves up, toss a lethal blade and run into it, shoot bullets into the air and have them rain down back on themselves or otherwise get fragged by their own projectiles/explosions. Some weapons offer knockback, like explosives that can be used to propel yourself forward, upward or wherever and they use this mechanic as a balancing aspect. Team boosting is free though because its a support move, so no ally will feel any damage, but will fly true.

Just to highlight - there is no fall damage in this game. Mecha can drop from any height and slam into walls no problem. Only weapons, hazards and self-destruct can kill them.

Mobility and movement:
Unlike soldat, where movement is very stilted, friction heavy and manual, I want movement to be more momentum driven, sliding/skiing, continuous, flowing. Soldat had great systems for advanced movement maneuvers like superman, cannonball, rolling, backflips, various jumps, momentum building movement and such, but it had this auto brake where upon letting go of keys would have the player stop like a rock. This made even forward movement a chore as I had to constantly hop and push every inch just to keep moving while advanced maneuvers like momentum buildup jumps were tough to pull off - it would tiring to move after a while and moving in the game is something that a player has to do all the time.

Meanwhile there are also cool ideas in Apex Legends/Titanfall/Sonic/Doom like wallrunning, sliding, grabbing surfaces to cling to them, double jumps, wall climbing, wall jumps, ledge grabs n pulls, monkey bar style ceiling climbing, rope swinging, level booster pads/trampolines, etc. These are also large mecha with rocket booster functionality and specializes feet/other thing that allow them to do things a human can't. T3Ms are also large heavy battle machines that can hurl themselves around with momentum that takes a bit counterboost to dissipate that momentum/speed. Impacts against surfaces also cause shockwaves that can cause destruction to surrounding props.

Potential ideas for movement...
- doom like floaty movement, acceleration based.
- transform into a flyer/waverider/caveship instead of prone
- floor slide instead of roll. Slide is continuous.
- either separate brake button or a threshold where auto breaking kicks in. If you're already speedy in one direction, then countermove to break, if you're slow enough and not moving anymore, start friction braking.
- jump is omnidirectional dash boost that uses mouse aim for direction, works by holding down just like jump to charge up the dash - not instantaneous like in Armored Core.

Controls
A/D - move accelerate left, right. Sideways move with wave rider left right. Wave rider is like a top down control style. Weapon can only fire in narrow cone front of wave rider. Gun can thus turn faster to face target than wave rider itself.
S - Crouch crab if slow or standing still, floor/crab slide if moving with A/D on floor. If initiated in air while moving, drop kick in direction of momentum as a melee attack. Or crab slide crouch. Airbrake as wave rider. Hold to hover in place like a midair turret and resist gravity.
Mouse aim, rotate as waverider, aim gun as mecha, direct boost dash as either.
W - vertical upwards jump. Press again for double jump. Also use for wall jumps or wall kicks. Unlike soldat where there's a charge up element for a jump, this is more of a Mario jump - hold down for full jump or shorten press for shorter jump that stops sooner. Time the double jump correctly for maximum height/momentum. Can't double jump until surface touch (floor/wall) or 2 seconds have passed.
Spacebar - boost dash. Tap for minimal push, hold to charge up large thrust or ac style overboost. Mouse aimed. Dashing will not collide unless you are dropkicking after launching a dash.
Mouse 1 - fire weapon or do steal gun grab
Mouse 2 - wave rider thrusters. As mecha, hold down for omnidirectional flight using wasd. Boost disabled, flight uses rechargeable fuel. (Reactor capacitor output) in zero gravity, this mode is default albeit weaker but uses no stamina, while mouse 2 is now faster/harder version of acceleration that does use fuel.
F - throw current weapon. Tap just drops, hold will yeet it with damage potential. Flying gun can be deadly if it's heavy and gained enough speed before impact. If holding nothing, shoot a retrieval wire to latch, grab and reel in the weapon closest to cursor - even a thrown one. Catching a thrown weapons will reset it's thrown status, add a percentage of it's momentum to the reeler and reel it into the arms directly. Cannot steal others weapons this way - on loot stuff off floor/floating in space.
R - manual reload, keep ammo topped up.
Q/mouse3 - switch weapons
E - use/abandon near object
G - throw grenade. Tap for drop, hold for throw.
C/Mouse 4 - quick melee attack (punch, kick, combo if unarmed or using one handed pistol, otherwise rifle buttstock smack or bayonet lunge).
Z/Mouse 5 - ability/powerup use
X - switch between wave rider and mecha.

Boost uses own stamina meter.
Thrust flight has own stamina meter too.

Combat is about constant choice between movement and aiming the weapon. The idea is to hurl myself beyond an opponent's gun arc, dodging their fire, see an opportunity and spin around to hit them with a salvo. Each fight is like a dance where you juggle two things, trying to apply them correctly at the correct timing. Boost allows for better map travel via overboost cruise engine, like a mix of armored core 4 FA and Freelancer.

Weapons define play style, where quick lightweight weapons allow for faster target acquisition and maneuvers, meanwhile heavier weapons are more lethal, provide armor and powerful melee but are slow to turn. Equipped weapon augments the mecha on the fly with stats. I think there is no weapon rotate delay here, but there is weapon put in/pull out delays for each weapon to make weapon switching have more weight. Usually weapons with shorter time to kill or excessive AoE and heavy mass are the ones that take slower to pull out. It may even be a start to drop a weapon, pull out a quick one, then put it back in and grab the heavy gun again to skip the delays.

There's positioning, stamina management, simple versatile intuitive controls, knowing your weapon, tactical awareness, ability/powerup use and health tracking to grasp to become a master at combat.

Health regenerates gradually via slow auto repair, but can be boosted instantly with health kits. Repair is slow but continuous and uninterruptible. It's never enough to outpace weapon damage, but using cover, staying unhit and minimizing damage is a great idea to extend survivability. It also forces to keep up the pressure and be aggressive, though not by much. It is *very* slow, not like in Call of Duty. More like 1 HP per 2 seconds, so a full healthbar would take 2*100=200 seconds, or 3.33 minutes. Most kills happen in under 1-5 seconds and most travel time in level is 5-15 seconds on average. You're always better off going to base to heal or find a medkit than covering in a corner.

Powerups:
Sometimes battlefields may receive drops that anyone can pickup, like overshields, stealth cloaks, flamegod, berserk, overboost, deployfortress mode whenever standing still, hazard protection, visual/UI enhancement, immortality, speed, jump, rate of fire, projectile size, gunzerking, miracle mod/heretic soldat server mod abilities (overwatch stuff, league of legends stuff, path of exile stuff, mmo stuff, etc.) All are temporary and eventually run out even if they didn't see any use. They also activate by pressing V or something.

Background battles:
Arms Forts usually come in many types, as seen in armored core, but they can also be typical naval style megavehicles - battleships, destroyers, cruisers, frigates, dreadnoughts, carriers, flagships, submarines, corvettes and such. Anything seen in Homeworld, Gundam shows or such. There's versions of these on land, sea, air, uw, ug, space and amphibious.

The background is actually an active canvas that partly reflects the battle on the game field. At the beginning, two massive fleets with varying ships are firing salvos at each other, there's explosions in middle and zipping lines of projectiles. As tokens diminish in the actual fight, background ship of the corresponding sides begin to explode as a reaction.

Technical aspect wise I would go with a simple solution where there are 10 ships per side that just sit there, firing their weapon, showing a muzzle flash once a while. Tokens and ships are counted as 100%, so once over 10% tokens are lost, 1 ship blows up and so on. In the middle area between the warring fleets there's a rectangle area that spawns random distant explosions (doesn't visually interfere with actual battle) there are also two vertical rectangle emitters slightly forward of each fleet that spawn projectiles that appear like shooting stars, appear out of nowhere, zip by, then vanish.

A slightly advanced solution could have the ships move slightly up and down, simulating avoidance/dodging maneuvers, while each token lost could signify a hit blast - like a cannon/missile/beam/shot hitting a ship - implying a real damage impact. Perhaps each list token causes an actual projectile to spawn from a far and fly directly at a ship and hitting it, instead of missing/vanishing like a shooting star. The order of ship destruction would be randomized so it's not a top to bottom kind of lame thing. Flying projectiles should also travel in a cone shape towards all remaining ships, not just blandly horizontally forward - ships can shoot above and below too, after all.

This background battle also excuses the powerups that land to the actual battle zone. Sometimes battleships may also randomly/accidentally/intently fire/launch a stray cannon shot or missile or weapon crate/pod as a "weather event". Debris, ejection pods, survivors and other elements might occasionally fall into the battlefield too, as hazards, pickups, or new destructible props/peeps.

Map structure:
The general theme is an ongoing epic apocalypse. There is not much low intensity combat in this game overall - there is always a massive duel between two fleets of megavehicles or starships or both. They alone cause great havok that has effects on the actual battlefield. There may also be natural phenomena like weather or natural disasters - some just because or because of the battle. Some props in the actual battle can also trigger disasters/weather too.

The common elements are 2-3 spawn areas per side, having that common low-mid-high pathways, even if there isn't full wall/platforms/terrain that separates them - they can open areas too or only partially separated. Very much inspired by classic soldat map design. The pathways can be also corridors that assume a certain height, which will determine the maximum size of an AF that can travel through it, as well as define what type of AF can be there (water, air, ground, space, uw, ug).

AFs are background movers but they have solid nodes/sections that can hurt with weapons fire, melee or dropkicks. Characters will pass through them, but non piercing bullets won't. Usually they're like those megatanks from Divinoids - turrets, cores, engines are shootable. Similarly they also explode with a powerful shockwave and fireball. In some way similar to Momentum Missile Mayhem enemy units.

AFs would essentially move on rails, back and forth. They're like a set of nodes connected by lines. Some more strictly like ground/sea AFs, while aerial, space, uw, ug can also slightly move up and down, perhaps to dodge or just highlight their floaty movement.
AFs would prioritize knocking out enemy structures by going close enough to them, then taking aim and blasting them apart. If no targets are in range, then the AF will shoot at enemy mecha with any of it's guns. Otherwise it'll only use it's lesser AA style point defense guns while it's attacking a structure with it's bigger cannons.

Once an AF has successfully cleaned up it's entire rail - probably reaching the other end of the map - it will then move back and forth on the rail to hunt mecha, actively checking the farthest horizontal coordinate an enemy has gotten passed it's spawn point and go there, even if the enemy isn't on the same elevation level as the AF itself is.  It'll lay an ambush, basically.

Eventually, the AF may end up spawn camping it's lane, shooting down respawnees as they appear. To make it fair, respawnees still get a bit of a buffer zone before entering it's firing range or atleast find some linesight breaking terrain cover to be suppressed in, but relatively safe. Opponent mecha can still get in there and kill anyone hiding in the terrain cover so enemies can't forever stay in cover eventually have to push in.

Unlike soldat, decor objects are often shootable or aoe affectable props that will be destroyed or changed. There's often nothing permanent on any map except for the terrain itself. Most objects are also live and active, like automatic defence guns, buildings, facilities, systems that mecha can use for mobility purposes or resupply. A map usually begins with a whole bunch of goodies and systems that will destroyed over the course of a match, but also replaced or supplemented with new deployables.

Spawning in as a mecha happens in waverider mode as a greyed out semi-background entity that cannot fire weapons or attack for a period of 2 seconds. As soon as this spawning mecha has exited it's spawn zones it will immediately switch to mecha mode from its initial waverider form, gain movement control, thus allowing for quick invulnerability maneuver before it runs out. Spawnees get thrown into the level from off screen via rectangle or circle spawn zones where the point of entry is randomized within that space. These spawn zones are usually at the side edge of the map, there are 2-3 of them,  usually one per lane and one extra backup to prevent total spawn camping doom. They apply force and propel to the side, from above or from below, depending on context. Diagonal spawnee throws are also fine. Entry speed is also slightly randomized per character spawn, but within a narrow range nevertheless.

V2 idea is that first a mecha enters the scene via an extra external vanguard booster or even a discarding pod/armor film/cocoon/plates - playing a jet fighter flyby sound effects. After 1 second into entry, this external booster/pod discards, revealing the mecha that was dormant within, suddenly activating and coming to life, playing a reactor/turbine/machine startup sound. This happens within half a second where after player regains full control of mecha movement and weapons, but still maintains a 1 extra second of invulnerability as the entry armor film/plates shed off. As soon as invulnerability is over, the mecha will visibly spark/flash and turn from grey to colorful, indicating to everyone that they are now 100% vulnerable to fire. I think this will look, sound, feel and play awesome as a badass spawning sequence that will never get old. As a finishing touch a small HUD/UI indicator red arrow will briefly flash, warning of an incoming mecha and it's entry direction/momentum 2 seconds before it appears.

DM spawns would work the same but the spawn circle/rectangle zones can placed all around instead of just one side. In essence, a mecha arrives via close background so it'll look natural no matter where the actual spawn location is, as long as it's correctly orientated.

AFs would enter via these spawns zones off screen, though they won't enjoy the same invulnerability stuff. They'll be immediately shootable.

Terrain wise there's two main types: solid and background. Solid is just solid terrain, an invulnerable hard surface that will withstand anything. Can be used for  walking, wall jumps, etc. Solid terrain may have material properties like friction value, to simulate slippery ice or thick mud to slow nonsliding movement. Another value is density to alter wall jump potential (too soft or hard enough). Solid can also inflict damage upon mecha that stand on it, either as a permanent capability, temporary when certain conditions are met (weather event or nuclear superheating). Damage can be mild, severe or lethal. Solid may also react to some projectiles and cause explosions/bolts/surges upon point of impact that may deal AoE damage to things in the radius. Beyond that I would actually avoid the more arbitrary polygon types seen in soldat, like those players/bullets/flags only colliders types.

Background terrain has an important role in providing cover against certain weather events and background hazards. As long as a mecha is standing over a background wall, a giant gamma ray burst coming from the background will not fry it, etc. Background walls could also be useful for mounting auto turrets or structures, wall running (though I don't recall having that mechanic in this game) and having signage/graffiti/markings/decals of explosions, bullets and carnage. BG terrain is behind AFs though. BG terrain also excuses "floating islands" solid terrain chunks so they're not magically just hovering in the air for no reason, but have actual supports. The only parameters for BG walls I can think of is how smooth it is so should decals/splashes/burns/dust stick to it or slide off. And the ice slippery thing if wall running was real that would prevent it.

A maps anatomy could be split into clear areas that are common in every battle map even if the specifics differ a bit. There's always a small buffer zone that is out of range for AFs, containing multiple spawn zones slightly spaced apart, then there's the faction owned frontline side area that often splits off as a junction to access all three routes of a map and may have various nation specific defence guns and structures - that an AF will come to shoot up - a lengthy middle area with potential opportunities for transit between lanes scattered about, an open sky, openings in the background walls or if it's an underground map with a cave ceiling then have holes to above from which supplies, hazards and AFs can enter. The rest is mirrored for the opponents team, though that doesn't mean maps are symmetrical - asymmetrical maps are normal too, even vividly so. 

The sheer variety of soldat maps could very well be available in this game, including inf abel, inf warehouse, inf outpost, most ctf maps, many dm maps. Inf fortress and that vertical building one might be unlikely because they are perhaps too unbalanced due to gravity or set up in a regular map. Inf fortress could work as a final stronghold map where the enemy is cornered and compressed against a wall/floor/corner and have to defeat enemy tokens to survive the onslaught and thus have a chance to abandon and escape safely. In these maps the fortress is like a barrier that is the last gate before the population shelters right behind/under it. Sort of like in the movie Matrix 3 - city of Zion and the final bunker shelter tunnels. 

Either way in such inf fortress style levels the defenders spawn zones are within the fortress and around it, while attackers come from both sides and/or above. Attacking AFs will initially not have access to the center core area, but will have to punch through walls - some fortress walls not being regular solid terrain but destructible hardy props that can be removed to create openings for predetermined lines and nodes for AFs to continue pushing forward. In this way, soldats ctf style maps and combat are the first 5 maps while the 6th one is an inf map. It's sort of like combining the assault game mode from UT2k4 with the obstacles that attackers must breach to proceed. Although unlike inf/asslt which used a time limit to determine defender victory, here attacker token depletion is the win condition for the defenders.

Regardless of ctf, inf/asslt maps, they all have the same common basics of thin SafeZone for spawns out of reach for AFs but not mecha, a greater national frontline zone and then an extended no man's land zone. This general structure still seeks to give enough distances and buffer zones so that maps are never too tight or too small or too short or too unbalanced against one nation vs the other, except for inf maps where defenders get a little bit more benefit via advantagous elevation, protective walls/barriers/obstacles and preplaced defense turrets.

The catastrophic weather events, disasters, background war spillover and such happen most commonly in the middle no man's land and slightly less commonly on the national frontline zones of either side. It's a risk reward thing; the crazy stuff in the middle can be lethal but there's also often powerups and goodies that can help assaults against enemy frontlines, be it heavy guns, exotic guns, mecha launchers/boostpads/springs, health kits or abilities. The disaster chance over map could be seen as a gradient; 1% at spawn, rising to 30% at edge of frontline, climbing to 75% in the very middle of a no man's landand then reversing the same way for the opponents side. Like a curve graph. Throughout the map there are also pockets of safety in the form of background terrain walls to be safe from some types of disasters, but not all of them. They could protect against a Streets of Rogue style gamma radiation burst, but they won't stop a falling fuel silo or meteor from falling onto me. Full protection can be attained at small bunkers that protect against most hazards, but they are easy to storm by an enemy mecha as there's not much room to dodge in these small bunker houses.

Besides open space lanes where AFs can roam, there are also mecha-only tunnels/passageways/chutes/rooms where AFs can't reach. There may be a few prop terrain covers that AFs and mecha can destroy with weapons fire if the area is significant and extended enough, but there will still be some small zones where only mecha can stay at. These places are tight areas for mecha vs mecha only combat. They're more common in inf maps as fortress/bunker interiors or in ctf maps as caves/transit tunnels between lanes.

Overall, players will encounter all kinds of combat in open spaces and claustrophobic tunnels that can be horizontal, vertical or diagonal. All levels are built on a large rectangle canvas with a minimum and maximum size a map can be. Combat is paced so that it isn't hectic at all times, but there are moments of calm between each fight/encounter/respawn via travel times or terrain that breaks line of sight. Combat is also always simple - go kill the enemy, there is nothing else that the player needs to think strategy wise - there is no UT2k4 style ONS maps with nodes, power cores or lockers. Just kill and don't get killed. All other elements exist naturally and spice up each moment and are entirely optional for player to ignore. Loadout screen also works like in soldat you can even hide it/turn it off and just always launch with whatever you last had.

AI + screen space:
Game is a scrolling 2D sideview game with verticality with characters that run, jump and fly. View is locked at a specific max zoom level where characters are far, appear small and visibility range is quite wide. Camera follows a point between character and crosshair. Or just follow character, cortex style. In either case, most combat vision and weapon max ranges/projectile lifetimes+friction drag happens within the visible camera space of a character. That said sniper gameplay is an exception as it can have projectiles travel beyond normal visual range and even hit lethally. The only cost is that sniper fire requires hightened accuracy, slower rate of fire and other drawbacks that depend per weapon. NPCs, both allies and enemy can use snipers but without visual warning indicators they would be an awful experience for the player - suddenly a highspeed projectiles hits from off screen without warning. To mitigate this, anyone that uses sniper weapons will draw a cortex style spaces 3-5 dot reticle, indicating direction and guessable distance within the lethal range of the projectile for anyone to see.

Perhaps I should go even further and do a Silver Knights style *caution*, *warning* and *danger* indicators as mecha usually have, but do it for every ally and opponent, showing 3-5 dot reticles by default, or sort of spidy sense thing where there are lines that point towards the edge of screens on anything with a line of sight, like an active radar, showing directions where an enemy signature is so you can home in on it, then encounter the 3 dot reticle - hopefully not pointed at you yet - and decide to go forward or dodge diagonally. Maybe the 3-5 dots (regular or sniper respectively) only appear when AI has aggro'd on you - as an SK warning indicator mention before. Besides enemies, active radar would also display allies regardless of line of sight and suggest how far they are based on marker size. The radar would also show AFs at all times, both friendly and enemy. The game would not have a minimap or other radar screens. Frankly I think the radial always on HUD radar + warning indicator could be cool and techy. Suitable for a mecha game for sure.

In general though, AI works similar to soldat, it's a bunch of bots that use waypoints built into the map to know the general direction of movement towards enemy spawn zones. Encounters with enemy will break off this waypoint movement in favor of combat navigation, tracking the opponent through walls as long as they're within proximity of an opponent distance wise. I.e if you can see them, they see you. AI will ignore sniper reticles in favor of waypoint movement. AI will also be alerted that an enemy got pass them and reached the spawn zones so that they can turn around and go hunt them down. Difficulty:
The game is made to play solo, but admittedly it is originally based of a multiplayer arena gameplay, so the player does indeed need some leverage over the AI to keep up. AI doesn't tire, they never get demotivated, they never good around and they have machine like precision to everything they do - which is wholly unfair towards a player that otherwise expects the same character flaws as they'd encounter in actual pvp against live human players.

Meanwhile everything in the game is standardized, so changing player health, weapon damage, self damage damage received or mobility speed is absolutely forbidden as it will break consistency of the universe and make it difficult to respect if nothing is grounded upon anything. Immersion also dies if those values are touched - super bad idea, makes the game feel fake, meaningless.

So, the AI itself where any difficulty calibration must happen. I want the AI to feel as close to human in terms of flaws. Delays to detect incoming projectiles, panicking decisions that make them run towards a falling projectile, occasionally pausing to take a breath, goofing off at the base for a little bit (rare), fucking up aiming constantly, failing to lead moving targets, forgetting to jump or fly, tossing a grenade onto ground then running into it, blowing self up with a weapon by failing to notice blocking terrain, not knowing when to take cover and retreat, failing to dodge incoming fire, failing to anticipate a dangerous situation yet walking into it anyway, camping, running away in fear after seeing teammate or two die in close proximity.

Granted, most of these are uncommon or rare behaviors that are mixed in with doing stuff right. Any individual NPC is often on the spectrum between noob and pro, where they exhibit more behaviors of either depending which one they are. That said, no bot is perfect and even a pro has a chance to fuck up sometimes. During each battle start, a set of random npcs ai profiles are saved with each team member - each mecha and AF has its callsign, similar to an active scoreboard in a multiplayer game where you can see deaths, kills, name/alias/nickname/callsign and current status. Lorewise each death is real, but gameplay wise there are only X slots for both teams, so they use callsigns, even if the player or npc dies multiple times. These ai profiles are locked for that battle/region and they may have any "skill level" between noob or pro. Noobs are more common, pros are rare, but not perfect.

Difficulty settings wise, I think it's great to have a mix of goobers and pros in any setting. For easier difficulties, the ratio of dumbs vs pros is larger, while hardest difficulty has majority of npc characters be competent. Generally, there's a rare total noob, average low/medium skiller, uncommon veteran/pro and a rare peak elite. On low difficulty the split would be between 15%/65%/15%/5% = 100%. In highest, something like 5%/30%/50%/15% = 100% split. Both in the beginning. Later the ratio would skew towards pro during final battles.

There's also an element of progression in the campaign. Later missions will increase the minimum of each npc closer to pro, especially for the losing side, especially during the most dire of missions for them. They are motivated, one could say. Early missions will have noobs that may be over confident, hence they're noobs. There's sort of a wave graph where each region starts easy, ramps up to region final, switches back to easy, ramps up at next region final etc and then at the last invasion starts low and ends high too.

As an idea, those callsigns could be permanent for a campaign or per region and they could contain random generated names or loaded from a list. Lorewise player transfer around, but team members might not, sort of like simulating transit between servers. The scoreboard is useful for getting team status and enemy team status too.

On one hand I could do a unreal tournament 2004 style single player campaign team roster management thing, but that'd be overcomplicating thing again. I'd elect not to.

Besides that, the AI will have some inadequacies that the player will be able to do better and thus gain an advantage at out playing the AI. Things like staying mobile as the AI will more often try to shoot at the player than lead in front of them, allowing dodging incoming fire. Player will always have an edge in reaction times and be able to shoot faster than the AI, player will see more uses for environmental hazards or deployed structures than the AI can't know to use. Tactically AI will have no real tactics - just go with whatever it has and try to win with how it makes sense for that weapon, meanwhile player can purposefully choose weapons, loot specific ones and use them in specific synergies that the npc don't know. It's kind of a cortex style combat against AI - they're dumb but they still hold a dangerous gun. You can blitz them, dodge them, cheap shoot them or finesse them in daring moves that they can't do, but you can never underestimate them too much.
