# Falling Sand Asteroid Miner
(loosely inspired by the brick breaker game genre mixed with falling_sand game mechanics)
 
## About 

Player controls an Arkanoid style paddle with a classic laser powerup against a randomized static falling_sand based exotic space rocks above. There is no typical brick breaker ball here, just the laser blaster guns that can upgrade and/or change with powerups that spawn and fall from the released pixels of the exotic materials asteroid. The lore context is about mining exotic rocks in the low orbit of an exoplanet, gathering their valuable materials for industrial needs.

An example of one weapon: Player shoot their standard blaster with mouse 1 to blast groups of falling_sand pixels and can detonate their projectiles mid-flight to get those pesky pixels that are difficult to hit directly. Some weapons may works differently, usually having primary and alternative firing/trigger modes, each giving player adequate tools deal with big chunks of pixels as well as straggler pixels.

The falling_sand asteroids are static and solid, when hit by blasters, depending on what material they were and what color they were (assigned randomly), they will morph into gas, liquid or loose solid style pixels that have forces imparted upon them determined by the weapon that was shot at them, and they will fade away in 2-6 seconds, depending on material type, with some random variation.

At a rate of 5% per each pixel destroyed, either one of these game objects are released:
- powerup: player can pick it up to gain its benefits - either as temporary power boosters or as consumables. Appearance wise they are special energetic crystals that the ship is designed to convert into useful abilities. They're normally too volatile as elements for any other use.
- valuable ores: player can pick it up to gain resources for the high metagame uses.
- hazardous debris or anomalies: player should avoid colliding with these or should try to shoot them down to defeat them. Otherwise the player might take damage from their effects. The stages may have ambient weather that may affect how these hazards behave, like space wind that may randomly at random intensity and direction cause debris to veer to the left or right while the fall down.

The goal is to clear out all solid pixels and pickup any remaining powerups or ores. The ores are used for research projects and space infrastructure constructions. As player acquires resources, some can contribute towards an extra budget for the player's personal benefit, letting them craft/purchase useful consumables into their persistent inventory for their use during the mining operations. Its sort of extraction game style; player has a bigger home base inventory and smaller on-person inventory that used as the active loadout for the next mission, letting the player choose and swap what consumables they launch with. Player can even donate some of their consumables to the organization they are with to help boost them with research and infrastructure stuff.

The paddle ship looks pretty much like the Arkanoid paddle with two blasters on each side and a wide body that is used as a surface for collecting goods into the ship's inventory. below it are two big thrusters on left and right for keeping the paddle ship in orbit.

## Game field
A forcefield box net is set up around the work area with only the bottom being open, this prevents both the blaster shots nor exploding debris from escaping into further space while ensuring that any goods will flow downwards. The remote operated robot ship is programmed to stay atleast a few meters away from the lowest asteroid pixel on the map for safety reason, as the rocks can easily be too dangerous up close. Its up to the robot paddle's remote operator to carefully move and shoot to avoid debris, collect goods and powerups without them falling down and burning up in the atmosphere of the exoplanet below them, trying their best to maximize their yield and minimize damage to self.

## Low Metagame
Player clears out a set of 5-15 locations with randomly generated exotic multi-material asteroids.

## High Metagame
In high metagame, has tabbed menu screen:
1. exoplanet orbital asteroid ring map, highlighting 9 new random location clusters (as sets of mining sites) player can tackle as a new randomized low metagame campaign. The locations include a 3x3 matrix of low, medium, high intensities vs short, medium, long sessions. These are meant for player to choose if they want a relaxing session or something more intense or something in between, and if they want it to be short or long or medium.)
2. inventory for both home base and current loadout for paddle ship. Player can swap and boost their paddle for the next mining run with bonuses that will last for the campaign. Theres a constant rotation of consumables and bonuses, so the player's inventory stays in flux naturally.
3. research/infra contribution screen and shop. Shows the materials collected in the last run and how they contributed to the next project player chose to invest their collected resources (the ones that they can't directly use themselves). Researches will develop new equipment for player to install on their ship, like a new weapon to replace an old one that got worn down after several campaigns (player weapons and upgrades have durability that lasts them a certain number of campaigns after which they fail and are sold as scrap material for the infrastructure development's benefit, so they need to research replacement every so often to keep their inventory loaded with other options than their fall-back default weapon that doesn't break. Player will usually amass about 6-9 options to choose from, taking 3 of them to a campaign, after which they will lose a durability point.) The shop lets the player buy consumables from home base, getting powerups as usable items the player can choose to trigger at will when needed as opposed to powerups gained in the field that are activated instantly. Player can take a maximum of 6 different powerups with a stack of 9 uses per each.
4. Visual progress of the local starsector infrastructure development network as a scrollable and zoomable Master of Orion styled map of star with planets/moon around them, with new sections of lines, new color-coded pixels appearing, showing how each of player's completed low metagame campaigns so far has added yet another element into the greater map, whether that be an interstellar tradelane between stars or a local one between planets, new space stations or shipyards, new megastructures, gas miner fleets, new outposts, new deep space buoys, new communication relays, new jump gates, new planetary global-scale developments. Its basically a very simple flat 2D map of a wide sector in space represented by bright circles for stars, desaturated smaller circles for planets, gas giants and moons, and maybe some space terrain like nebula fields or gravitational space rivers or something like that so its not just plain black void. On this map, as player completes campaigns and collects various resources, earning them points to spend to add a new line or a new node in the shape of a diamond, square or triangle to denote what type of built thing was set up there, with appropriate standardized color coding to tell what economic sector it primarily belongs to (civilian, research, logistics, mining, farming, industry, military, etc). Player can view a log that lists what was built and what its called. Player can influences what they want to add to this slowly growing map by first clicking what area they want to expand, then clicking buttons to build tradelanes, stations or whatever, using the point they have or just press a auto-button to let it distribute the points randomly for them. With enough playthroughs and collected resources, this map will grow into a complex network of lines, nodes and labels, turning a backwater undeveloped sector of space into a vibrant civilized colonial network. Worth mentioning that resources gathered at campaigns decide how many points the player can spend to expand this bonsai tree like experience. If they do well and managed to collect more resources, more rare resources and generally just earn more during each stage, the more points and the faster the map will grow. The player can also name/rename their star system and decide the colors and shape of the flag that makes it their own. They can then export this map as an image or SVG and share it with other people.

## Controls
Mouse movement - directly controls paddle within a rectangle area that starts from the bottom and goes up to just below some safety distance from the lowest falling_sand pixel currently on the level.
Mouse 1 & 2 buttons - activate primary and alternative fire modes of current weapon. Weapons are always pointed upwards and cannot be aimed, generally. Player has to position their paddle ship to line up their weapon with the target they want to hit.
Mouse wheel up/down - switch between equipped weapons in slots 1,2 or 3.
D - hold down to use tractor beam to pull nearby resources towards ship within a limited circle radius around the ship.
S - press to activate currently selected consumable item.
A - press to cycle to select next consumable item.
