Gameplay General:

Slow tacticool squad command action with physics enabled interactions for everything. Control upto 4+1 units, 2 piloted vehicle and/or autonomous vehicles/robot/machine(all 7 deployed), 2 delivery and/or aerial combat craft (that come/go via off screen, non-deployable). Direct control of one unit like an fps char while basic AI will do what it's told (sentry, patrol, go there/follow/evac/go use/context, deploy/special/ability, sleep/idle/ambush).

Characters are usually humanoids that can swap, loot, drop, toss anything they hold. They can hold multiple weps, many gadgets, equip wearables, empower with abilities, be passenger on vehicles and do plenty mobility stuff to get around levels. Can dual wield stuff. Call of Doom style weapon groups.

World is semi destructible, tile based (8x8 or 16x16 tiles, while chars are 16x32px, 12x30 collider wise). Chars can duck to shrink to half size or prone to rotate 90 degrees to become horizontal. Somewhat zoomed out view, but still enough that chars are not too small to see. Mouse aim can shift camera ala soldat.

Some characters are not humanoid, but like crabs or something. Don't have flexibility of human, but have abilities that make em special.

Ammo is limited, but plentiful. Resupply possible.
Buying new bodies/weps/gear/vehicles via trade menu is possible, costs gold, usually cheap. Player and opponent can replace squad many times. Apron's shop offer premade orders (fast to select) or custom ones(slow due to manual input during battle).

Apron offers multiple brands, player can choose license per campaign for 1-5 brands or omni-pass. Brands are like "factions" in cortex command - set of units, weapons, systems, turrets, vehicles, tools, gear and machines that cover all aspects of a military force in all scenarios. Each brand has unique gameplay and style. Less brands chosen means discounts, using extra brands outside of current license costs extra and may be faulty.

Build, expand, maintain bunkers, bunkerships, injector ships in campaigns. Run resource farms outside of campaign - first exploit with industry, then mend with nature restoration ala Terra Nil. Move with units via turn based tactics gameplay on a square grid map of a local region, be it land, sea, air, space, megastructure, underwater, underground, crust/mantle/core. 

Move with several squads and bunkerships via an advance wars style turn based tactics game to find and destroy all enemies on map and defeat their HQ/Command Core/Terminal Core/Command Transceiver. Campaign can have up to 4 teams, player included. They can all be opponents, or up to 2 can be friendly allies and opponents can betray alliances sometimes, making it a free-for-all.

AI uses of 7 sins/virtues based archetypes for their unit styles, designs, loadouts, tactics, strategy.

Overworld/missions has many mechanics; prisoners+modules, fog of war(hard-soft)+flares/spysat, stealth, jamming, ECM, ECCM, fake/facade cores/buildings/modules/units, 3-4 way battles via sneaky squads, veterancy ranks, weather, flora, fauna, raiders, MOBA mobs, hazards, terraforming, Forts style industrial resource gain + Minecraft feed the beast quarries(non farming OW additional hustle money/resources, along with looting/extraction) advance survey of OW tile for more materials, doomsday devices/machines, superweapons, weather controllers, special bunker/bunkership modules that can do different things either in combat gameplay or overworld gameplay, looting/scavenging and selling stuff to apron's service for gold, x-ray scans for revealing enemy shipment contents+anti-xray service to hide it, kaiju like space monsters, SCP astral entities, demons, life support for organics and equivalent server unit for robotics (organics thrive in Atmos, while robots have advantages in space without oxygen time limits). Bundled orders in a package with tarkov/PUBG like backpack lootable inventories. Aerial craft sensors/zones/markers/symbols for threat detection beyond visual range. Semi destructible terrain - tile based. Scene/level border entry/exit mechanics/collisions. Gas or liquid zones/fields, variable/directional gravity, elemental phenomena standardized. Outside a campaign
- manage farming OWs, cannot visit directly as a battle map. These sites are only active as initial resource and weekly-ish supply runs, while other resources are to be gained at the local OW. Supply run frequencies and amounts are determined by star system conditions (distance from home, hazards/obstructions/weather/interference present and current farming OWs output capability. Supplies may be narrow/smaller and/or take a month instead of a week, so player may need to adapt to exploit on-site resources to defeat the challenges at the local campaign.
- farming OWs may have a turn based time aspect that forces player to choose between farming or going after a high priority target that may grow stronger and change if player ignores them, changing the campaign type into a more difficult one. For balance reasons, only upto 3 priority threats can exist at any given time and they may appear between 2 weeks or 3 months, with a maximum of 12 per year. Each farming OW turn is a day, so time is still relatively abundant, though a crisis may strike at an awkward moment, forcing player to choose. Severity has stages and ticks up like XCOM 2's avatar project doom clock. Player may chose to ignore but they will require a forced donation of assets to someone else in the agency to go deal with it. It's overall like a chill 4X game, like the new Master of Orion. Planning/organization/editing is free, but advancing construction of tiles or resources gain requires cycling to next turn.




Metagame(s):

(OW marks Command Core's/others projected command influence zone. Anything outside this zone will go limp and will need to be manually dragged back by apron's drone to make it controllable again. Because of this, no one can go outside by default, it's just disallowed. If CC is moved on OW it will adjust it's projection offset accordingly.)

OW stands for OverWorld, a square grid map of the local region akin to AW2,  very similar gameplay with a major difference:

Move a unit first, then decide context sensitive action based on what its circumstances turned out to be after the move. A move is first depicted as a ghost trail that shows where player can go. Ideally player would not venture into fog of war but first scout the area with vision and scans - both provided passively by unit. Player can still risk it but may end up ambushed and forced into combat unfavorably.

Player can choose to use stealth to any degree for either none/some/most/all of their units, bases, bunkerships and locations. See the lights/stealth doc for details.

Generally, player moves around with units, extends existing bases, creates squads/bunkerships, captures location or sends an injector ship to create a new outpost/base/facility elsewhere on the OW. Goal is to find and destroy the enemy faction cores. This happens by a squad or squad controlled bunkership initiating direct combat or infiltration upon confirmed enemy on ab adjacent tile. Player may also engage in espionage/infiltration/assassination/kidnapping/rescue/mafia messages against opponents. Meanwhile each OW may contain anomalies, opportunity, discoveries that can yield benefits or be a trap as part of a mafia message.

There may be up to 4 factions on an OW plus local neutral fauna and territorial unaffiliated raider squads/camps native to the region. Factions can team up to 1v3, 2v2, 1v2 and all other variations, either in favor or not for player.

Regardless of circumstances, every OW is technically beatable and doable either via direct open assault, systematic attrition advance, guerrilla warfare, stealth strikes or a combination of these. It's up to player to establish themselves, gain resources, build more bases/squads/bunkerships, do scout/recon/surveillance to gain intel, create a plan, prepare it and then keep trying until they win.

Before arriving at the OW, player can build up their initial fleet. There is an element of time that keeps going forward which cycles the entire universe, meaning the game is always active even outside the OWs. If player rushes too early with a weak or poorly planned fleet or just fails on-site due to mistakes, then the enemy factions gain more ground while a player prepares a new fleet to try again, this type conditions been changed.

Difficulty wise game is relatively easy and all about player choice. Opponents are like obstacles that push back against the player and mock them to gitgud until they succeed satisfyingly. Game is mostly focused on the cool  emergent moments that come from physics gameplay, randomization, spontaneouty, AI mind games, awesome combat arsenal usage and tacticool loadout fun.

Both starting a campaign, playing it in regards to combat, resource acquisition, unit/base/ship creation, loadout prep and defence/stealth mechanics is made as easy, streamlined, automated/dismissed and clean as possible. It is a joy to play every part of the game without any part feeling like a chore, annoyance, grind, too intimidating or frustrating. Whatever you do as a player, it's always meaningful and interesting.

No campaign is the same. The sheer variety of starting conditions, factors, elements, progression vectors and surprise mutators make every session unpredictable and unexpected.

Play experience progression wise, player will often start with more resources than they really need to win but as time goes on and player masters the game, they will begin to optimize their stuff to be more optimal and skillful. "Endgame" consists of player doing challenge runs with self imposed restrictions and leisure runs where player just enjoys the flow of gameplay as means to relax and have a good entertaining time.

- Gauntlet of battles

- Single OW mission
- Chained OW mission
- FTL/SF64 style selected fork OW series

Player must choose their brand licenses before starting a campaign/mission and allocating a budget (a default one is usually good enough, unspent money isn't lost).

All these serve a persistent universe where player clears out a galaxy of threats as an agent of a large administration agency. Each completed mission either progresses galaxy conquest upon victories or delays/postpones it upon failures. Progress never goes backwards and as a whole player cannot ever lose or ruin it. Even if they fail, someone else in the agency can pick up the slack.

Players actions also grow the agency and make it more powerful until the entire galaxy is completed. Then a small pioneer team is sent to the next nearest galaxy - with new galaxy-wide quirks unique to that galaxy - and the process begins a new. Each completed galaxy generates a unique generative art piece that marks as a badge/trophy of a successful job, something that reminds of moments and feels personal to the player.

Mission types also serve as a play session lenght chunk, shortest being anywhere from 1-10 minutes of combat, while longest ones involving OW turn based tactical gameplay serving for several hours. Mission type and difficulty influence galactic development proportionally.

Missions are offered on a bounty board and player can either dismiss, accept or reserve the ones they find most exciting personally. Nothing is forced but some missions offer incentives and bonuses to make them lucrative/priority.

Besides missions, player can always tend to their farming OWs for any lenght of time, be it 5 minutes or an hour. Or spend time at the loadout garage/hangar/workshop to prepare their tacticool fleet for the next missions in advance. Farming/manufacturing OWs do advance time - they play like a 4X game where you can do infinite amount of choices but eventually have to commit/end the turn to let the managed assets actually bear fruit. 

Meanwhile, loadout garage does not advance time. 

Garage does bother with brand licences too. Any squads/ships/injectors created here will first require setting with brand(s) it uses. When deploying for a campaign, player may only select the squads and bunkerships that are covered by the selected licenses. Player always has the option for an omni-pass, but this comes with extra drawbacks.

Sometimes apron may have personalized service errors that render a particular brand unavailable for the next mission. Sometimes it's specific items that are unavailable instead of a whole brand. (Can't use them, can only sell them);

Purpose of these things is to both reduce the amount of stuff player may be flooded with in the buy menu (it isn't really a problem thanks to collapsible subcategories and grey outs, but still, player will be guided into a smaller selection to keep it focused), but also play a role in having the player sticking to a playstyle defined by a brand - receiving delivery/operability benefits and discounts the less brands are selected, while also to occasionally force the player to switch up their brands and have atleast two loadout setups to always be ready for combat even if apron is having maintenance hiccups that are user specific (so opponent may still use that brand despite you can't).
