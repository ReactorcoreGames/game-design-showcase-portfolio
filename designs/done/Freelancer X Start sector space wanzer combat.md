# Spacelancer Open Explorer

Combine all the major elements of the game Freelancer with gameplay from Starsector, Nova Drift, Homeworld, including Wanzer transformations (from Diable Avionics, a mod for Starsector where starfighters and frigates can change into mecha mode, which are better at dogfights).

## Inspirations from games 
(web search each game mentioned both above and below to understand how the relevant mechanics worked in those games, unless you already have that info somehow)

### Freelancer
- infinite ammo (energy weapons)
- forward guns, turret guns
- always on shield, plus regen. Plus shield batteries.
- nanobot repairs, limited uses.
- infinite fuel, infinite life support 
- throttle movement, +strafe, engine cut-drift
- free look/turret aim vs direct control forward aim
- tractor beam for loot (smart, prioritize fresh loot over recently dropped items)
- auto pilot
- cruise engine mode
- missile, mine, cruise disruptor, torpedo, 
- each ship is mostly sufficient to be omni-capable, although some ships have specialities (cargo v combat, combat subtypes brute v engineer v precision, carrier v fortress). Every ship can fight small target, large targets, swarms, tank damage and go fast, but some are better at one than another or have a flavor ala Nova Drift.
- the entire open world with places to land/dock with that belong to different factions that have different dispositions against one another, there are planets, space stations, nebula clouds, asteroid fields, deep space navigational hazards, anomalies, tradelanes for high speed travel, jump gates or jump holes for entering/exiting other far-away regions of space.

### Starsector/MechWarrior 
- firing arcs
- hardpoints (energy, ballistic, missile/launcher, Omni, semi-omni EB, BM, EM)
- turret vs fixed guns
- ship active ability
- light medium heavy naval class equipment
- armor hp besides just hull and shields.
- Mass Effect 2/3 style hp/armor/shield hitpoints system with different weapons that are effective against different layers. (Build loadouts that knockout shields, strip armor, kill hull.)

### Space Pirates and Zombies (SPAZ)
- hardpoints splitters (big-2x medium, big-3x small, rotating big to 1x fixed XL/naval mount, 
- utility hardpoints/boosters
- weapon types (laser, cannon, particle, mass bombs, including FFU mod weapons)
- switch between dogfight/Omni directional flight ala nova drift/spaz/etc vs freelancer's throttle based flight. Some ships use the Omni directional flight as wanzer mode, akin to strike suit zero.

### Borderlands 2
- randomized weapons
- companies with design philosophies

### Fallout 3
- inventory (with tabs, drop/use/equip/repair/mix/craft)
- usage hotkeys 1-9
- drugs: buffout, medx, psycho, jet, radx, rad away, (for ship, not pilot)
- cripple limbs (wep and equipment)
- weapon degradation 
- item weight
- but have a tug beacon to go collect all stuff in area and drag it to your stash or mobile carrier ship. (Area needs to be clear of threat otherwise no tug will go in. It also needs to be escorted if it's a risky area. Sort of like a fulton recovery balloon thing.)
- special, perks and skills tied to character per universe (each playthrough is about a sector with some elements randomized, like hq (corpo start, raider start, military start, civvie start, brotherhood start, pirate start, mafia start). Skills and perks mostly affect space combat stuff, trade, followers/favors, inventory, etc.)
- vats and bullet time. (Vats for when you don't wanna aim at cost of losing agency, bullet time when you wanna be fancy and finesse with shots and assess overwhelming situations.

### Nova Drift
- ship auto repair
- specialization paths for ships and mods
- mod upgrades but used like Mass Effect 3's weapon mods.

### Homeworld 1+2
- prebuilt ships with preset weapons + modules/upgrades
- ship classes:
-- fighter class = small enough to go anywhere. Analogy: a wasp
-- Corvette/frigate = multi target engagements, heavier power, not burdened by capital ship woes. Analogy: a dragonfly
-- destroyer/battleship/dreadnaught/carrier = siege capable, can "ice-breaker" through lesser/small debris fields at reduced speeds, but stopped by larger obstacles. Analogy: a crow.
- player can pilot any of these but certain areas are locked out from access or otherwise too hazardous to navigate depending on ship. Some zones require small ships (dense asteroid fields), some require big ships (intense radiation or heat) for safer traversal.
- that's why player has a mobile base (large ship) that can carry medium or small ship(s) inside like the Cyclops submarine carrying a Prawn Mech Suit in Subnautica. If player loses their stuff, they can either start from nothing, use their accumulated credits in their bank account, use a stored ship from their hq hangar, join a team/corp/police/military to get a ship and starter stuff that way (gotta work though), take out a loan with drawbacks, find some garbage in a dump or call in a favor (limited) from someone you helped prior.
- besides reactor output for weapons, also have a heat mechanic. Overheating will cause faster weapon degradation rather than Mechwarrior 3's style of shutdown or coolant/heatsink stuff. It's like that "we kept shooting them until our gun barrels melted" line from Homeworld 1.

### Armored core 4
- instead of afterburner, have a system of boost, quick boost and overboost. Each have their uses and drawbacks (boost is standard and longer than quick boost and more efficient, but not as quick. Overboost requires charge up and burns for a continuous amount and has a cooldown period afterwards, but can propel you very far, albeit isn't as sustainable as cruise engine mode. These are directional (WASD in wanzer mode or forward, strafe, back/brake in flight mode).
- maybe even weapon switching system (dxm pylons, shoulder weapons?)


## General info:
Overall goal is still a streamlined, easy to learn system that feels intuitive and easy to play, but offers plenty of depth and let's the user think about management of their resources while they fly their ship and do jobs. Ship building is still Fisher Price tier "plug square peg into square hole" type of thing.

Unlike original Freelancer, the ships and equipment are easier to get and replace but they can be easily lost too.

Almost no one dies in space combat due to everyone having teleport tethers that yank them to safety so every will always talk smack upon win or loss. Opens room for a nemesis like system for recurring rivals.

Metagamewise, player is a freelancer with their own ship and equipment that can take on jobs, join a faction, or be independent and get rich to retire in any number of ways in a vibrant ever-shifting universe. Ways to retire a player character are inspired by the different win conditions a player can achieve in Civilizations or Master of Orion, by trading lots of wealth, by combat to become ruler of a faction either by subjugation or through promotion, by a miracle cultural pilgrimage tour, by faction tech project quest-chain, by treasure hunting a set of legends, by being a hero for eradicating a local alien menace, etc.

Player sessions consist of playing a multi-hour campaign of as a new random freelancer with a randomized start and a total freedom to pursue any victory condition to achieve retirement in a cozy safe place or die trying. Player can save and load the game, but theres an optional honor system to make a character death permanent and have the save file for that character to be automatically deleted as an opt-in permadeath mechanic. Doing so will reward the players next start with bonus starting equipment and spawn lootable wrecks and hidden stashes somewhere in space that belonged to that player as bonus mini-treasures player can find. It may also tie into a nemesis system where previous dead players might reappear is optional resurrected zombie/cyborg minibosses that drop even more loot than what that character had.

Virtually everything happens in the same persistent universe, but theres a timeskip of a few years and the world is reshuffled for each different freelancer playthrough, mostly explained by celestial movements.

Via main menu, player can visit a hall of fame that has each successfully retired character presented as a statue that the player can inspect to see details about their name, victory condition and the ship and loadout they finished with. Player can also visit a graveyard where they can find the sessions where they died and voluntarily quit instead of reloading a save.
