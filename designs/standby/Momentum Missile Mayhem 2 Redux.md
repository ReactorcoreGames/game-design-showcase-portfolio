# Momentum Missile Mayhem 2 (MMM2) Redux
New true name for this game would be: 'Graviton Singularity Mecha: Physics Missile Mayhem'

## Original inspiration
Momentum Missile Mayhem 2 is a top-down tower defence game that featured a single player controlled "gravity launcher" - a giant sized energy orb flinging slingshot that could generate and launch projectiles made out of energy in various configurations to collide with waves of incoming slow moving scifi military vehicles, causing them to be knocked back by physics forces while causing a mild temporary EMP stun upon hit. This orb would usually then bounce off and continue bouncing around the battlefield, off of walls, obstacles and enemy vehicle units, until it lost all momentum or expired after a certain amount of time, detonating with yet another EMP+Plasma blast. The orb itself would do less damage directly, but the knockback it could cause to send an enemy vehicle flying into another enemy vehicle or into a solid terrain wall - now that caused a lot of damage. The game's biggest enjoyment comes from scoring those fun hits against vehicles that send them flying and exploding, especially chain reaction explosions and cascading crashes where the most extreme physics collisions take place, to the point they're outright hilarious.

## About & Lore
Play as a powerful "Graviton Singularity Mecha" against an army of an automated undead alien empire death force without a master, classified nowadays merely a space monster (see info on space monsters). Overall theme is innovation high quality futuretech vs conventional high quantity wasteful brute force.

"Death Force" is a nod to the Anime OVA 'M.D. Geist' where Death Force is a name of a planetary-wide fully automated robot army with the directive to eradicate all human life from the planet - intended as a last resort "mutually assured destruction" doctrine doomsday weapon by one of the planet's leading parties that was at war with another party/nation that was sharing the same planet. It was intended as the ultimate security guarantee until there was a plot to sabotage it, trying to bring it down, but it ended tragically when an unintended third party managed to walk into the control room and instead of permanently turning it off, they instead activated it on purpose, leading to the genocide of all people on the planet, leaving only masterless robots roaming around, with only their directive leading them forward. This game has a similar antagonist albeit instead of just staying on the planet, they ventured uncontrollably throughout the stars trying to find more humanoid things to kill. This happened in a far away star system somewhere in the milky way galaxy, and eventually crawled up to human controlled colonial space leading to the events of the game.

Humanity has to defend, fight and push back the menace to its source and eliminate it eventually. Luckily this Death Force is mindless; sure they have huge army of various tanks, but their strategy and tactics are predictable and relies on simple-minded brute force to achieve its goals. Humanity had already advanced in science and technology to the point where they were experimenting with truly exotic weapons such as the "Graviton Singularity Mecha" and it was the exact kind of weapon that could single-handedly destroy the entire enemy army on its own if used correctly. The Mecha would exploit the very weaknesses of the Death Force army against itself, by forcing their battletanks to crash into each other and destroy themselves in the process by using powerful gravity manipulation driven weaponry.

The game takes place on a large distant barren planet at the edge of human colonial space that is mostly home to resource mining facilities and research labs, mainly for experimental weapons testing in remote areas where they're free to try exotic tech without needing to worry about environmental impacts nor collateral damage.

The gravity singularity mecha and its tech only works on a planet as it taps into its gravitational field to wield its weapon properly, making it almost useless if it were to be deployed airborne or used in space, so its used for planetary ground operations only, preferably on larger planets than moons, for maximum effect of its various weapon systems.

## Metagame

Stage types:
- Base defence (enemy has landed outside the range of Anti-Air Gun Fort and are moving in with tanks to try to get close enough to destroy it)
- Confrontation Battle (mecha meets enemy force head on in an all-out arena)
- Escape Prevention of fleeing tanks or Ambush a convoy (mecha playing goalkeeper, minimizing the amount of enemy tanks that get to live another day)
- Enemy base assault (base produces tanks, slam them towards enemy base to break it while dodging base artillery)

Low Metagame Campaign:
- Start with any mixture of above levels, as a string of 10 stages(with 1-5 waves per level at random), each of them being random in difficulty curve. Some easy, some medium, some hard. Some can even take place on the same stage a few times in a row 1-5 times as different waves. Player is presented a map with of a planetary region with hotspots and they get to decide where the mecha is deployed. Once all hotspots are cleared, a final stage is unlocked to destroy the last regrouped remnants of the enemy from that region. The final stage can be any of the 4 types, but it will have a special boss enemy entity that doesn't appear in any other stages than the final one.

High Metagame Campaign:
While low metagame campaigns focus on a limited region of a planet, the high metagame spans the whole planet. It acts as a generator to create low metagame campaigns, usually each one having a twist that makes each campaign feel a bit different compared to the last one. Either the colors are changed due to the region being a different biome or the intensity or lenght of the generated campaign is longer or shorter or leans more heavily towards certain levels instead of a balance.

This is the only game mode in the game and it is virtually infinitely playable. As the planet is on the fringes, new waves of the death force occasionally will come back to harass the planet over and over again. the player does unlock an achievement after clearing 100 campaigns to sort of mark for themselves as "yeah, the worst of them has been kicked off the planet properly and from now on, its just remnants and stragglers coming from outside, while the real enemy has already been virtually defeated satisfactorily as far as this particular game is concerned." Lorewise, the death force's origin is eventually found and stopped for good in the events that happen after the game.

## Combat Gameplay
Topdown-view of a wide zoomed out large rectangle area with solid border walls. Coverage of solid walls depends on stage type, enemy tanks usually roll into the scene from offscreen border edge that isnt solid, while the rest of the level will have solid border. 

The tanks come in from one side and depending on the stage, usually try to reach the other side to stop when they get close enough to fire their weapon and keep shooting at what they need to shoot until it breaks. (base defence)

In other times they just try to reach the other side to exit. (ambush)

Or its a demolition derby arena where tanks drop into the arena from near the border walls that will either drive forward slowly if already facing the player or turn around gradually, attempting to lineup with the player within a 20 degree arc and then continue driving towards them - their intention to get close enough to use their weapons to destroy the player. (confrontation or enemy base assault)

Generally, enemy tanks are slow moving, clumsy and generally lack the ability to avoid each other, making collisions more likely to happen by accident too. Levels are designed easy for the enemy tanks to navigate in a straight line so its simpler as a game, both in terms of gameplay but also in how less complex it is to code enemy AI.

Meanwhile, player start off on the opposite end controls their mecha to launch orbs, use direct cannon weapons or use indirect artillery weapons, use powers or deploy structures or mines.

Player movement feels like sliding with maximum speed acceleration cap as the player's mecha uses a directed gravity well propulsion system to create a gravitational pocket that it "falls into" as a form of movement, thus not needing rocket boosters or anything else translate itself anywhere around the battlefield.

Generally player tries to either stay away from enemy tanks and launches orbs strategically from a distance or atleast tries to avoid firing arcs of enemies by moving around them. In last ditch effort, player can also attempt to dodge incoming projectiles by strafing to the side. If that fails, player can use a shield to soak up the hit - sometimes this is useful to prevent an incoming shot from hitting the allied base in base defence stages.

General pace of game is slow and strategic and the player has the ability to turn on 0.1x timescale slomo to line up a shot. When equipping an orb to shoot, a guiding trajectory line appears, supporting up to 4 bounces to let the player see the bounce prediction and speed over the course of the orbs flight time, letting the player know how their shots will fly overall, giving more control over getting the exact kind of hit the player is looking to get. Usually its smarter to shoot at an angle towards a wall and let the orb bounce into an enemy tank from its side to make it slam into a wall or another tank right next to it, as shots launched directly at it may perhaps score one good hit and then fly away without having a chance to hit another enemy, leading to a less efficient shot overall.

Technical performance wise the game tries to keep the enemy tank count to around 10 at any given time, usually encounters range from waves that come in 2-10 units. The faster the player defeats waves, the sooner they come, letting them finish a level quicker thus. Though there is a hard delay between waves as to not overwhelm the player.

### Enemy tanks
Most are generally slow armored rectangle tanks, cars or mechs on treads or wheels, usually armed with a basic slow firing weapon with a delay and telegraphing before it fires, a slow projectile and medium-low damage, allowing the player to survive multiple hits before being destroyed. The weapons may be fixed, turreted (full 360' rotation or only a partial angle or rotation towards a particular direction, like a 45 degree forward facing turret).

Each unit may either by equipped a single gun/weapon or a set of them that are part of the same system (like 3 cannons mounted at the front that fire together in unison). Enemies come in small sizes, medium sizes, and large sizes. In some respects they take inspiration from Doom 2 monsters in how each has roles/weaknesses/strenghts that forces the player to avoid doing certain things with them to not give them an opportunity of hurting the player badly.

## Controls
WASD - move
Mouse pointer - in relation to mecha, determines facing direction that mecha should turn towards. For purposes of firing, distance of pointer from mecha determines how hard/fast should the mecha's slingshot launch the orb.
Mouse 1 - launch orb (new orb takes a while to manifest when the previous one is launched. new orbs are always generated and kept ready when that weapon is selected).
Mouse 2 - shield hold, limited charge. auto recharges when not active.
Q - trigger anchor mode on/off. locks player position so they are unaffected by sucking force hazards or lets them come quickly to a stop if player is accidently sent flying to do a concussive shockwave blast during combat or using their own powers too close to themselves. (also known as siege mode)
E - hold to project a strong shield around on mecha to let it tank incoming projectile hits. Has a total of 5 seconds of usage that recharges at a slow rate of 5 seconds per 1 usable second. Player is ment to tap it just before a hit comes to let them tank it safely without wasting most of the charge, only using it when direly needed.
Z - weapon wheel to select either orbs or direct cannon shots or artillery designators. Pauses time while shown. Gives nifty details and useful stats for things to let player know when or where they should use them.
X - Powers wheel selector, once picked, will activate it at the location of the mouse pointer. Same pause system as with weapon wheel.
C - Turret/deployable/structure wheel selector.
E - Slomo toggle on/off, 10 seconds, recharges at a slow rate of 10 seconds per each usable slomo second.

Note: certain weapons may or may not be available per stage or per campaign.
