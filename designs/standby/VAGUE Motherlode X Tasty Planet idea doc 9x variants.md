# Project idea: Motherlode X Tasty Planet

Combine Motherlode (2d tile digger upgrader genre) and Tasty Planet (or Fishy, a grow genre game), with some Metroidvania elements. Maybe some G.E.O. (that sideview space mining corp planetary drill clicker game) influences too in regards to metagame or the planetary drill part of the game.


## How

Motherlode (ML) mode has player dig and explore an under surface area in special locations to find "something" that gives player a new ability to use in the Tasty World (TW) mode, while the TW mode, player usually eats smaller things to grow bigger, while avoiding/ignoring bigger things and avoiding or removing hazards with newly gained abilities. Usually the ability is either combat related (tactical mobility, targeting, weapon types, defeat certain armor type, stronger/wider/faster attack), environment related (access new areas, survive hazards, more mobility, able to devour new things). Sort of like that game Spore's primordial stage.

Low Metagame wise, player starts small and weak, progresses by collecting something that either increases size of player entity or unlocks a new ability that opens up the possibility to overcome an obstacle that was blocking progress. The game often flips between sideview ML mode and topdown TW mode. TW mode can also be sideview too depending on the game context or situational context.

High Metagame wise, player sends a new seedling/offspring of the greater mass to a new location to devour/conquer that location too. Depending on the lore context I'd decide to make this game in, this would entail a new city, a new region, a new planet or some other place added to a pile of things the player has consumed, devoured or conquered.

High metagame is like a generator for Low metagame campaigns, letting player have some traits or bonuses from previous campaign carry over to the next one or maybe the opposing forces adapt or change that forces the player to use a different approach to do their next playthrough.

## General mechanics

These are things that occur in most games in some form.
Both use some form of primitive arcade physics and collisions.

### ML Mode
AD to left/right movement, while W to go up, S to drop down faster or descend. Spacebar to trigger an ability. Or maybe use mouse + mouse buttons for ability use.
Tiles with different hardness values.
Various resource types that are embedded in tiles that are released and collected when said tiles are broken.
Sources of damage that player must avoid or work with to repair their digging unit to prevent it from breaking and losing resources they had collected by that point and also get a setback that requires some more grinding on the TW Mode to gain another chance to use ML Mode again.
Upgrades for mobility speed, armor, digging power, resistance to hazards (heat, cold, pressure, water/moisture, acid, radiation, etc.), fuel capacity, vision range, digging speed.
Consumables for explosives to destroy more tiles at once, to help escape back to HQ/source easier, to mitigate or nullify certain encountered hazards found during digging, etc.
Critters, hazards and other elements that exists besides the player, tiles and resources.
AI opponents and enemy NPCs may operate similar to enemies from classic Super Bomberman games (including both lesser enemies that are confined to tile collision rules but also bosses that can disregards some or all tiles rules, with other elements to make them balanced and fair when encountered by the player.)

### TW Mode
Topdown WASD movement + mouse for directing and firing abilities.
Smaller things than player = safe to collide against and will be eaten by player to gain size.
Larger things than player = not safe to collide with as they will cause damage to player or shrink their size, causing a setback that player will need to grind to restore their original size back again.
Attachments on things, applies both to player and other entities, such as spikes, thrusters, shooters, maws, grinders, saws, grapple tentacles, legs/arms, harpoons, repulsion fans, attraction vacuums, 
(note: some game variants may be simpler or more complex, like a grey goo context game is about contact with entities regardless of size, while in a meat tentacle monster context game player has direct prey into a mouth to actually devour something.)
AI enemies split into passive roamers, active hunters and something in between. Roamers randomly drift around. Hunters are like roamers that have vision and if they see player close by, they will go towards them if player is vulnurable, while run away if the player is stronger/bigger. Some AI enemies can be hybrids, they will be hunters if far from player, but become roamers if close by. Some hunters/roamers can be unmoving and just sit in one place, hoping for a weaker player to collide with them or operate as a turret or as an ambush waiting to attack a prey if they come closer.


## Low Metagame Campaign differentiator aspect

There needs to be an element that makes each randomly generated campaign feel different in some way, since the overarching high metagame is a persistent timeline with a persistent player entity that continues its adventure in some form between low metagame campaigns.

It can involve positive or negative mutators/traits/perks that affect stats (player is faster, enemies are faster, player has more armor, enemies leave a trail that causes slow down, player grows faster from each meal, etc.) or have some elements/abilities/equipment carry over from the previous playthrough as a starter gift.
