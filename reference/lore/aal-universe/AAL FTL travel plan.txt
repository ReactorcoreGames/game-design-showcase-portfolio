For solar systems with no infrastructure at all, ships will rely on hyperwave scans; first 360 around then in all axis to determine other surrounding FTL travellers that may intersect with their own path essentially creating a ping of their own location and intent while doing so. Then a forward vector fine narrow hyperwave scan is made to detect obstacles in the flight path. If all good, a relatively safe FTL passage can be made, with the unlikely possibility of a freak accident or deliberate trap by complicated circumstances. To eliminate even this risk, chartered routes between homeworlds have been pre-cleared and are monitored for any disturbances.

Infact, it is standard protocol that anyone that is intending to travel to a new uncharted solar system, the local or any nearest fully developed homeworld star will launch two autonomous and self-sufficient hyperwave nodes, one to top of the star system and another at the bottom (so that no matter where someone is, they can have a direct line of contact with at least one of the two nodes).

These nodes will automatically set up themselves at the location, just at the edges of the uncharted solar system and remain there forever or until a homeworld class starway infrastructure replaces them.

The nodes will scan the new uncharted solar system, noting any significant celestials, any major or unusual activity, any lifesigns or civilizations, any hazards or instabilities or hostiles.

Clearing that, the node will then scan the path from the uncharted system to the location where the willing explorer is, as well as other convenient routes nearby from other homeworld class solar systems. It is more patient and thorough with its scans and may yield information on the quality and sustainability of the route that a ship on its own wouldn't want to bother with. It will more or less standardize the path, making it safer and clearer. If it detects obstructions or other problems with a route, either now or later, it'll give a signal to a nearest homeworld star to send autonomous drones to tidy up the route and fix any issues with it.

This entire process takes time and depends on what is encountered in the new area. The initial launch of the 2 nodes may take an hour to get to their locations. For the local scans, 1-7 days may pass, for the route scans another 2-3 weeks may go by. If problems with route are found, the drones may take a day, week or months before solving the issue.

If the explorer is in a hurry and willing to jump into the unknown and possibly bare the dangers that lie ahead, then they can do an arcing vertical hop FTL flight to the uncharted solar system after using their ship's hyperwave curve vector scanner. Ship FTL process:
. Start at 0km/s, front shield on, engine on, go.
. Accelerate to 10.000km/s, activate FTL drive ramjet
. Blast off + cone shield plasma lance activation.
. Hypervelocity FTL acceleration is go.
. Calculate exit point countdown ETA.
. Brilliant engine circular wake, cone shield shines.
. Ship is bathes in an rainbow color shifting spectacular FTL field, depending on current speed.
. Keep acceleration going until ETA zero.

At ETA zero:
. At this point acceleration is max.
. FTL drive shutdown / suspend.
. Ship begins to rapidly lose speed, going back towards the beginning 100.000km/s.
. Ship will still drift for a while as it looses speed and will arrive at destination with correct speed precisely
. While slowing down, lance cone shines gradually less, while ships FTL momentum field is shifting its colors in backwards order, engine wake gradually shrinks to normal size.
. At FTL speed exit, cone lance vanishes, regular frontal impact shield is back visible again. Last of ships FTL momentum field disappears with muted color, engine wake back to normal.
. Regular slowdown to 0km/s. Stop.


FTL technicality:
The FTL drive essentially creates a condition, field, phase mode that immerses or wraps the ship. This field allows vessel to ignore the limitation of lightspeed cap. Its a limit canceller. It creates, builds and maintains this field when its on and certain conditions are met, such as the minimum speed of 100.000km/s and an ongoing engine burn. Without these there is no achieving FTL speeds.

If its turned off while in FTL flight, then the conditions for an FTL field to stay sustainable are impaired, as FTL drive is off and it is no longer built and maintained. At this point, the field starts to dissipate and gradually vanish. 

It does not do so instantly, so the ship will continue to drift at high FTL speeds for a potentially very long distance, with larger ships and higher already achieved FTL speed having tougher residual fields that will take longer to dissipate than those on smaller or slower vessels.

The FTL drive can be turned back on at any time, that's totally fine. If just the engine burn is stopped, then the field will remain strong, but will start to gradually get weaker, albeit at a far slower pace than the FTL drive shutdown. In this case, after a long while the speed will eventually decrease below the minimum threshold and the ships FTL drive won't be effective any more.

This residual field is equal to the FTL speed and will be gone entirely once sub-ftl speeds have been achieved (or the 100.000km/s minimum, still open which one I'll use). Again, at that point the ship is now cruising or drifting at the same velocity it started before going FTL. The mass relay catapult can absorb this normal speed and slow the ship down to a halt. In the case of a hub FTL stop, the reverse tradelane tech suspends the ships movement to a crawl (like deadspace stasis) at which the catapult will dispel the FTL field and strip the remaining normal velocity as just mentioned.

Because of this, the onboard ship ai will use its navigational computer to calculate how long to accelerate, how powerfully to accelerate and at what point to turn off the FTL drive so that at end of the dissipation drift, the ship would precisely arrive at the correct location. These values will differ for each ship size class, engine type and flight strength (full power rapid or slow steady gain).

Outside of FTL movement, the ship has powerful sublight propulsion drive and a frontal field ram shield that protects from in-path collisions. In FTL, this field get supercharged into a magnificent plasma lance cone, making it incredibly powerful at annihilating almost anything in its path, capable of going several kilometers of solid rock before it gets overwhelmed and lets through the colliding matter, with catastrophic results to both the ship and the matter.

If this shield wasn't there, the ship would be in grave danger as any object in the ship's path could mean doom for the ship. Usually the shield and sublight drive are linked / work together, so if the shield fails, the ship would either rapidly stop or refuse to go past a certain basic velocity (100-1000 km/h) if there is any risk of collision with anything nearby. Still, with the shield on, a ship could plow through a 100m thick wall easily, meanwhile in FTL flight, it could penetrate through up to 10km of matter no problem. Even more - exponentially so - if the FTL velocities are super high and the cone lance is enhanced somehow.

Thankfully getting stranded after FTL flight or combat is no issue, should an engine, shield or other damage occur. Ships have their own self-sufficient repair drone that can fix the entire ship and even replicate it (very slowly), so getting a broken shield or engine back online can be done. If engine, shield and drone are busted, the survival kit allow the crew to repair the bot or build a new one. If hyperwave or even regular comms are functional, then the crew or ai can just request aid and rescue from the nearest allied station or friendly drone.
