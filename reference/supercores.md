# Supercores

A recurring mechanic in Reactorcore's designs. Many design files mention "supercores" or "a supercore-like effect" without explaining it, because the term comes from one place: Reactorcore's GammaVoid overhaul document (`C:\08 Projects\Project_Gammavoid\GammaVoidOverhaul Document Project\GammaVoid_OverhaulDocument_Finished.doc`, section "Progression, Wrecks, Relics & Supercores"). This file is the shared reference, so a page writer knows what the word means and how far each design bends it.

## The original (GammaVoid)

A supercore is a rare, physical object (a glowing red and white hexagon) that gives one **global boost** to everything on the side that holds it. Each one does a single thing:

- doubles a positive trait of one specific part (a hull piece's health, a weapon's fire rate or power)
- halves a negative trait of one specific part (a hull piece's mass, a weapon's energy use or reload time)
- boosts a whole group of parts (all beams get double range, all missiles double speed, all drones double health, all explosives double blast radius)

The rules:

- Supercores can't be built. Each opposing faction in a new starzone already has a few, attached to its major stations, and they fit what that faction uses: a missile-heavy faction tends to have a missile supercore. A rare relic somewhere in the zone may hold one too.
- To use one, you attach it to one of your stations, and its benefit applies to your whole side at once. The ship builder highlights the parts it boosts.
- You can steal an enemy's supercore (cutting it out of the station without damaging it) or destroy it. A destroyed supercore is gone for good. A loose one gets hunted by the factions, who carry it home to use.
- A supercore you've installed makes that station a priority target for the enemy.
- Detached or destroyed, its benefit stops at once.
- Supercores don't travel to the next starzone: each campaign starts without them.

Its two stated purposes:

1. **Freshness.** Every new campaign's enemy is specialized in different systems, so the player has to rethink tactics and designs instead of repeating the last campaign's answer.
2. **A target worth pursuing.** The enemy's superpower is something you can find, cut out and turn against them, which rewards exploring and dismantling cleverly over blind destruction. Once you hold one, it invites new designs built around the boost.

## The core idea, in Reactorcore's own words

From the Vector Thunder design (`designs/done/KITT Hawk Thunder game premise.txt`):

> generally supercores as an idea is where player can go into a campaign with enemy having some unique bonus to them that the player can steal and turn it against them by taking that bonus for themselves and depriving the enemy of that campaign from having it, then being able to carry it over to another campaign as a persistent item that the player can use from the start of the next campaign. Player can stack up to 5 supercores before needing to discard a supercore when they steal a new one. For each game, I vary this mechanic in a way that suits the game more.

So the constant parts are: **each campaign's enemy has a distinctive edge; the edge is a thing in the world, not a hidden stat; taking it weakens them and strengthens you; and what you hold keeps changing**, so no two campaigns play alike. What varies per game is whether supercores carry between campaigns, how many you can hold, and how they wear out.

## When presenting supercores on a page

- Present them as things in the world (a core in the enemy's base, a stolen edge, a looted quirk), never as unlocks or rewards. They're spoils taken from a specific enemy, so the player's power comes from the world, by the world's own logic.
- Their job is variety and a reason to dismantle cleverly, which fits the bible's balance by real-life logic: the enemy's power has a source, the source can be taken, and things wear out.
- A carry-over limit (five, ten) or wear is the natural limit that keeps the player from growing stronger forever. Say so plainly.
- Use the design's own variant. Don't graft the GammaVoid rules onto a design that only says "supercore-like".
